## Script

so i just got accepted into plzdontkillus. it's an AI safety residency in san francisco and i'm getting my visa in order right now. and the thing is, i've known for almost ten years that i would end up there. i didn't know what i was going to do, but i knew it was going to be important. i told people. i said it out loud. i held that image so clearly in my head that at some point it just became a fact.

and i think that's how it works. not manifesting in the instagram sense — this is hyperstition. you tell a story about a future that doesn't exist yet, you believe in it, you act like it's already true, and eventually the fiction pulls reality toward it. that's how religion worked for thousands of years. we collectively imagined god and god showed up as computation. the story reached forward in time and made itself real.

but here's what i've been thinking about. i've been reading baudrillard, listening to rick roderick's lectures on him, and something clicked about why most people can't do this anymore. why we've lost the ability to dream.

baudrillard says we live in hyperreality. the simulated version of experience is better than the real one. kids don't dream about dinosaurs because they've already seen them on screen, more vivid than anything their brain could render. you don't dream about the future because every possible future has already been simulated for you, packaged, streamed, consumed. you can step inside a video game and feel what it's like to be in a war. you can scroll through someone else's life and feel like you've lived it.

and that kills hyperstition. because hyperstition needs a gap — a space between what is and what could be. that's where the dream lives. simulation closes that gap. it gives you the feeling of arrival without the journey. you feel like you've already been there. so you stop reaching.

and then it becomes a spiral. simulation replaces experience, so you stop dreaming. when you stop dreaming you get depressed — not depressed like a diagnosis, depressed like mark fisher's slow cancellation of the future. there's nothing left to reach for. and when you're depressed you stop creating. and when you stop creating, more simulation fills the space. and the loop tightens.

hyperreality is killing hyperstition. and hyperstition is the thing that keeps the future alive.

so i'm saying this because i think it matters: make your dreams visual. say them out loud. tell literally everyone. believe in things that haven't happened yet so hard that they have no choice but to happen. because that's not delusion — that's the oldest mechanism we have for making futures real. and the fact that simulation is trying to replace it doesn't mean it's broken. it means it's more important than ever.

the future eats what you feed it. feed it a dream.
