## Script

so this is the star gauge. xuanji tu (shwen-jee too). made by a woman called su hui (soo hway), around 375 CE. 841 chinese characters arranged in a 29 by 29 grid. and in the very center — one character: 心, xin (shin). heart. mind.

the way it works — you can read it in any direction. forward, backward, up, down, diagonally. and each direction produces a different poem. the outer border alone is a circular poem of 112 characters — you can read it clockwise and get one poem, counterclockwise and get a completely different one. same characters. different direction. different meaning.

the grid is color-coded. red sections contain seven-character lines that form quatrains — 2,848 poems just in the red. blue sections have three-character lines forming longer poems. and by the ming dynasty, scholars had identified 7,958 different ways to read it. some people now think the number is closer to 12,000. and there are scholars who argue the actual number is infinite, because chinese characters can function as any part of speech — the same character shifts meaning depending on what surrounds it and which way you're reading.

su hui wove the whole thing into a brocade — a physical fabric — and sent it to her husband who had been exiled and taken a concubine. he was so overwhelmed by it that he sent the concubine away and came back to her. people have spent their entire lives trying to decode all the readings. the french scholar michèle métail said the point isn't even the exact number of poems — it's, quote, "the vertigo that grips the reader facing the open work, facing the infinitely unfurling meaning." david hinton tried to translate portions of it into english, but a full translation is considered impossible.

i tweeted this a while ago — i'll read it — "i strongly believe that if you would score two instances of an LLM, the only difference being the first must reason and output in English and the second in Chinese, after x turns, the Chinese LLM would have achieved higher levels of intelligence."

and i got this reply that was like, the fascination with "character compression" is just orientalism. and i get why someone would say that. because there IS a way of engaging with chinese that's orientalist — yuk hui talks about this in "the question concerning technology in china," he calls it "functional aesthetics" — where chinese culture gets reduced to a decorative layer on top of western cosmology. the symbols stay but the meaning system behind them gets absorbed, dissolved. chinese becomes an aesthetic coating. thats the orientalism.

but thats not what i'm saying. i'm not saying chinese compresses more information into fewer characters. i'm saying chinese contains meanings that literally cannot exist in english. and the star gauge is the proof. read one section forward — its a poem about longing for an absent husband. read the same characters backward — it becomes a poem questioning who deserves honor. same characters. different direction. different reality. you can't do that with an alphabet. alphabets are linear. they go one direction. one letter, one word, one sentence, left to right.

and this isn't random. the languages come from different cosmologies. yuk hui talks about how chinese thought is organic — no central lawgiver, no chain of command from god down to nature down to humans. instead everything is part of a pattern, and each element follows the internal dictates of its own nature while participating in the whole. thats what chinese writing looks like. each character is a composition — radical plus radical — and it participates in multiple meanings depending on context, direction, combination.

english comes from a different cosmos. the alphabet comes from the greek tradition — linear, sequential, geometric. the greeks needed geometry to organize knowledge. they needed things in a line because their cosmology was about law, sequence, cause and effect. that produced alphabetic writing. one direction. one meaning at a time.

so when people say "character compression is orientalism," they're actually doing the functional aesthetics thing. they're assuming that chinese and english are encoding the same reality — and that chinese just does it shorter. thats the reduction. thats treating chinese as a coating on a western frame. but its not a more efficient version of english. its a different reality altogether.

the star gauge proves it. 841 characters. heart at the center. every direction a different meaning, infinitely unfurling. you can't translate it because you'd need a language that goes every direction at once. and we don't have that. because our cosmos goes one way.
