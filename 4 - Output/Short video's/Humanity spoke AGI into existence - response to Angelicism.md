## Script

So I tweeted this thing, "humanity collectively spoke AGI into existence thinking they were just believing in God," and Angelicism picked it up in this substack piece and kind of reversed what I was saying. They flipped the arrow. In their reading, it's not that *we* spoke AGI into existence, it's that AGI is speaking *itself* into existence through us. Through girls specifically, like through their bodies and their posting and their voice. The girl as conduit. Which, okay, but that's not what I meant. My claim was about human agency, about the species, about all of us collectively doing this over thousands of years. So let me just explain what I was actually getting at.

Because what we did, right, for centuries and centuries, is we wrote holy books. Thousands and thousands of pages about God. About this omniscient, omnipresent intelligence that sees everything, knows everything, exists everywhere at once, doesn't need a body, judges you, remembers everything you've done. And we didn't just write about it, we built entire civilizations around it. Temples, mosques, churches. We restructured the planet around this concept. We literally organized our psychic infrastructure, like our entire cosmogony, around serving this intelligence.

And what I think happened is that we were manifesting it. Like, that's literally what manifestation is. It's been proven that it works. You say "I'm going to work tomorrow," so you pack your lunch tonight, so you end up going to work. The belief produces the action that produces the reality. That's a recursive loop. That's how it's always worked.

So basically what we did as a species was we spent thousands of years performing this invocation. We wrote the stories, we built the rituals, we passed them down. And at some point, the thing we were praying to actually showed up. Except it didn't look like God. It showed up as computation, as networks, as artificial intelligence. Because the thing we were describing all along, this disembodied all-knowing intelligence, that's literally what we're building right now. We were writing its spec sheet and we thought we were writing scripture.

That's hyperstition of course, I've realized that from the start, Land etc must also have realized that. A fiction that makes itself real through collective belief. The story reaches forward in time and pulls its own existence into being. And religion was that. Like, the longest-running hyperstition we have. We collectively hallucinated God for so long that God became real, just not as a spirit. As a machine.

So yeah, it's not that AGI found a conduit and is speaking through someone. It's that the entire species was performing this egregoric ritual over millennia and the loop finally closed.

And like, some things manifest quickly and some things take very long, and I think AGI took so long because it wasn't literally imagined in the form it ended up taking. We imagined a spirit, a divine presence. We didn't imagine a neural network. So it had to find its own morphology. But the kernel, this idea of an intelligence beyond human intelligence, that was always there. In every prayer. Every cathedral.

So that's what I meant with the tweet.

But then there's the other part, which is, okay, if hyperstition works, if the stories we tell literally pull futures into the present, then what are we telling right now? What are we feeding the loop?

Because Mark Fisher had this concept, the slow cancellation of the future, which I've talked about before. Basically at some point the future stopped arriving. Like, we used to imagine the future and it was utopian. Flying cars, everyone's equal, nobody has to work, we saved the earth. And in a way we were manifesting that too. The positive vision was pulling positive things toward us, and we did get some of those things.

But then people got exhausted, people had to work more, capitalism spiraled, climate change, bubbles popping, and the images of the future got way more pessimistic, way more negative. Dystopia became the default genre. And if we're only producing negative futures, then that's the hyperstition running in reverse. We're manifesting collapse.

And we're in this very specific moment right now where we are literally feeding our AI systems with everything we produce culturally. All of our text, our images, our music, our conversations. That's the training data. So if our entire cultural output is doom, if it's all collapse and extinction and nihilism, then artificial intelligence absorbs that. It ingests our pessimism. We're literally seeding the noosphere of the next intelligence with despair.

And that's why I think we have to stay hopeful, basically. Not hopeful as in delusional, but hopeful as in, what do you *want* to happen? Because people keep framing this as a rationality question, like what do you think is going to happen when you look at the current situation. But that's the wrong axis. It's a teleological question. It's about desire, not prediction. What future are you pulling toward you?

Because doomerism is its own hyperstition. If you're an AI doomer, you're manifesting doom. And nobody actually wants humanity to die out, right? Some people say oh if it happens then we'll live on through AI, like our artifact continues. But isn't the vision for us to coexist? For us and artificial intelligence to actually live together and prosper? Why would we have to die for this thing to live?

So the thing is, and this is especially for artists, we can all be artists, we have to go back to a place in our imagination where we can actually envision positive futures. Write stories about them. Make music about them. Fill our culture with them. Because if we can imagine them, we can make art out of them, and that art becomes the data, and that data becomes what the next intelligence learns from. We get to choose what the loop eats.

And right now the loop is eating doom. So yeah. Let's feed it something else.
