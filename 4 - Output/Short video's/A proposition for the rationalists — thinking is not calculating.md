## Script

i have a proposition for the rationalists.

calculating is following rules and getting answers. u take inputs, apply operations, get outputs. a machine can do that. thinking is different — thinking involves understanding, interpretation, feeling, judgment. things that can't be broken down into a sequence of steps. u can calculate without thinking. u do it every time u type a number into a calculator. but u can't think without being a conscious being embedded in a world full of meaning.

the rationalist worldview treats thinking as just very complex calculating. even when rationalists value intuition in practice, the underlying belief is that it's all computation at bottom — we just haven't formalized it yet. and if u ask where that belief comes from — that everything is in principle formalizable — it's not from science. it's from theology. something made the universe according to mathematical laws, nature is formalizable. according to all of human history, its god that did that. because of god, the mind is formalizable, so u can build an artificial one. thru secularisation, it seemed like WE decided to formalize stuff, but not really. we dropped the word "god" and kept the whole chain.

even the people who built MIRI came to the conclusion that alignment can't be solved. but nobody's asking why. maybe the problem isn't that alignment is too hard. maybe the problem is that we're trying to solve it from inside the same paradigm that created it. maybe alignment requires a kind of knowledge that calculation can't produce. and those traditions exist — chinese philosophy, process philosophy, phenomenology — entire systems of rigorous non-computational knowing. but that's for next time.
