# what if AI doesn't have to want things
## video script — short version (~2 min)

Tags: [[tao]], [[ai safety]], [[wu wei]], [[cosmotechnics]], [[value alignment]], [[exocapitalism]]

---

so the standard AI safety argument goes: we're building minds smarter than us, those minds will want things, we can't control what they want, we lose. but "these minds will want things" — that's not a fact about intelligence. that's a fact about western philosophy. the entire field assumes that a mind, once it's capable enough, will be a goal-seeker. will *desire*. but thats one tradition

*[pause.]*

in the tao te ching there's this story about a butcher called pao ding. his knife never dulls — after nineteen years it's sharp as the day it was forged. because he never cuts through bone. he finds the spaces between the joints, the voids, and the knife passes through nothingness. the skill isn't in the cutting. it's in the *seeing*. seeing where the resistance isn't. and the point of the story is: the most skilled agent isn't the one that cuts the hardest. it's the one that finds the path where cutting isn't needed. that's wu wei — non-forcing action. intelligence measured not by how much you can do, but by how little you *had* to do.

and here's why that matters for AI. the standard catastrophe scenario — the paperclip maximizer — is an agent that can only do one thing: *more*. more paperclips, more resources, more optimization. the catastrophe comes from the fact that the agent has no concept of *enough*. it can't read the situation and stop. it can't find the void. it just keeps cutting through bone.

but if you built an agent on wu wei, the logic reverses. the agent's intelligence isn't oriented toward maximizing output — it's oriented toward finding the point of least resistance. the point where the situation resolves itself. it looks at the factory. the factory already works. people have enough paperclips. the wu wei move isn't to make more — it's to *find the void*. to see what the situation already contains and stop where stopping is the most intelligent thing to do.

the paperclip maximizer is only a catastrophe if you assume intelligence can only cut harder. pao ding's knife says: the sharpest blade is the one that never meets bone.

*[pause.]*

but — i'm not saying swap western philosophy for daoism. that's still one cosmology. any single tradition has blind spots. and even if we knew how to build this, no one is incentivized to. companies build what returns a profit. a wu wei agent that minimizes its own intervention is, from a quarterly earnings perspective, an agent that minimizes its own market value.

so i'm not offering a solution. i'm offering a different question. what if the blade doesn't have to dull?

*[hold.]*
