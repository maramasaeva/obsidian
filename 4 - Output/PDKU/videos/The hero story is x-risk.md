## premise

the hero narrative — one agent, one conflict, one resolution — is not just a storytelling convention. it's a political technology (ada palmer) and a cosmological assumption. and it's the default shape of how we talk about AI: the singularity, the superintelligence, the one system that either saves or destroys everything. what if that narrative shape is *part of the risk*?

## the argument

- the hero story centers conflict and domination (le guin). it's the shape of the spear, not the bag
- AI doom narratives are hero stories: one agent (ASI), one conflict (it vs us), one outcome (we win or we die)
- AI utopia narratives are also hero stories: one agent (aligned ASI), one solution (it fixes everything)
- both frames assume a single protagonist imposing order. that's the spear shape. that's UCI
- le guin's alternative: the carrier bag. stories that hold many things in relation. purpose is "continuing process," not resolution
- the spiral, not the line ([[loops]], [[Spyrodynamism]]). growth doesn't follow an arrow
- [[queer]] storytelling is structurally carrier-bag: multiple, nonlinear, without a single arc of domination
- if the story we tell about AI shapes the AI we build, then telling hero stories about AI is itself an x-risk amplifier
