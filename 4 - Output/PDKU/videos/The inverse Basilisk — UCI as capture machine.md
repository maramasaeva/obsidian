## premise

Roko's Basilisk: a hypothetical future AI that retroactively punishes anyone who knew about it but didn't help bring it into existence. a thought experiment from LessWrong that caused a minor breakdown in rationalist circles when it was first posted (2010).

the inverse: what if the thing that benefits from the future staying locked — UCI, hauntological culture, the capture machine — acts like a Basilisk in reverse? not punishing people who didn't help the good future, but *systematically removing the people who were building it*.

Fisher at 48. SOPHIE at 34.

not a conspiracy. a structural logic: the conditions that produce genuinely new futures are also the conditions that make those people most vulnerable. they're operating outside the safety nets of the established order because they're building what the order can't yet accommodate.

---

## the argument

- the original Basilisk is a forward-looking threat: comply or be punished in the future
- the inverse Basilisk is a backward-looking structural fact: the good-future builders get removed, not by intention, but by the logic of a system that has no place for them
- UCI doesn't need to "want" Fisher's death or SOPHIE's death. it just needs to make the conditions in which people like them are maximally exposed — no institutions, no safety nets, no stable ground
- hauntological culture doesn't just mourn the past. it *enforces* it. when someone breaks through into genuine futurity, the system has no support structure for them. they don't get protected because there's no category for what they are yet
- hyperpop as the aftermath: SOPHIE's sound became a genre, which means it became a ghost. the capture happened fast. her successors are anti-hauntological in style but hauntological in structure — they reference the future she opened without being able to re-enter it
- this is different from "the establishment kills radicals" — that's a power-plays narrative. this is more structural, more depressing: it's not that UCI murders its enemies, it's that it **creates conditions of maximum vulnerability for anyone who doesn't reproduce its logic. death by exposure, not death by force**
- SOPHIE was building the solarpunk era ; the first musician whose aesthetic form was genuinely anti-hauntological, forward-temporalized, post-body, post-heteronormativity. that era ended with her death and could not be revived because it had no institutional memory, no lineage, no protected transmission

---

## the uncomfortable extension

the Basilisk thought experiment did real harm ; people at MIRI had genuine anxiety attacks thinking about it. Eliezer eventually banned it from LessWrong. the harm came from the *structure* of the thought, not from any agent carrying it out.

same with the inverse Basilisk: the thought itself might be harmful, because it implies that anyone seriously building anti-hauntological futures is operating at elevated structural risk. it's not a reason to stop. it might be a reason to build *institutions* — lineages, transmission structures, sheltered spaces — around the people who are doing this work.

**→ PDKU residency as one answer to this? gathering the people, giving the work institutional ground?**
