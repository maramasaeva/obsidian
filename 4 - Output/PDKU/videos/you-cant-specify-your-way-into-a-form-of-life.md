# you can't specify your way into a form of life
## video script — draft 2

Tags: [[alignment]], [[symbolic technology]], [[emergence]], [[art]], [[forms of life]], [[simulation]], [[experience]]

When you write software or programs you write rules which are mostly if-then statements etc. So you specify behavior and the well the software basically does what you tell it. But AI isn't written like that you just give it huge amounts of data you give it time with that data and then something emerges from that. So there are no instructions well there are but it's more about exposure. So artificial intelligence knowledge is emergence these systems they're grown we don't build them. And I'm just wondering if that is true we assume that it is true if you can grow a system that has real emergent knowledge of language and context and reasoning then why is alignment not seen as something that you can grow? This is something that I've been coming back to lately  realize when they talk about AI, and i think it changes everything.

AI is not made the way other software is made. when you write software, you write rules. you tell it: if this, then that. you specify behavior, the program does what you tell it. that's what software is.

AI isn't that. you don't write the rules. you give it data — huge amounts of data — and you give it time with that data. and something emerges from that. not from instruction. from exposure. from pattern. like learning.

so AI knowledge isn't installed. it emerges. we grow these systems, we don't build them.

and if that's true — if you can grow a system that has real emergent knowledge of language and context and reasoning — then why is alignment not seen as something you can grow?

that's the question i keep coming back to.

---

so the standard approach to alignment — the one that hundreds of serious researchers are working on — is basically: specify the values, constrain the behavior, verify the output. and i understand why. these are engineers. specification is what they do.

but there's this thing wittgenstein said — late wittgenstein, philosophical investigations, not the tractatus — that meaning is use. like, words don't carry meaning in themselves. meaning is what happens when language is embedded in a form of life. in practice. in shared activity. in the whole context of being a creature that does things and cares about things and lives in the world.

AI systems don't have forms of life. they were trained on the traces of ours. they learned the shapes of what we say without ever being inside the situation of saying it.

and so you can't specify your way into having a form of life. you can't add enough RLHF to bootstrap participation in human meaning. it's not an engineering problem. it's an ontological one. the frame is wrong.

so what do you do?

i don't know. i'm genuinely not going to pretend i have the answer to the thing that the entire alignment field is actively working on without resolution.

but i think if the frame is wrong, you need different epistemics. you need a different way of knowing what you're even trying to do. not specify, test, iterate. something else.

---

so i want to say something that i've been thinking about for a while. and i want to be clear — this is a hypothesis, not a solution. i talked about it in the attic at PDKU with nate soares, who is one of the people actually working on this, and he didn't fully agree with me. but i think it's worth saying out loud, because i think it's pointing at something real.

think about how humans actually develop ethics.

you don't teach a child what's good and bad through a rulebook. obviously some parents try that. it doesn't work. what works is experience. the child falls and someone helps them up. they take something that isn't theirs and they see what that does to another person. they're loved and they feel what love is from the inside. they're hurt and they feel what that is from the inside.

values don't get installed. they emerge — from being in situations, from feeling what happens, from connection. from other people, from nature, from the planet. and this is also why your values now are shaped by things that happened before you were born — we carry the scars and the tissue of the people before us and their experiences also inform what we now find ethical and valuable.

so if emergence is how humans develop moral knowledge — and it is, this is not controversial — then why is emergence considered irrelevant when we talk about alignment? if you can grow knowledge through exposure to data, why can't you grow values through exposure to experience?

---

and what i keep thinking is: what if instead of giving an AI data about human experience, you put the AI inside human experience.

not metaphorically. actually simulating the full breadth of human situations and putting the system inside each of them. not as an observer. as a participant.

and i mean all of it. joy, loss, hunger, belonging, violence, tenderness, loneliness, transcendence. situations from the past, not just the present — because the present is not the whole of human value. i mean situations from every age, every culture, every context. including contexts that western modernity completely disagrees with. that has to be included. you can't build something that's supposed to understand human value by only sampling the value systems of people in a specific place at a specific time.

and i think — maybe this is naive, i'm open to that — but i think a system that had experienced that full breadth of human life would not just make one decision about what is good or bad. it would have a sense of: depends. depends on the situation, on what's at stake, on who is there and what they need. which is actually a more sophisticated moral understanding than most current systems have.

---

obviously we can't directly simulate human experience in a way a machine can inhabit. we don't even know if that's coherent — what it would mean for a machine to actually be inside a situation in the relevant sense, to have something at stake.

and this is where i keep landing on art.

art has existed since the beginning of recorded human time. and what art does is proxy for experience. a novel puts you inside a life you've never lived. a painting holds a feeling that has no other container. music does something to your nervous system before you've decided whether to let it. film makes you afraid for people who don't exist.

art is literally how humans have always tried to transmit the interior of experience across the gap between one person and another. and crucially — art is cross-cultural, cross-temporal. every human situation that has ever existed has left traces in art, in music, in storytelling, in ritual, in the things humans made when they needed to say something that couldn't be said directly.

so what i'm thinking is not just art — but everything. music, conversations, interactions, stories, everything humans have imagined and expressed. all of human imagination. not as data to be processed. as experience to be inhabited.

---

when i brought this up with nate at PDKU he raised the real objection, which is: even if you simulate these experiences, does the AI actually experience them in any meaningful sense? or is it just processing data shaped like experience? and is emergence even possible for moral knowledge the way it is for linguistic knowledge?

those are serious objections. i'm not dismissing them.

but i still think the hypothesis is worth taking seriously. because the alternative — RLHF, specification, guardrails — is not working at the level the problem actually lives at. and it seems strange to me that emergence is the mechanism by which these systems develop everything else, but alignment is the one thing that has to be hard-coded from outside.

---

so if any of this is even partially right, the implications are big. it would mean alignment is not a specification problem. it's a curriculum problem. it's a question of what experiences you design, what situations you include, what it means to have lived inside something versus just processed it.

and it would mean the people most relevant to working on this aren't only engineers and philosophers of mind. they're artists, historians, anthropologists, people who understand what human experience actually contains and how to transmit it across the gap.

wittgenstein said: the limits of my language are the limits of my world. and we built these systems inside language. trained them on everything we've ever said. and then we're surprised when the world they model doesn't quite match ours.

the gap isn't in the data. it's in the experience the data is trying to point at.

---

i don't know if this is right. i think it's pointing at something. and if you're working on something adjacent to this, or if you disagree with me seriously and can explain why, or if you see a piece i'm missing — i genuinely want to hear it.

i'm just thinking out loud, i guess. but i think it's worth thinking out loud about.
