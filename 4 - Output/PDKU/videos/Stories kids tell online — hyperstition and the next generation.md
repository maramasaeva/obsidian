## premise

younger generations are posting stories — memes, threads, manifestos, worldbuilding, shitposts — that function as hyperstition whether they know it or not. the things they put online shape the models that get trained, shape the culture that gets built, shape the futures that become real. we can teach them this. media literacy meets sorcery.

## the argument

- the angelicism point (via [[Humanity spoke AGI into existence - response to Angelicism]]): we spoke AGI into existence. the next intelligence is trained on what we post. every post is a vote for what the model learns
- milady understood this: *"there is no meme. i love you."* — choosing what the loop eats. feeding it love instead of extraction
- kids don't know they're doing this. they think they're "just posting." but they're casting spells
- this is not about censorship or control. it's about awareness. you can choose what stories to feed the future
- connects to [[pedagogy]] and [[education]] — how do we teach this? not as top-down curriculum but as a practice, a skill, a way of seeing

## who this is for

- teens and young adults who are online constantly
- parents / educators who don't understand what their kids are doing when they post
- the pdku audience — this is the "applied hyperstition for normies" pitch

## format

short, punchy, direct-to-camera. the tone: "you are already doing magic. you just don't know it yet."

## open

- need concrete examples of online stories that became real (beyond the obvious crypto/meme stock ones)
- the dark side: misinformation, QAnon, incel ideology — these are also hyperstitions. how do you teach discernment without teaching people to shut down?
- connect to [[cognitive safety]]
