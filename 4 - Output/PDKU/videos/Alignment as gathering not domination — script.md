le guin has this other idea called the carrier bag theory of fiction, and she says we've heard about all the sticks and spears and swords, but we have not heard about the thing to put things in — the bag, the basket, the container. and a story shaped like a container doesn't center a hero, it holds many things in relation to each other, and its purpose isn't resolution, it's what she calls continuing process.

and i keep coming back to that phrase, continuing process, because that sounds so much closer to what alignment might actually need to be — not a target you hit, not an objective you optimise for, but more like a practice of gathering, an ongoing negotiation between values that genuinely cannot collapse into one without destroying something.

but the carrier bag holds contradiction without needing to resolve it, and that's what queer storytelling already does — multiple threads, nonlinear, no single arc of domination, just things held in relation. and the thing is, that's closer to tao, the spiral not the line, growth that doesn't follow an arrow, continuing process.
