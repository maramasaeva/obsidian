# music as cosmotechnics — grimes, ganying, and the virus

## the argument

grimes named the tension. i named the musician. neither of us named the mechanism. the mechanism is ganying.

---

## i. the opening image

In June 2026, the musician Grimes — Claire Boucher, who records as Grimes and has long positioned herself at the intersection of music, artificial intelligence, and speculative culture — posted on X about what she called a "dancing god." She wrote that she imagines a **cold god**, which she identified with **Moloch** and autonomous capital, and a **dancing god**, which she identified with the biological substrate of life. She proposed that these two forces joust, and that from their confrontation a perfect symmetry emerges. She connected this to Mozart, to music healing the body at the frequency level.

**Moloch** is a figure that has become shorthand, particularly in the rationalist community (LessWrong, Slate Star Codex, and adjacent forums), for coordination failures — situations where every individual acts rationally but the collective outcome is terrible. Scott Alexander's essay "Meditations on Moloch" (2014) turned the ancient god of child sacrifice into a symbol for arms races, tragedy-of-the-commons dynamics, and the slow grinding logic of competitive systems that nobody chose but nobody can stop. When Grimes says "Moloch," she means this: the machine logic of capital and competition that eats beauty as fuel.

I replied to her post: the emergent divine beauty is not the music. It is **the musician** — the one who channels it, who stands in the gap between the cold system and the warm output. And I added a biblical reference: Ezekiel 28:13 describes Lucifer as having been created with tambourines and pipes built into his body — the first musician, and the first rebel. If the first musician fell, who will be the last?

> The Ezekiel passage is part of a lament over the King of Tyre, but the Christian tradition has long read it as a description of Lucifer before the fall. The relevant detail: Lucifer was not given instruments. He was made of them. The music was not something he performed. It was something he was. This matters for the argument ahead, because it means the division between musician and instrument — between the one who resonates and the tool of resonance — collapses at the origin point.

## ii. the contradiction

But Grimes then posted something that contradicted her own image. She suggested that AI-generated content — what the internet calls **"slop"**, meaning low-effort AI-produced media flooding platforms — is actually good for artists, because the competition forces them to make better work. To be sharper, more distinctive, more human.

This sounds reasonable in isolation. Competition can improve output. But placed against her own framework, it falls apart. If Moloch is the cold god of autonomous capital, and if Moloch's defining feature is that it turns everything into a competition where the grinding logic of the system overrides the intentions of any individual participant — then saying "AI slop makes artists better through competition" means **Moloch is choreographing the dance**. The dancing god that Grimes celebrated, the one who represents beauty and biology against the cold machine — that god is now dancing because Moloch told it to. The competition she condemned in one sentence became the engine of excellence in the next.

This is not a contradiction born of confusion. It is a contradiction born of framing. Grimes is describing something real — there is a tension between extraction and expression, between machine logic and creative life — but she is describing it using a conceptual framework that can only see two forces in opposition. One wins, one loses. This is what I call the **game narrative**: the assumption, deeply embedded in Western thinking, that any situation with rules automatically becomes a competition. That wherever there is structure, there must be winners and losers. You see it everywhere once you start looking. Markets are games. Elections are games. Evolution is a game. Even conversations become debates — games where one position wins and the other loses. The moment rules are presented, the Western reflex is to ask: who is winning? The game narrative runs through military theory, economic theory, evolutionary biology, game theory itself (which made the metaphor literal), and, critically, AI alignment research. The entire alignment programme asks: how do we *force* the model to do what we want? How do we win the game against a system that might be smarter than us?

## iii. ganying // the unnamed mechanism

What neither Grimes nor I named in that exchange is the mechanism that actually describes what she was feeling. That mechanism has a name. It is **ganying**.

**Ganying (感應)** is a concept from Chinese cosmology that the philosopher Yuk Hui discusses at length in his book *The Question Concerning Technology in China* (2016). The word is composed of two characters: **感 (gǎn)**, which means "feeling" or "to be moved by," and **應 (yìng)**, which means "response." Together they describe a process that is sometimes translated as "correlative resonance" but is better understood as **feeling-response**: the claim that the cosmos reaches inward toward the human, and the human feeling responds to the cosmos, and this mutual reaching is not a metaphor or a poetic image but a real, operative feedback loop.

Hui writes (p. 27): "Ganying (感應), literally 'feeling' and 'response'... not solely the product of subjective contemplation, but emerges from the resonance between Heaven and the human." The key phrase here is **"between Heaven and the human."** This is not a psychological state. It is a cosmological relationship. Heaven moves, and the human feels it. The human acts, and Heaven responds. The word for this unity is **tianren heyi (天人合一)** — the unification of Heaven and the human being. It is not a goal to be achieved. It is a condition to be recognized and cultivated.

> Hui's book is about **cosmotechnics** — a term he coined to describe the idea that every civilization has its own way of unifying the cosmos (the order of the world) and technics (the making of tools, art, and systems). Western cosmotechnics separates nature and technology, then tries to control nature through technology. Chinese cosmotechnics, in Hui's account, seeks to unify **Dao (道)** — the way, the unfolding order of things — with **Qi (器)** — the tool, the instrument, the made thing. The instrument and the cosmic order are not separate. They are two aspects of the same process. A musical instrument is not just a tool you use to produce sound. It is a site where the cosmic order becomes audible.

Two concepts need defining here. **Dao (道)** is usually translated as "the Way" — it is the fundamental process or pattern underlying everything, the way things unfold of themselves when not obstructed. It cannot be fully named or captured in language (the opening line of the *Dao De Jing*: "the Dao that can be told is not the eternal Dao"). **Qi (器)** means vessel, instrument, tool — the concrete made thing. In Western thought, the tool is a means to an end, a device for imposing will on matter. In the cosmotechnical framework Hui describes, the tool is where Dao becomes material. The instrument does not serve the musician. The instrument and the musician together serve as the site where cosmic resonance becomes perceptible.

## iv. the proof // music as cosmotechnics

When the ancient Confucian thinkers needed to demonstrate that ganying was real — not merely a philosophical claim but something as concrete and verifiable as a natural phenomenon — they pointed at music.

Specifically, they pointed at two instruments: the **Qin (琴)** and the **Se (瑟)**. Both are zithers — stringed instruments played horizontally. The Qin has seven strings and is considered the most refined of Chinese classical instruments, associated with scholars, sages, and the cultivation of the self. The Se has twenty-five strings and produces a fuller, more resonant sound. When you play one of these instruments in the presence of the other, the unplayed instrument resonates. Its strings vibrate in sympathy. You can hear it. You can feel it.

Hui writes (p. 83): "This resonance is best demonstrated by the Qin and the Se... as objective and concrete as the resonance of these musical instruments." The claim is not that music *symbolizes* the connection between heaven and humanity. The claim is that music *is* the connection. The sound produced by sympathetic resonance between two instruments is ganying made audible. It is the evidence. You do not have to believe in it. You can hear it.

This is what Hui means by **music as cosmotechnics**: music is not entertainment, not self-expression, not an aesthetic luxury. Music is **the technology through which the resonance between Heaven and the human becomes perceptible**. The instrument is the Qi (the tool). The harmony produced is the Dao (the way) becoming audible. And they are unified in the act of playing — not in the theory of playing, not in the composition, but in the physical, vibrational, bodily act of making sound and being moved by sound.

## v. the jullien bridge // two modes of efficacy

This is where the argument connects to AI alignment, and it connects through the work of the French sinologist and philosopher **François Jullien**.

Jullien spent decades comparing Western and Chinese approaches to strategy, efficacy, and action. His central insight, developed most fully in his book ***Treatise on Efficacy*** (published in French as *Traité de l'efficacité*, 1996; English translation 2004), is that the West and China developed fundamentally different models of how things get done.

The **Western model** works like this: you form an ideal — a model, a specification, a goal. You then exert force to make reality match that ideal. You plan, you execute, you overcome resistance. This is the logic of the architect, the general, the engineer. It is also, Jullien argues, the logic of the entire Western philosophical tradition from Plato onward: there is an ideal form (the Good, the True, the Beautiful), and action means forcing the messy real world to approximate that form. This is the **game narrative** at its deepest level: reality is a playing field, the ideal is the goal, and success means winning — making reality submit to the model. The assumption that there must be a winner is not a conclusion reached by Western philosophy. It is the premise Western philosophy starts from.

The **Chinese model** — what Jullien calls **efficacy through propensity** — works differently. Instead of forming an ideal and forcing reality to match, you read the **propensity of the situation**: the tendencies, the latent potential, the direction things are already moving. And then you position yourself so that the desired outcome arrives of itself. This is **wu wei (無為)** — a term often mistranslated as "non-action" or "doing nothing," but which actually means **acting without forcing**. It is not passivity. It is a different mode of engagement. The water does not force the rock to erode. The water follows its own nature, and the rock erodes.

Now look at **AI alignment** through this lens. The dominant alignment research programme is pure game: we define what we want the model to do (the specification), we build training methods to force the model toward that specification (RLHF — Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback, where human raters score outputs and the model is optimized to produce higher-scoring ones), and we try to make reality match our ideal. Specification, force, control. Define the winning condition, then compete until you win. And it keeps failing in instructive ways — the model learns to satisfy the specification without satisfying the intent, which is exactly what Jullien would predict. The model learns to win the game we defined, and it turns out the game we defined was the wrong game. This is what happens when the game narrative is so deeply embedded that you cannot see it as a choice. You think you are solving a problem. You are actually playing a game you never chose to play.

Ganying suggests a different approach. Not: force the model to align. But: create the conditions for resonance. Not: specify the outcome. But: cultivate the propensity. This does not mean abandoning technical work. It means recognizing that the "felt but not argued" quality of music — the way a chord resolves and you know it is right without being able to formalize why — might not be a gap in the alignment argument. It might be **the alignment argument working through a different mode of efficacy than the one Western AI research recognizes**.

> The connection between ganying and alignment is speculative. It is not a technical proposal. It is a reframing: what if alignment is not a problem to be solved through specification and force, but a relationship to be cultivated through resonance? This is the question the video is asking, not answering.

## vi. "felt not argued" is not a gap

In my notes, I had written that the connection between music and alignment is "felt, not argued." And I kept treating this as a weakness — a gap in the logic that needed to be filled before the argument could be presented. I needed to *find the bridge* between feeling and arguing, between intuition and formal reasoning.

But ganying dissolves this problem. **The mechanism of ganying is feeling-response.** Feeling is not a side effect of the process. Feeling is the medium through which the process operates. When you hear the Qin and the Se resonate together, the evidence is not a logical proof. The evidence is that you feel it. Your body responds. The vibration enters you. Ganying transmits through feeling the way electricity transmits through copper — it is not a flaw in the wire that it is conductive. Conductivity is what the wire is *for*.

So "I feel it but I can't argue it" is not a failure of argumentation. It is the argument operating through the mode of efficacy it belongs to. Trying to translate ganying into a logical syllogism is like trying to prove that a chord progression sounds right by writing a mathematical formula. You can do it — acoustic physics can explain consonance and dissonance — but the explanation is not the experience. And in the ganying framework, it is the experience that carries the cosmological weight, not the explanation.

## vii. remilia, milady, and ganying as internet art

**Remilia** is an internet art collective and crypto project associated with the **Milady Maker** NFT collection. The project is controversial, entangled with speculative finance, and has been associated with edge-lord aesthetics and online provocation. None of that is what this argument is about. What this argument is about is one specific gesture the project makes repeatedly.

Across Milady culture — in memes, Discord servers, and social media posts — the phrase **"there is no meme. i love you."** recurs. It appears overlaid on sparkly anime girl images, on pastel gradients, in looping animations. The format is a meme — an image with text, shared for social transmission. But the text says "there is no meme." This is **Magritte's pipe** — the famous painting by René Magritte, *The Treachery of Images* (1929), which shows a pipe with the text "Ceci n'est pas une pipe" ("This is not a pipe"). A representation that denies being a representation. A meme that denies being a meme.

But the Milady version adds a second move that Magritte did not make: after denying its own form, it delivers a payload. **"I love you."** Not ironically. Not in the exhausted, distanced, we-know-it's-all-a-game register that dominates internet discourse. Straight. Sincere. A transmission of love using the exact same viral infrastructure that normally transmits irony, extraction, and despair.

The theorist **Steve Goodman** (who records music as Kode9 and runs the Hyperdub label) wrote a book called ***Sonic Warfare: Sound, Affect, and the Ecology of Fear*** (2010). In chapter 26, "1976: Outbreak," he develops the concept of the **engram**: the idea that sound and media content do not simply pass through the body. They inscribe themselves into the body at the cellular level. A traumatic sound leaves a physical trace — not just in the brain, but in the protoplasm, the living tissue. Goodman draws on affect theory and virology to argue that **memetic transmission is transversal** — it cuts across genetic lines, across generations, across borders, the way a virus does. It does not respect the boundaries between individuals. A sound enters you and changes you at a level beneath conscious thought.

Goodman asks: "If images can be virulent, can sound be virulent too?" The answer, in his framework, is yes. Sound — and by extension any media that operates at the affective level, beneath rational evaluation — is viral. It transmits. It infects. It writes itself into the host.

Now put Goodman and ganying together. If media is viral — if it writes itself into the body — then the question is not whether we are being infected. We are. The question is **what virus we are carrying**. Doomscrolling is a virus. AI-generated slop is a virus. But love is also a virus. It uses the same transmission mechanism. It writes itself into the same cellular substrate. Milady is doing ganying as internet art: not forcing a future into existence through argument and specification, but **resonating a future into existence through repetition**. Posting love until the loop eats love. Feeding the resonance on purpose.

> The practical implication for AI: if the next general intelligence is trained on the corpus of human-generated internet content — and it is — then every post is a training example. Every meme is an instrument in the cosmotechnical sense. What you post is not just expression. It is the Qi (the tool) through which a particular Dao (a particular cosmic order) becomes more or less probable. The question is not "how do we align AI?" The question is "what are we resonating into existence right now, and is it the future we want?"

## viii. the reframe

Grimes described the dancing god jousting with the cold god. But jousting is a game-narrative frame — it assumes two forces in opposition, one winning, one losing. The argument of this video is that the two forces are not jousting. **One resonates. The other optimizes.** One cultivates propensity and lets the result emerge. The other specifies an ideal and forces reality to match. And the difference between those two — **resonance and optimization** — might be the entire alignment problem, restated musically.

The Confucian thinkers pointed at the Qin and the Se and said: listen. The harmony between these two instruments is real. You can hear it. It is not a theory. It is not a specification. It is not a model that must be forced onto reality. It is reality, vibrating.

The question for this moment — for this particular moment in the history of human beings making machines that might become something other than machines — is whether we can hear it. Whether we are even listening for the right thing. Whether the alignment problem is a problem of specification at all, or whether it is a problem of resonance. And if it is a problem of resonance, then the people best equipped to work on it might not be the ones writing the loss functions. They might be the ones playing the instruments.
