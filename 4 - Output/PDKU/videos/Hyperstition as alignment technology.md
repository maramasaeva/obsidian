## premise

the stories we tell about AI become the AI we build. this is hyperstition — fictions that make themselves real. the alignment community doesn't use this word, but they're already doing it: every "AI doom" scenario is a story that shapes what gets built and how. the question is whether we can do this *intentionally* — tell stories where alignment works, and have those stories become true.

## the argument

- history shows that stories shape reality (ada palmer: the renaissance was fabricated by historians, then became real)
- the placebo effect works at the individual level — what if it works at the collective level?
- giving up on alignment is itself a story that makes alignment less likely. the doom narrative is self-fulfilling
- but you can't just *intend* to manifest something. you have to actually believe it. it's a weird epistemic place: knowing something is true *because* you know it's true
- this is not new (CCRU, land, neolumerianism use hyperstition). but nobody in the rationalist/alignment scene is making this connection explicitly
- the bridge to the room: jullien's *treatise on efficacy* — the chinese strategic tradition doesn't *force* outcomes, it reads the propensity of the situation and lets the result come of itself. wu wei as alignment strategy
