# music as cosmotechnics — short script (~90 sec)

---

the thing about AI safety is that everyone treats it like a math problem. define the risk, formalize it, write the proof, solve it. and the entire field is kind of built on that. the people doing this work are overwhelmingly rationalists, effective altruists, very logical thinkers. and that's not a bad thing, that thinking is genuinely necessary. but it's the only thinking that gets taken seriously. and i think that's a problem.

because when formal reasoning is the only mode you accept, feelings become bias. intuition becomes something you should get past on the way to the real answer. the human psyche is basically seen as this thing we need to bypass. and so you end up with an entire research field that is trying to align a machine with humanity while filtering out most of what makes humans human.

and this isn't just like a research preference either. it's a cultural one. this whole hyper-logical, everything is a system, reduce it to formal rules thing, that's a very specific very western way of thinking. most philosophical traditions in the world don't separate reason from feeling like that. in chinese philosophy for instance the idea that you can understand something through feeling, through resonance, that's not seen as less rigorous. it's just a different framework. but none of that is in the room right now.

people who think in stories, in patterns, who kind of sense when something is off before they can explain why. they're just not part of this conversation. and we're building something that's supposed to understand all of us, using only one way of thinking about it. and then we're surprised when the solutions feel incomplete.

maybe alignment doesn't just need better math. maybe it needs the rest of us in the room.
