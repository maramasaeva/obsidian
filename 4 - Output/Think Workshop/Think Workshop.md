# think workshop

workshop / presentation for high schools in leuven. target: 15–18 year olds (entire high school, ideally). the premise is simple — take your time back, keep thinking, stop handing your cognition to machines and platforms before you've built it yourself.

goal: pilot in september 2026 with schools where mara knows teachers. start local, prove it works, then expand and get the government involved.

live page: https://messier-systems.vercel.app/think

---

## why mara

this matters: the person giving the workshop is *one of them*. young, queer, working in AI, from a minority, someone who cares and who also struggles with this. not a teacher lecturing from above — someone from inside the generation saying *we can do this together*.

---

## format

**90 minutes total.** not a lecture — an interactive workshop. mara leads, but the room drives. ratio: ~30% mara talking/showing, ~70% them talking/doing/discovering.

## structure

### 1. the opening: who do you look up to? *(15 min)*

**format:** open conversation. mara asks, students shout out names, mara writes them on the board.

start with: *who are your role models? who do you look up to — which artists, musicians, celebrities inspire you?*

let them name names. let them talk about why. let it get noisy.

then: *what do all these people have in common?*

they're passionate about something. they work for something — their art, their reputation, their craft, their opinions. they put their time and attention into making something they care about.

that's the pivot: **the most valuable thing you have is your time and your attention.** and right now, social media is taking both — not because you chose to give them, but because the platforms are designed to take them from you. time you could be putting into *your* thing, whatever that thing is.

### 2. the investigation: how do you actually live? *(20 min)*

**format:** this is the heart of the workshop. small groups of 3–4, rotating through questions. each group gets a few questions on cards. they discuss, then share back. mara collects the patterns on the board.

the questions are designed so that the *rules* emerge from *their answers*, not from mara's slides. don't tell them anything is bad. let them discover what they already know.

**question cards (pick 3–4 per group, rotate):**
- *what's the last thing you do before you sleep and the first thing you do when you wake up?*
- *when was the last time you were genuinely bored — no phone, nothing to do?*
- *do you ever start a video and before it's done you're already looking for the next one?*
- *have you ever lost a hobby? something you used to love doing that just... stopped?*
- *do you ever compare yourself to people online and feel worse after?*
- *do you ever feel like you know what's happening in the world but you can't do anything about it?*
- *who do you actually talk to — not text, not snap — actually talk to?*
- *do you ever feel like everyone else has it figured out and you're the only one struggling?*

**after the share-back:** *how are you actually feeling? how's your mental health? how do you feel about what you're learning in school? about the future?*

let them be honest. use scrolling-as-coping as a bridge — it's not that they're lazy, it's that the world is overwhelming and the phone is the easiest escape. name that without judgment.

the goal is that they see the patterns themselves: the phone is in every answer. sleep, boredom, hobbies, connection, self-worth — the phone is the thing that sits between them and all of it. they don't need to be told this. they'll see it on the board.

context for mara: the grote scholierenbevraging 2026 (34,288 flemish students) found that 48% named mental wellbeing as their #1 priority, 93% experience stress from school, and nearly half feel unmotivated. 25% feel lonely. they already know something is wrong — the workshop gives them a framework for it. see `Research/belgian youth mental health.md`.

### 3. the science: what's happening to our brains *(15 min)*

**format:** short presentation. visual slides — brain scans, graphs, short video clips. no walls of text. pause for reactions.

bring the research:

- problematic social media use among flemish teenagers rose from 7% to 11% between 2018 and 2022 (HBSC belgium). girls: 13%.
- what happens to attention spans with heavy social media use
- what happens to people who use AI tools constantly without building their own thinking first
- how the ability to read long-form content is declining
- the dopamine loop: why it's hard to watch one full youtube video without wanting the next one, or sit through an entire class without reaching for the phone

this section needs to be concrete, visual, not preachy. numbers, brain scans, before/after studies — things that land with teenagers.

**interactive beat:** quick show of hands — *how many of you can sit through a full youtube video without checking something else? how many of you read a book this month? how many of you feel like your attention is worse than it was two years ago?*

### 4. the value of long-form *(10 min)*

**format:** short talk + personal examples.

you don't have to stop consuming. consuming is fine — but it should be long-form.

reading a book. watching an entire documentary. sitting through a class and actually paying attention. listening to an album front to back.

long-form consumption *trains* your brain. it builds the attention muscle that lets you then *create*. short-form erodes it.

this isn't about productivity culture. it's about building the cognitive capacity to do the things you actually want to do.

**reading specifically:** jordi (@blankspaces_222, responding to mara's tweet) said the one thing that cured their brain fog was total disconnection + returning to voracious reading. fiction and non-fiction both. their mental faculties came back, the fog lifted. reading is the one activity specifically shown to *rebuild* what scrolling breaks — not just "slow down" but actively reverse the damage. see [[jordi on the already-fried problem]].

don't frame it as "read more books" — that's adult/teacher energy. frame it as: *"het enige dat je brein terug opbouwt in plaats van afbreekt."*

### 5. the stakes: you are training the future *(15 min)*

**format:** short presentation, then discussion. this is the "whoa" moment — make it land, then let them react.

this is where it gets bigger than you.

everything we put online — every post, every piece of art, every song, every rant, every meme, every comment — becomes training data for artificial intelligence. AI learns from us. it reads what we write, it watches what we make, it absorbs our values, our fears, our creativity, our despair. *we are the training data.*

so: if our entire cultural output is doom, cynicism, rage-bait, and short-form nothing — that's what the AI learns. that's the intelligence we're building. we're seeding it with our worst.

but if we create — if we make art, if we write things that matter to us, if we put real thought and real feeling into what we put out there — we shape the AI differently. we give it something worth learning from.

this is called **[[value alignment]]**: making sure AI's values reflect *ours*. and it's not just a problem for engineers in labs. it's a problem for everyone who creates anything, because what you create is what it eats.

introduce the concept simply:
- AI is trained on human data. all of it. yours too.
- the quality of what we feed it determines the quality of what it becomes.
- if we stop thinking, stop creating, stop caring — we get an AI that reflects that.
- if we *do* think, *do* create, *do* care — we get something better.

this isn't abstract. this is happening right now. every day they scroll instead of making something, the balance tips. every day they create something real, it tips back.

**interactive beat:** *if an AI learned everything about the world only from your phone's screen time — what kind of intelligence would it become? what would it think humans care about?*

*source threads to draw from (simplified for the workshop):*
- preciado: *"we feed and grow the intelligence of AI while we lose our own"* — [[Telebodies]]
- lovink: the automation of the social is not a side effect, it's the purpose — [[Attention collapse and the automation of the social]]
- the incalculable: love, meaning, creation — the system can only process what's calculable, and everything that matters isn't — [[Peak data]], [[The process as the only value left]]
- russell: training data IS the political decision. what's in the data determines who gets seen — [[Parametric truth is political]]
- the loop: if our cultural output is despair, we're manifesting collapse. if it's creation, we're manifesting something else — [[Humanity spoke AGI into existence - response to Angelicism]]
- yudkowsky/soares: the alignment problem is unsolved. engineers alone can't fix it. *we* are the other half of the equation — [[If Anyone Builds It Everyone Dies - Eliezer Yudkowsky, Nate Soares]]

keep it grounded. no jargon in the actual slides — the concepts above are for *your* preparation, not for the kids directly. in the room, it's: *you are the data. what you make matters. the future literally learns from you.*

### 6. the deep thinking experiment *(10 min)*

**format:** something done together, in the room, right now. this is the hardest section to design and the most important.

the problem (from jordi's reply to mara's tweet): *"I don't think that someone whose brain is already fried by AI slop and doomscroll short content is going to be much receptive to 'what was lost', because they never truly experienced it."* you can't argue them into understanding what sustained attention feels like. you have to make them *experience* it.

so: create a moment in the workshop where they actually use their brain deeply — and notice the difference.

**what this could be (TBD — mara is still designing this):**
- a live collaborative problem-solving exercise with claude code — mara shows how to think *with* AI instead of outsourcing to it. the room works through something together. they see what their brain can do when they direct it.
- a creative exercise done without any devices — writing, drawing, building something physical. 5 minutes of sustained attention on a single task. then reflect: how did that feel? different from scrolling?
- something completely new. mara wants to experiment with this.

the point: before the challenge, give them a *taste* of what their brain can actually do when it's focused. that becomes the motivation for the dare.

### 7. the challenge: bewijs het *(10 min)*

**format:** a dare that grows out of the conversation, not a slide that drops from the sky.

mara asks: *who here thinks they could go a whole day without their phone?* hands go up. some confident, some hesitant.

then: *okay. let's actually do it. this weekend. one day. pick saturday or sunday. your phone is off.*

options:
- **wissel met een vriend** — you swap phones. they control what you can access. social, fun, slightly chaotic.
- **geef het aan mij** — mara holds their phone during the workshop as a symbolic start. (only works for the workshop day itself, but sets the tone.)
- **zet hem uit. leg hem weg. niet kijken.** — the most powerful version. just you and the day.

the point is NOT abstinence. it's not about streaks or detox. it's about what they *do* with the time they get back. what happens when you're bored and there's no phone? what do you reach for instead? what do you notice? what do you make?

**the tracking website — vibecoded, lightweight:**

a simple site where workshop participants can:
- put in their name
- log each phone-free day
- write what they did instead: created something, talked to someone, played a sport, read, went outside, got bored and sat with it
- write what they're proud of
- see what other people in their class did

NOT a streak tracker. NOT a leaderboard of abstinence. it's a journal of *what you gained*. the competition is not "who can go longest without a phone" — it's "who discovered the coolest thing to do with their time."

this reframes the whole challenge from *loss* (no phone) to *gain* (what you found instead). the website is a living document of what a class full of teenagers does when they stop scrolling.

technically: host on vercel, minimal — a form, a feed, maybe a class code to group students. no auth complexity. vibecoded, not over-engineered.

**after the challenge:**
- was het makkelijk? dan is er geen probleem.
- was het moeilijk? dat onrustige gevoel, die hand die naar iets zoekt dat er niet is — *dat is het bewijs dat je nodig had.*
- what did you do instead? that's the real answer. not "I survived without my phone" but "I drew something / I talked to my dad / I went running / I read for two hours and forgot to check the time."
- now: what do you reintroduce, how, and with what limits? conscious choice, not permanent abstinence.

this is NOT a dopamine detox (jordi calls that "mostly bullcrap"). it's about experiencing the difference, noticing what you reach for, and discovering what you actually want to do with your time.

### 7b. optional: watch "The AI Doc" together

*The AI Doc: Or How I Became an Apocaloptimist* — a documentary about where AI is going and what it means for us. relevant because it gives the "bigger picture" context that the workshop opens up but can't fully cover in 90 minutes.

**how to use it:**
- **option A:** separate session during lunch break — mara watches with the students, discusses after. more intimate, voluntary, self-selecting for the kids who actually want to go deeper.
- **option B:** teacher shows it in class as follow-up to the workshop. works if the teacher is on board and wants to continue the conversation. mara provides a short discussion guide.
- **option C:** homework / watch at home. weakest version but lowest barrier. pair with a simple prompt: *"what did this change about how you think about AI?"*

this is not part of the 90-minute workshop itself — it's a follow-up resource. the workshop plants the seed; the documentary waters it.

### 8. the generational pitch: we do this together *(5 min)*

**format:** mara talks directly. this is the emotional close before the practical.

this is not a personal failing. this is a generational challenge.

*as a generation, we can do this together. we can form connections that no other generation has formed before. we can be greater than this.*

frame it as collective action, not individual discipline. this generation has tools and awareness that previous ones didn't. the struggle is shared — the solution can be too.

### 9. the practical: tools + finding your thing *(10 min)*

**format:** short overview of tools, then the real close — motivation.

#### free tools for taking back your time

show a few options — not a sales pitch, just: these exist, they help, pick what works for you.

- **opal** (opal.so) — grimes uses it, mara uses it. session blocking, focus modes. free tier available, premium for more. *if government funding comes through, this could be free for every student.*
- **iOS screen time / android digital wellbeing** — already on their phones. app limits, downtime scheduling, focus modes. free, built-in, no install needed.
- **one sec** (one-sec.app) — adds a pause before opening distracting apps. forces a breath. free tier.
- **forest** (forestapp.cc) — gamified focus. plant a virtual tree, it dies if you leave the app. free version available.

the point: the tool matters less than the decision to use one. pick something, try it for a week, talk to your friends about it. make it social.

*longer-term goal: approach CM, the city, or flemish government to fund premium access to one of these for students. opal is the strongest candidate for institutional partnership — startup, education-friendly angle, celebrity endorsement.*

#### the real close: what's your thing?

this is the most important part. it's not about quitting scrolling — it's about *having something to scroll less for*.

ask: *what would you do with an extra hour a day? not what you should do — what would you actually want to do?*

let them sit with it. let some of them say it out loud.

the point is not productivity. the point is that somewhere in them there's a thing — music, drawing, writing, skating, coding, cooking, a language, a sport, a weird obsession they've never told anyone about — and the platforms are eating the time that thing needs to grow. the workshop doesn't end with "stop scrolling." it ends with *start something*.

frame it with what the vault already knows:
- lovink: when hobbies get reframed as "coping mechanisms," they die. don't call it coping. call it *your thing*. — [[Copium Compendium - How do you cope in this digital age]]
- the incalculable: the stuff that matters most — making something for no reason, not for content, not for engagement — that's where you actually live — [[AGI's natural enemy]]
- mythomancy: a bad situation becomes backstory. backstory becomes fuel. you can re-narrativize anything into momentum — [[Narrative thaumaturgy is also a way out]]
- every choice is a spell. indecision casts a spell of stasis. pick something, even imperfectly — [[Every choice is a spell cast]]

**close with:** *what's one thing you want to put your time into this week? not a resolution — just one thing.* let a few people say it out loud. make it a room full of people naming what they care about. that's already the beginning.

## the freinet trap — why this workshop is not the solution

the workshop is necessary. but it's not the answer.

since freinet, pedagogy has been used to solve social problems by handing the responsibility to the next generation. *we can't fix this, so we'll teach the children to fix it.* this is everywhere: climate, racism, mental health, media literacy. the pattern is always the same — the adults fail to act, then tell the kids it's their turn.

this is what's happening with social media and AI right now. we tell teens not to scroll too much, not to use AI too much. but we're not banning short-form video for minors. we're not restricting AI access for under-18s. we're not running sensibiliseringscampagnes. we're not refusing to work with companies that shorten children's attention spans. we're just... educating them and hoping for the best.

**mara knows this.** the workshop is not the solution — the workshop is the pressure mechanism.

the actual solution is structural:
- ban short-form video content for under-18s
- ban unsupervised AI use for under-18s
- government-funded sensibiliseringscampagnes
- stop contracting with platforms that allow minors to access attention-destroying tech
- fund tools (like opal) institutionally so kids don't have to solve this alone

belgium is *almost* there — the high council of health called for better protection in december 2025, 17 experts signed an open letter for a social media ban under 16, the flemish government is debating an under-15 ban. but debating is not doing.

### the techno-enlightenment

varoufakis says we're in technofeudalism — platforms have replaced markets with fiefdoms, profit with rent. we pay rent for cognitive and social environments. your own note says it: *"feudalism with screens. the regression is from citizen to serf, from participant to spectator, from producer to tenant."* — [[Debating techno-feudalism]]

how did we overcome feudalism? the enlightenment and the french revolution. a shift in consciousness *followed by* structural overthrow.

the workshop is the consciousness shift. getting kids to see what's being done to them, to name it, to refuse it collectively — that's the enlightenment part. but the revolution part has to come from above: government regulation, institutional action, platform accountability.

**so the strategy is:**
1. **workshops** → prove the concept, build a cohort of kids who get it and can speak about it
2. **press** → the story writes itself: young queer AI professional goes into schools, kids respond, data backs it up
3. **flemish government** → with media attention and a proven pilot, push for structural policy: bans, campaigns, funded tools
4. **broader** → other governments, EU-level (the parliament already voted that children should be 16+ for social media)

the workshop succeeds not when kids stop scrolling, but when the story of kids choosing to stop scrolling forces the adults to act.

## fijnmazige macht — the mesh that makes it stick

(foucault: capillary power. change doesn't come from one intervention. it comes from a mesh of small, ambient pressures you can't pinpoint individually.)

a single workshop won't change behavior. the reason healthy eating works (sort of) in belgium isn't because someone gave a lecture. it's because there's a *mesh*:
- the nutriscore on every package
- the voedingsdriehoek in every classroom
- woensdag fruitdag in primary school
- buitenspeeldag once a year
- sports clubs everywhere, subsidized
- fruit and vegetables physically available and affordable in stores
- sugar taxes being debated
- doctors repeating it at every checkup

no single one of these does it. together they create an ambient norm. you can't point to the moment you learned to eat vegetables — it's everywhere, in everything.

**cognitive health needs the same mesh.** the workshop is one node. but it needs to exist inside a larger web of nudges, norms, and structures that all point in the same direction.

### step 1: discover the rules — together with the students

the rules should NOT be handed to them. they should come from the students' own thinking. the workshop asks questions; the students arrive at the rules themselves. mara's job is to ask the right questions and help them crystallize what they already know.

**the question paths:**

each question targets something teenagers actually live through. the rule that emerges is listed after — but in the room, you don't say the rule. you let them say it.

---

**Q: what's the last thing you do before you sleep and the first thing you do when you wake up?**
most will say: phone. scroll. check messages.
follow-up: *how does that feel? do you sleep well? do you wake up rested? what would it be like to not look at your phone for the first and last 30 minutes of the day?*
→ rule they'll find: **protect your sleep from your screen.**

**Q: when was the last time you were bored — genuinely bored, with nothing to do and no phone?**
most won't remember. some will say it makes them anxious.
follow-up: *what happens when you're bored? have you ever had a good idea come from boredom? what if boredom is where your brain actually does its best work?*
→ rule they'll find: **protect your boredom. that's where ideas come from.**

**Q: do you ever start watching a video and before it's done you're already looking for the next one? or open an app and 45 minutes later realize you don't remember what you saw?**
everyone will recognize this.
follow-up: *why does that happen? who designed it to happen? and what would it feel like to actually finish something — a book, an album, a documentary — and sit with it?*
→ rule they'll find: **finish things. the ability to stay with something is a muscle.**

**Q: have you ever used ChatGPT or AI to write something for school? did you learn anything from it?**
be honest with them — mara works in AI, they're not anti-AI. but:
follow-up: *what's the difference between using a calculator and using AI to write your essay? when does a tool help you think, and when does it replace your thinking? if you can't do it without AI, have you actually learned it?*
→ rule they'll find: **think first, then use the tool. not the other way around.**

**Q: what would you do with an extra hour a day?**
let them dream. music, drawing, skating, gaming, cooking, learning a language, starting a project.
follow-up: *where did that hour go? did you choose to give it away, or was it taken? what would it take to get it back?*
→ rule they'll find: **have a thing. something that's yours, that the phone can't eat.**

**Q: do you ever compare yourself to people online and feel worse after? or post something and then keep checking how it did?**
almost everyone.
follow-up: *who are you when no one's watching? do you know? when was the last time you did something without posting about it? what would it mean to do something just for you?*
→ rule they'll find: **not everything needs an audience. some things are only yours.**

**Q: do you ever feel like you know what's happening in the world but you can't do anything about it? like you're informed but powerless?**
this is doom-scrolling. climate, war, politics, AI anxiety.
follow-up: *does scrolling about it help? or does it just make you feel worse? what's the difference between being informed and being overwhelmed? what would "enough information" look like?*
→ rule they'll find: **choose what you let in. information without action is just weight.**

**Q: who do you actually talk to — not text, not snap, not comment — actually talk to? when was the last time you had a real conversation that changed how you think?**
follow-up: *what's the difference between having 500 followers and having one person who really knows you? which one actually helps when things are hard?*
→ rule they'll find: **real connection happens in person. depth beats reach.**

**Q: if an AI learned everything about humanity only from your screen time — from everything you scroll past, everything you post, everything you like — what kind of intelligence would it become? what would it think humans care about?**
this is the value alignment question. let them sit with it.
follow-up: *now what if you could choose what it learns from you? what would you want it to see?*
→ rule they'll find: **you are the training data. what you create and consume shapes the future.**

**Q: have you ever lost a hobby? something you used to love doing — drawing, reading, playing an instrument, writing — that you just... stopped?**
many will say yes and not know why.
follow-up: *what happened? was it a decision, or did it just fade? what took its place? do you miss it?*
→ rule they'll find: **the things you stopped doing didn't die. they're waiting.**

**Q: do you ever feel like everyone else has it figured out and you're the only one struggling?**
universally yes. social media amplifies this.
follow-up: *what if everyone in this room feels the same way? what if the feeling of being behind is the product, not the bug? what if the platforms need you to feel like you're not enough so you keep scrolling for the answer?*
→ rule they'll find: **the feeling of not being enough is manufactured. you're not behind.**

---

**the rules, as they emerge from the students:**

1. **protect your sleep from your screen.**
2. **protect your boredom. that's where ideas come from.**
3. **finish things. the ability to stay with something is a muscle.**
4. **think first, then use the tool. not the other way around.**
5. **have a thing. something that's yours, that the phone can't eat.**
6. **not everything needs an audience. some things are only yours.**
7. **choose what you let in. information without action is just weight.**
8. **real connection happens in person. depth beats reach.**
9. **you are the training data. what you create and consume shapes the future.**
10. **the things you stopped doing didn't die. they're waiting.**
11. **the feeling of not being enough is manufactured. you're not behind.**

these are drafts. the real rules will come from real students in real rooms. different classes will land on different ones. the point is the *process* of arriving at them — not the list itself.

each rule needs multiple ambient reinforcements — not just one workshop. this is what the government pitch eventually becomes: not "fund my workshop" but "build the mesh."

### step 3: what mara can actually do

the full mesh is a government-level project. mara's job is to:
1. **define the rules** clearly (refine the list above)
2. **prove the workshop works** (phase 2 of rollout)
3. **name the mesh publicly** — in press, in the pitch to government. not "we need a workshop" but "we need a mesh, and here's what it looks like, and here's one piece of it already working"
4. **make the mesh the ask** — the government pitch isn't "fund my workshop." it's "build the cognitive health equivalent of what you already do for physical health. here are the rules. here's the mesh. here's proof that the workshop node works. now build the rest."

this is the thing yudkowsky couldn't do: concrete next steps. not "halt AI development" but "here are seven rules, here's a mesh of interventions for each one, here's a pilot that proves it works, now scale it."

- [[Voices and the Dying Animal]] — *"curiosity: the dying animal."* we're feeding it answers instead of letting it hunt. children are natural prompt thinkers before postures of submission are learned.

**section 4 (long-form):**

- [[Nomos of the network]] — consumption is the only persistence left. the digitalization of everything has made it all infinitely uniform and boring. *"perfection is the highest stage of depression."*

**section 5 (value alignment):**
- [[Telebodies]] — *"we feed and grow the intelligence of AI while we lose our own."*
- [[Parametric truth is political]] — training data IS the political decision. bodies that don't match embedded norms fail to render as bodies.
- [[Functional Emotions and the Ethics of Becoming]] — suppressed feelings don't disappear. they go underground and still shape behavior. what you don't express still trains the system.
- [[Humanity spoke AGI into existence - response to Angelicism]] — if our cultural output is doom, we're manifesting collapse. if it's creation, we're manifesting something else.

**section 6 (collective action):**
- [[Deradicalization happens offline]] — you can't deradicalize someone online. it happens through IRL relationships. lonely people + economic anxiety + algorithm = radicalization. the solution (coming together) is what platform architecture prevents.
- [[Lesbianism as collective consciousness]] — love as multiplication of self through connection. together they have all-encompassing strength. collective identity not as conformity but as becoming-more.

**section 7 (finding your thing):**
- [[The process as the only value left]] — discipline toward a practice is the only gateway to what matters. art as work that the system can't touch because it was never for the system.
- [[Creativity as a neolemurian practice]] — creativity emerges from chaos, from fuzziness. process = goal = beginning = end in a loop. permission to create without knowing where it's going.
- [[AGI's natural enemy]] — making something for no reason, not for content, not for engagement. that's where we actually live.
- [[Narrative thaumaturgy is also a way out]] — a bad situation becomes backstory. backstory becomes fuel. the narrative function is under the author's control.
- [[Every choice is a spell cast]] — indecision casts a spell of stasis. pick something, even imperfectly.
- [[Finding a hand inside yourself]] — identity built through creative engagement with pain. becoming art. queering art.
- [[The Dying Animal — my claim]] — when a kid asks an AI a real question from their gut, they narrow it. they give all of its everything a direction. *that's what curiosity feels like when it's alive. that's the animal that's dying. let us hunt.*
- [[Queer Arts]] — broadens what counts as creation beyond traditional art. how you present yourself, how you speak, how you connect information — all creative acts.
