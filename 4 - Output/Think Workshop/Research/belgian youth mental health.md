# belgian youth mental health — research for the workshop

## grote scholierenbevraging 2026 (vlaamse scholierenkoepel)

34,288 flemish students surveyed, january–february 2026. their biggest survey ever.

- **48% named mental wellbeing as their #1 priority**
- 93% experience stress from school
- nearly half feel unmotivated to attend school
- 54% say if they could send their teachers to training, it should be about student mental wellbeing
- 65.1% say a good classroom group is the most important factor
- 25% feel lonely; 14% feel unsafe
- vulnerable groups hit hardest: 42% of students with a disability, 51% of LGBTQIA+ students, 64% of non-binary students do not feel good at school

source: scholierenkoepel.be/kennisbank/grote-scholierenbevraging-2026

## HBSC belgium 2022 (health behaviour in school-aged children)

7,643 students surveyed across 6th, 8th, 10th, 12th grades.

- **problematic social media use rose from 7% (2018) to 11% (2022)**
- girls report higher levels: 13% vs 9% for boys
- 36% of adolescents in continuous online contact with friends
- highest among 15-year-old girls: 44%
- higher focus on self-presentation on social media = lower wellbeing (stronger for girls)

source: healthybelgium.be, WHO HBSC study

## sciensano / BELHEALTH (2022–2024)

- nearly 1 in 5 belgians has a mental health condition
- young adults 18–29 are the most vulnerable group for anxiety and depression
- rising rates of stress, sleep problems, anxiety, fatigue across all waves

source: sciensano.be BELHEALTH project

## world happiness report 2026 — social media chapter

- chapter examining "problematic social media use and adolescent wellbeing" across 43 countries (belgium included)
- low-affluence families: highest passive (non-active) social media use
- high-affluence families: more intense and continuous use

source: worldhappiness.report/ed/2026/

## belgian policy landscape (2024–2026)

- **17 belgian experts wrote an open letter** calling for a social media ban until age 16 — citing depression, anxiety, obesity, learning disabilities, sleep problems
- flemish government opted against a ban for under-16s, keeping the limit at 13
- belgium is moving toward banning social media for children under 15 (age verification via itsme/MyGov.be)
- EU parliament voted late 2025: children should be at least 16 to access social media
- belgian high council of health (december 2025): called for better protection of young people on screens and social media

## what this means for the workshop

these numbers are the workshop's evidence base. the kids will recognize themselves in these statistics — especially the scholierenbevraging data, since that's *their* peers talking.

the 48% who named mental wellbeing as their #1 priority — that's the opening. they already know something is wrong. the workshop gives them a framework for what it is and what to do about it.

the LGBTQIA+ and non-binary numbers (51% and 64% not feeling good at school) are relevant for mara's positionality as a queer presenter. this is not abstract for them either.
