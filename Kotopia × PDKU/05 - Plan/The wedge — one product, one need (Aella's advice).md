# The wedge — one product, one need (Aella's advice)

The single most useful thing from the Aella conversation. US investors don't buy a worldview or a bundle of funnels — they buy *"X people have problem Y, this solves it, here's the response so far."* Kotopia's breadth feels like strength to us and reads as unfocus to them. (2026-07-23)

## What Aella actually taught (both examples are the same move)
- **Askhole:** noticed a *specific recurring moment* (party lull, nobody knows what to say) → built the tiniest thing that fills exactly that gap.
- **Glosso:** started broad, watched what people *actually* engaged with (quizzes), then rebuilt the whole platform around that one proven thing.

The wedge is **discovered, not brainstormed.** Find one specific person, in one specific moment, with one specific unmet need — then commit everything to it.

## The test (fill this in for every candidate)
> **[specific person]** in **[specific moment]** wants **[specific thing]**, and right now they **[bad workaround]**.

Askhole passes: *people at a party, mid-lull, want something to talk about, and right now they awkwardly reach for their phones.* If you can't fill it in concretely → it's a feature, not a wedge.

## The honest correction on the device idea
- The insight is **right**: the real need is communication / expression / belonging, NOT "a keyboard." The keyboard is a feature; the need is *"a way to talk and express that feels like me and isn't extractive."* Keep this.
- But **hardware (tamagotchi / mp3+chat / fashion) is the wrong vehicle for the test phase.** Wedge-finding runs on speed + cheapness. Hardware is the slowest, most capital-hungry thing — you'd have to raise money to build the thing you're testing in order to justify raising the money. Backwards.
- The device/fashion is a beautiful **later** — the physical expression of an *already-proven* wedge (Playdate / actual Tamagotchi playbook). Not the test.

## Use what's already live
The **kaimoji app is in the App Store today** = a running instrument. Watch who uses it, for what, what they come back to. That's the Glosso "people liked quizzes" signal — free, real, running now. Don't invent a new product before reading the one you shipped.

## Next steps
1. **Pick ONE audience.** Not "young people online" — *who exactly?* (keyboard users already? gen-z who love cute net culture but feel AI eating everything?)
2. **Fill in the wedge sentence** for that person.
3. **Mine live kaimoji usage** for the signal.
4. **Reframe the ask to Koto:** not "I'll go find the one product" (slow, open-ended, and he's been carrying funding) → bring **2–3 wedge candidates with the sentence filled in** and ask *"which do you believe?"* Give him something concrete to react to. (thread-then-pause, same as Aella.)

## The answer may already be written
Locked consumer line: **"Kotopia is a cute messaging app where you actually belong."** That's *already* a wedge statement — messaging + belonging + a specific feeling. The one product might not be a new invention; it might be sharpening the expression/messaging thing you already have, aimed at one audience.
