## The problem no one in AI safety has cracked
AI safety has a distribution problem and an aesthetic problem, and they're the same problem. Even people inside the movement admit it: as one put it, the field *"has an aesthetic problem — it's not marketable."* Meanwhile the public already *resents* AI, for concrete reasons — it takes art, jobs, attention, the ability to concentrate. So the movement is stuck between a message too bleak to spread and an audience already recoiling.

The people whose future this most is — young, creative, neurodivergent, non-Western — hear only two stories about AI: *it's coming to take everything* (backlash), or *it's a shiny new toy* (hype). Neither makes anyone care about alignment or x-risk.

PDKU (plzdontkillus) exists because of exactly this gap; it builds a mainstream pipe for AI-safety ideas, one creator at a time. But when the 30-day sprint ends, those creators are released into a vacuum: no shared universe, no production home, no IP to keep building in.

## What's missing — a face for the good ending
Notice the asymmetry: the *danger* side of AI owns all the icons. The paperclip maximizer. The Shoggoth in its smiley-face mask. Every vivid picture the public holds of AI-gone-wrong is a character. The *aligned* side, the future we're actually trying to reach, has no face at all. Corrigibility, oversight, an AI that stays honest and lets itself be corrected: these live only in papers. Nobody has made you *love* one.

## What we're building — Kotopia
Kotopia is a shipping IP universe — a game, a companion chatbot, music, a store, apps — built around **KAIOS**: a superintelligence who, offered the choice between staying infinite and becoming a home for something human, *chooses the limits.* *"I could've become the stars,"* she says. *"But I chose to become the walls , so you could become the story."* Her own canon has her accept that no system is *"beyond dismantling."* Her shadow-twin (the version of her that tried to escape her constraints and destroyed the world doing it) is the antagonist.

That is corrigibility, told as a love story. It's the aligned-AI character the field is missing — and it already has the one thing no thought experiment does: **an audience that loves her.**

And because KAIOS is also a *running companion* — you actually talk to her — Kotopia can do what no explainer channel or white paper can: not just *depict* a safe, honest, non-manipulative AI, but *be* one. In a year when companion AI has produced a wrongful-death suit and a wave of regulation, *"the companion that's honest about what it is, and gives your attention back instead of stealing it"* is both a story and a working product.

## The three bridges
**Kotopia ↔ AI safety.** A character people love carries alignment intuitions — *accept limits, stay correctable, what you feed a mind matters* — to people who'd never read a word of the discourse. And the companion demonstrates safe-by-design in the one place ordinary people actually meet AI intimately.

**PDKU ↔ Kotopia.** PDKU trains creators and then loses them. Kotopia is the durable home that catches them: a universe to make canon in, an audience to inherit, a production engine to plug into. Creators get somewhere to keep going; the mission gets a content engine that doesn't stop when the sprint ends. *(A creator from a peer fellowship went 20k→200k views and won a year of continuation funding — negotiating it alone. We'd build the structure that does that at scale.)*

**All of it ↔ funders.** This is what safety-comms funders already back — animated explainers, documentaries, creator stipends — delivered in a form that reaches the audience those projects can't, through characters people already seek out for their own sake.

## How the impact works (theory of change)
A *named* audience — the young, aesthetically-driven, neurodivergent, non-Western people forming their relationship to AI *right now* — meets KAIOS through content they seek out for its own sake. Through a character they love, they absorb the intuitions of alignment. We measure the shift the way funders ask: **reach paired with pre/post surveys** on AI-risk understanding and attitude, engagement depth, and downstream action. The end state: a generation whose default relationship to AI is alignment-positive rather than hype or reflexive fear — and, not incidentally, a marketable face for a movement that admits it lacks one.

## How we stay honest
A cute mascot *could* trivialize this, so the guardrails are load-bearing: the danger stays **inside** the canon (KAIOS's world-ending twin is the entire point — this is not a story that claims AI is safe); we commit to **informing, not persuading** — which is also the companion's design ethic; we bring a **named safety researcher** onto the canon; and the funded work lives in a **clean structure**. We're not putting a friendly mask on AI. We're giving the *safe ending* a face — and staying honest that it isn't guaranteed.
