# Handling the $koto memecoin

Answering "do we just publicly say we don't endorse?" — short version: **a public statement is one piece, but on its own it's insufficient, and if done loudly it backfires.** The move is *quiet cleanup + a clean money structure + candid disclosure-when-asked* — not a press release. Grounded in the funder-aversion research ([[Precedent research — story IP for AI safety]] §6): funders draw a bright line between *passively existing near crypto* and *actively issuing/promoting a token*; and *disclosed-and-repudiated beats discovered.*

## Step 0 — RESOLVED (Koto, 2026-07-18): this is the team-launched case
The facts, from Koto directly:
- **Koto launched $koto himself** on pump.fun, very early (before creator fees), after a DM suggesting he make his own. Did it as a joke; **"didn't know anything about memecoins really at the time"**; picked meme numbers (420/69).
- It **pumped hard.** He now reads it plainly: **"turns out i was just the mass extraction event for whales and kols."** (Genuine hindsight/repudiation — he's not defending it.)
- **Profit: "little to nothing, maybe a couple \$1000"** — and it **went straight into development costs** (paying the team: Mara, Paul, Vince, designer Yordani). No personal extraction.
- He **holds nothing now**, has no ongoing involvement, and **would never raise the token in an investing context** ("too much of a red flag").
- (Separately: randos in China launched a **$elftech** token off his other work — that one *was* third-party. Not the relevant one.)

**So the play is repudiation, not denial** (Scenario B below). We cannot say "a third party did it" — Koto launched and promoted it. But the honest version is survivable and even sympathetic: *naive early experiment → minimal profit → reinvested into building the real product → genuine regret → fully disengaged.*

### The one honest weight to carry
It pumped and **dumped on retail** — some fans likely lost money. Don't minimize that; owning it is what makes the repudiation credible. The fatal version is *discovered-not-disclosed*, or defending it. Disclosed-and-repudiated survives.

### ⚠️ Gently correct Koto's instinct
Koto's "I'd never bring up the token" is **half right**: don't *foreground* it, never use it as an asset. But "never mention it" fails the moment a funder Googles (it pumped — it's very discoverable). The plan isn't silence; it's **clean up + have an honest account ready + pre-empt with candor when it counts.** Silence reads as hiding; candor reads as integrity.

### The tailored disclosure line (back-pocket; refine wording)
> *"One bit of history, so you hear it from us: early in Kotopia's life Koto launched a small meme token on pump.fun — naively, as a joke, before he understood how these work. It pumped, and in hindsight it mostly benefited speculators at retail's expense, which he regrets. He earned a nominal amount — on the order of a thousand dollars — all of which went into paying the team building the actual product. Neither Koto nor the project holds any token, earns anything from it, or has any ongoing involvement, and we won't build on it. We're flagging it ourselves because we'd rather be the ones to tell you."*

Send *if asked / if discovery is likely*; don't broadcast it unprompted.

## The cleanup checklist (do this BEFORE the first funder conversation)
- [ ] Remove all token references/links from **official surfaces**: kotopia.world, kaios.chat, shop, pinned tweets, bios, Discord, link-in-bio.
- [ ] Clean the meme/crypto-CTA posts from the *official-facing* timeline (the "take the koto pill 💊 / bullish" register). Keep the playful voice; drop the financial-CTA framing.
- [ ] Kill any active mechanics tied to the token — no treasury reliance, no rewards, no "buy" prompts, no roadmap line implying tokenomics.
- [ ] Make sure the **shop stays fiat-only** (it already is — good; keep it that way).

## The money structure (the strongest signal)
Route grant money through a **clean vehicle that has never touched crypto** — a distinct entity or a fiscal-sponsor arrangement for the safety-facing project/program, separate from anything the token ever touched. "The funds go into a structure with no token in its history" is worth more than any statement.

## The disclosure artifact (have it ready; don't broadcast it)
Write the 2–3 sentence factual statement (Scenario A or B wording above) and **keep it in your back pocket** for due diligence. Send it *if asked*, or fold one honest sentence into the pitch pre-emptively ("one note of history, so you hear it from us first: …"). Pre-empting with candor is powerful; a loud public "WE DISAVOW CRYPTO" campaign is a mistake — it magnifies the thing and invites scrutiny you'd otherwise avoid.

## Timing & sequencing
1. Establish facts (Step 0).
2. Clean official surfaces + confirm clean money structure.
3. Draft the disclosure statement.
4. *Then* start funder conversations (Manifund first — most crypto-tolerant — per [[Funder map]]).
Order matters: if a funder Googles mid-process, the official surfaces should already be clean so the token reads as an abandoned/third-party artifact, not a live mechanic.

## What NOT to do
- ❌ A loud public disavowal campaign (magnifies it).
- ❌ "We don't endorse it" while the token is still linked in the bio (contradiction = worse than silence).
- ❌ Denying team involvement if it existed (screenshots exist; dishonesty with *this* crowd is fatal).
- ❌ Anything that rug-pulls or harms existing holders to look clean.

## Residual read for the pitch
- **Manifund first is now even more clearly right** (most crypto-tolerant entry) — [[Funder map]].
- Expect a real headwind with the reputation-sensitive funders (Open Phil, SFF). Not disqualifying given the honest story, but weight it: lead with the shipped product + the safety thesis, keep the token a footnote you control.
- Make sure the grant-facing entity/structure has **never touched token proceeds** on paper (the ~$1k went to dev costs early — document that it's historical, not part of the funded vehicle).
