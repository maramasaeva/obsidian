two quotes, seven pages apart, in *The Essential Slate Star Codex* (ed. gregor stocks):

*"divine grace is more complicated. I was tempted to call it spontaneous order until I remembered the rationalist proverb that if you don't understand something, you need to call it by a term that reminds you that you don't understand it or else you'll think you have explained it when you've just named it."* — Scott Alexander (p. 53, *In Favor of Niceness, Community, and Civilization*)

*"Cthulhu may swim slowly, but he only swims left."* — Mencius Moldbug (quoted, p. 60)

scott's proverb (riffing on yudkowsky — "semantic stopsigns," "fake explanations") says: name the mystery in a way that *reminds* you it's still mysterious, otherwise the name will feel like the explanation. moldbug's cthulhu is technically obedient. "cthulhu" doesn't claim to explain why society drifts left — it says *there is a force, and we don't know what it is*. lovecraftian horror as stopsign. the rationalist move done correctly.

but moldbug then builds an entire political program on top of cthulhu's swim, treating the name as if it diagnosed the drift. exactly the failure scott was warning against. p.53 and p.60 are next-door for a reason — the codex is staging the argument. stocks knew what he was doing.

*[? — land naming "cthulhu" specifically as his cipher for capital / the Outside: unverified. land + CCRU are saturated in lovecraft (the numogram, Cthelll, the lemurian demonology, *meltdown*'s capital-as-autonomous-horror) and the underlying parallel holds — both moldbug and land drink from the lovecraftian well, both refuse to "contain" what they name. but land mostly worked with his own / CCRU's entities. needs sourcing before this paragraph stands.]*

and then there is the ccru side, where it gets interesting. land grabbed cthulhu from the same lovecraft well but used it the other way — not as a thing to fight but as the Outside, the hyperstitional abomination, capital-as-cthulhu rising from the deep. for land, cthulhu's swim *is* the point. the unknowability is generative, not threatening. you don't try to slow it. you ride it. [[Spyrodynamism]]'s older sibling.

three scenes — rationalism, NRx, CCRU — all reaching for the same lovecraftian name. all agreeing it points at something they cannot name otherwise. but the rationalists treat it as the limit of explanation, moldbug treats it as the cause to combat, land treats it as the becoming to accelerate. same stopsign. three different politics layered on top of it.

cthulhu *is* doing the work the proverb asks for. what each scene does after they've named it is the politics.
