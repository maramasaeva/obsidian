Quote/moment:

*"we can never make a strict distinction between what Husserl used to call primary and secondary retention, between perception and imagination — in other words, between reality and fiction"* (p. 49)

My claim:

we can only know what we believe we think we know. the rest — what we don't know — can never truly be known. this is basically saying we cannot know what we don't know, but what's sharper is the inverse: what we *do* know is inherently a fiction, because knowing is incited by a primal impulse. a primal impulse is what makes you *want* to know. so the act of knowing is already contaminated by desire, by the drive that initiated it. knowing in itself is not something you can trust.

this collapses the distinction husserl tried to hold between perception (primary retention — the just-past still ringing in the present) and imagination (secondary retention — memory, reconstruction). if both are generated by the same primal impulse, and if that impulse is itself a kind of fiction-engine, then perception is just imagination with better latency.

→ this feeds directly into the next page's claim about hope. if reality and fiction are structurally indistinguishable, then desire becomes the real operating system. and desire with no delay = no hope needed.

### the primal impulse — unpacked

husserl's phenomenology of time-consciousness has three layers: the *primal impression* (Urimpression) — the raw now-point, supposedly unmediated contact with what's actually there; *primary retention* — the just-past still ringing in the present (the note you heard a second ago that makes the current note part of a melody); and *secondary retention* — memory, recall, reconstruction. husserl wanted these clean. the primal impression was the anchor: pure, untouched.

stiegler's move: the anchor doesn't hold. what you *perceive* (primary retention) is already shaped by what you've *previously experienced* (secondary retention). your memories, cultural conditioning, expectations — they filter the "now" before it arrives. the primal impression was supposed to be pre-cognitive, pre-desire, just raw contact. but stiegler shows it's always already driven by *libido* in the broad psychoanalytic sense — not sexual desire but the energy of investment, the drive that makes you care about *this* thing rather than *that* one.

so the primal impulse = the pre-rational movement-toward that initiates all knowing. before you know anything, something has already made you *look*. that looking isn't neutral — it's desiring, affective, bodily. and if the impulse that starts the knowing is itself not rational, not transparent, not "true," then what it produces (knowledge) inherits that contamination. knowing is fiction not because it's wrong, but because it was never observation — it was always already a creative act initiated by a drive you didn't choose.

→ which is exactly why the culture industry claim on the next page lands so hard. if the industry can capture that primal impulse — redirect what you desire to attend to — it controls the conditions under which knowing begins. it doesn't need to lie to you. it just needs to aim your attention.
