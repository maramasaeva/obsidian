He states that: 'Throughout the 20th Century, music culture was a probe that played a major role in preparing the population heterosexual, a future in which the relinquishing of identities that were in any case poor fictions would be a blessed relief, in the 21st Century, by contrast – and very existence of contemporary culture is how genuinely this is anti-hauntology.'"* (p. 25)

*"if hauntology could be reframed as a reactive force, then anti-hauntology is a purely active one."* (p. 28)

Nietzsche/Deleuze distinction via Fisher: reactive forces = forces that only define themselves in relation to what they oppose (resentment, nostalgia, hauntology). active forces = forces that produce difference from within themselves, that push forward rather than resist. SOPHIE as active force: her music didn't argue against anything, it simply *was* something new.

trans aesthetic as anti-hauntological: *"the destruction and recreation of the digital images in the videos represent perfectly what producers like SOPHIE and Arca are trying to do with their music. They represent the new and innovative into the popular consciousness."* (p. 26) — breaking down what the image is supposed to look like, what the body is supposed to sound like.

My claim:

the trans aesthetic isn't just politically subversive ;  it's anti-hauntological in form. the body as site where the future arrives. transition as individuation in real time. what SOPHIE's music does to genre is what transition does to the gender binary: it doesn't oppose the structure, it renders it visibly fictional by building something that never existed before. this is the alignment argument in its sharpest form — we need artists who make *active* interventions, not reactive critiques.
