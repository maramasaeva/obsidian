Quote/moment:

*"Solarpunk aesthetics is also about developing an 'economy of potentialities,' it is about building a new world by Bernard Stiegler, that is out of and against the capitalist economy. It is about imagining a different future that may already be here and now. The pessimism of capitalist realism must be countered by the optimism of post-capitalism. But so is the future"* (p. 13-14)

*"[Solarpunk] breaks free of the Western-industrial world order in a way that would satisfy Yuk Hui's need for a techno-diversity. A truly solarpunk world would embrace vernacular knowledge and practices to build a world that is truly adaptable to its environment."* (p. 13-14)

*"solarpunk is inherently anti-colonial. Where the colonial project invokes a sense of displacement, an eeriness (in Fisher's sense of the word) brought about by forcibly implanting the lived environment of a particular culture into a society that neither wanted nor asked for it, solarpunk instead grows with the needs of that particular society, not displacing their culture, but adapting it."* (p. 14)

cyberpunk as aesthetic of the hauntological: *"Cyberpunk was one vision of the future, a dingy, dark, dystopian vision, but a vision that produced a kind of electric excitement"* — it imagined things as they were likely to go, not as they could be. the dystopian imagination as a trap: we built it because we saw it.

My claim:

the question of what aesthetics we make for the futures we want is literally a question of what futures we build. hyperstition operating through form. solarpunk is an attempt to make the counter-image. but — as Mara's note implies — it can't just be a "nicer version" of now. cosmotechnical diversity means starting from different assumptions entirely, not greening the existing order.
