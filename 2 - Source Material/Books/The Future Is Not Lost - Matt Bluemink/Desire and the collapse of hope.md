Quote/moment:

*"all over the world the image industry has destroyed the horizons of the libido and therefore the horizons of hope. nevertheless, if hauntology is a logic of despair, then anti-hauntology can be seen as a logic of hope."* (p. 50)

*"indeed, to lose one future is to find another."* (p. 50, bottom)

My claim:

every desire can be imagined, every desire can be fictionalised. and since there's no real distinction between reality and fiction (see [[Primary and secondary retention — knowing as fiction]]), we can desire basically anything and then immediately receive it — the imagination already *is* the having. this eliminates the need for hope as a tool to get what you want. and eliminating hope has a natural consequence: the organic establishment of a depressed state. you need hope for the future in order to want to live in that future, to see use in it.

the image industry destroyed the horizons of the libido by making all desires instantly available as images — and images *are* reality (since the perception/fiction collapse). so desire gets satisfied at the level of the image before it ever becomes a *drive toward* something. hope — the gap between wanting and having — collapses. and without that gap, depression fills the vacuum.

artists who have a lot of listeners, people that others look up to, people with a lot of narrative potential energy — as per [[mythomancy]] — they have this power and they should use it wisely. the capacity to shape desire at scale is a cosmotechnical weapon. you can reopen the horizon or you can flatten it further.

*"indeed, to lose one future is to find another."* → new [[cosmology]] first for music, the rest will follow. music as the probe-discipline. the first place where new futures arrive. if a new cosmology — a new total frame for what reality is and how it moves — enters through sound before it enters through theory, then the task is sonic before it's philosophical. build the music first. the worldview will crystallise around it.
