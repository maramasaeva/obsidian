*"music's power is a double-edged sword a pharmakōn. While music has the musical ability to try and innovate"* — Stiegler: music externalises consciousness, crystallizes it into structures. this means the music industry doesn't just distribute music — it distributes *forms of consciousness*, standardised and looped. the pharmakon cuts both ways: it can also shock, disrupt, re-open.

Stiegler on cosmotechnics and music from Simondon's angle: music isn't a product of consciousness, it's a condition of it. what we can hear shapes what we can think. industry standardization = attentional monoculture. new music = new attention.

My claim:

if music externalises and crystallises consciousness, then genuinely new music doesn't just sound different — it *makes* different kinds of thinking possible. SOPHIE's music made new subjective positions available. that's not metaphor — it's a literal claim about the pharmakon. this is why art matters for alignment: it doesn't just represent possible futures, it *produces* the cognitive conditions under which different futures become thinkable.

→ link this to Bratton/KAIOS: if AI is also an externalisation of cognitive processes, then the aesthetics of AI — how AI is expressed, performed, made perceptible — are also a form of cosmotechnical intervention.
