*underlined passages on p. 38 — the idea of SOPHIE as icon, as endpoint and re-opening.*

My claim:

SOPHIE as the icon of the end of music — not death but inflection point. not a final note but the last note of the old scale. she didn't just make music of the future, she *incited* a whole lineage of music that wouldn't exist without her. chain remover, nina jiraci, the entire scene of producers who blend digestibility and experimentalism in sound design — they carry her signal forward. grimes said the same thing in interviews: wanting to make the music of the future. but SOPHIE *did* it, and the proof is in the artists who followed.

there's a counter-claim worth holding: a lot of underground artists are doing this blending of experimental and accessible *right now*, very well, but the music industry as it exists today makes it incredibly hard to get heard. so the perception that these new sounds aren't being made is a distribution problem, not a creation problem. the sounds exist — you find them if you pay attention, if you dig. the industry's failure to surface them is UCI at work again: the destranging machine filtering out what it can't recombine.
