Quote/moment:

*"SOPHIE was the exact opposite of the cultural malaise that Fisher had written about. her album Oil of Every Pearl's Un-insides was still burdened with the weight of a pessimistic diagnosis of hauntology. Even in its most progressive forms; hauntology was still burdened with the weight of a depression, while SOPHIE's music was filled with hope. As Byung-Chul Han writes: 'Depression is the exact opposite of the zest, that liberates us from our depression, from an exhausted future' (Han 2024, 15). This music, the new, the future, would become music of the hopeful, the music of tomorrow."* (p. 11)

*"I had discovered the music of the future."* (p. 11)

*"SOPHIE's music was something truly unique. Her interplay of abrasive sounds and textures with pop-style vocals and melodies was a sonic combination that seemed to be pushing the boundaries of what pop music was capable of."* (p. 19)

*"SOPHIE herself was a transgender woman who played with the current landscape of popular music and leftist culture, and subverted the concepts of identity in her music."* (p. 26)

producer Iglooghost on SOPHIE's legacy: *"TO ME, SOPHIE'S LEGACY IS UP THERE WITH DELIA DERBYSHIRE, KRAFTWERK, JUAN ATKINS, APHEX, FLY LO, ENO, ETC. MY HEART IS SPLIT IN TWO. REST EASY..."* (p. 27)

SOPHIE died in Athens in February 2021. the book circles her loss as its gravitational center — not hauntologically (mourning a lost future) but anti-hauntologically (her work as proof the future arrived, briefly, in music).

My claim:

SOPHIE is proof of concept. the argument that genuinely new aesthetic forms can open imaginative space for futures we couldn't previously conceive; that argument has a sonic body. it wasn't theoretical. people heard Oil of Every Pearl and something changed in what they thought was possible. this is what new art does to alignment: not by prescribing solutions but by expanding the space of what can be imagined as good.
