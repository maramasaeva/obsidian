Quote/moment:

*"'The destranging of music culture in the 21st Century is haunted by the spectre of the past, and it is precisely this ghostly image that it cannot seem to move away from: "The destroying of music culture in the 21st Century – the ghastly return of industry moguls and boys next door to mainstream pop; the premium put on 'reality' in popular entertainment; the increased tendency of those in music culture to dress and emote in singing – has played a major role in conditioning us to accept consumer capitalism's model of ordinariness"'"* (p. 23)

*"Fisher essentially sees that contemporary music has been trapped in a cycle of repetition that has allowed the capitalist culture industries to trap listeners in a state of suspended animation; a state through which novel and new ideas are not being created or even expected by the listener."* (p. 23)

*"there's no doubt that his diagnosis is an apt one for the particular aspects of culture that he describes, but as the 21st Century has matured, a whole host of new sounds have started to enter the public consciousness, and these, I would argue, are something completely new."* (p. 23)

hauntology as cultural-economic capture: the machine that turns everything into a repetition of what sold before. not just nostalgia as feeling but as structure — capitalism eating its own tail aesthetically, using the past as safety net. the "destranging" = making the strange familiar, stripping shock from any form that threatens the model.

My claim:

UCI at work in culture. the music industry as a UCI sub-organ: it doesn't want shock, wants recognition. the recombinant is lucrative; the genuinely new is a risk. fisher sees this clearly — but his diagnosis was of a transitional period, not an endpoint. bluemink's counter: the 21st century has already started producing something fisher didn't see coming in time.
