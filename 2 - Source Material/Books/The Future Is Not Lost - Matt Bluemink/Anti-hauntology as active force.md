the distinction sbordoni draws is crucial: hauntology (fisher/derrida) = mourning for lost futures, the mood of the ghost. anti-hauntology = not nostalgia's inverse (optimism, "it will be fine") but something harder — *hope as structural openness to the unknown*. byung-chul han: hope is blind to negativity, its time is closed unto itself. not the same as confidence. not prediction. pure opening.

My claim: 

this is where the alignment thread lives. if we are trapped in hauntological culture — recycling aesthetics, unable to imagine genuinely new futures — we cannot imagine good ones either. anti-hauntology isn't just a music theory move, it's an *epistemic* move. new art opens imaginative space that wasn't there before. you can't engineer a future you cannot see. SOPHIE proved it sonically. the question for KAIOS: what does anti-hauntological AI sound like?
