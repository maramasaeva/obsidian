Quote/moment: *"If Laozi were alive today, he'd be great at Twitter... Twitter is a platform that excels at misunderstanding. You could go on there and remark that the sky is blue and some would be outraged... To be good at Twitter, you have to not care that you'll be misunderstood. To be great at Twitter, you have to not care and also say interesting things."* (p. 120)

and then: *"He is a troll in the best sense of the word... someone who is meant to sting and shock and unsettle — but for a purpose beyond mere emotional manipulation, beyond the haha."* (p. 122)

and: *"Only when we've been jolted out of the conventional, the self-evident, the unquestioned, the accepted... can we see the deeper patterns, the lasting patterns that form and are formed by Dao."* (p. 122)

My claim: ken liu is describing a specific communication mode — trolling-as-dao-practice. the troll stings and unsettles not to manipulate but to break the grip of the conventional so you can see the deeper pattern underneath. this is not the same as provocation for attention. it's provocation as *care*. and it maps onto exactly the communication problem plzdontkillus is trying to solve: how do you say something about existential risk that actually *jolts* people out of their default, without being doom-content or mere manipulation?

laozi's answer: you say things that are easily misunderstood. you accept that misunderstanding is the cost of saying something true. you don't optimize for engagement — you optimize for the jolt. the understanding comes later, in the people who stick around.

this also connects to the aella thing. aella's communication style is laozi-on-twitter — she says things that sting, that get misunderstood, that draw massive attention, and she doesn't care about the misunderstanding. the question is whether the purpose behind it is beyond mere haha.
