Quote/moment: *"Temporality is thereby replaced with a spatiality and human experience is constrained by being rendered one-dimensional, computable, predictable and profitable."* (p. 83) and: *"Can we therefore speak of a complex world of overlapping Machinic Language-Games? Rhizomatic Families of Robots? Algorithmic Plateaus of Immanence?"* (p. 108)

My claim: vachnadze identifies the computational episteme as a machine for spatialising time — reducing lived duration to sequential data points. but this spatialisation is not neutral. it is a *cosmotechnical* operation. it belongs to the greek-western tradition that hui describes: geometry as the foundation of both technics and justice, the cosmos understood through measurement, time exteriorised through geometric marks in the sand ([[The circle, the zero, the spiral — geometry as cosmotechnics]]).

the chinese didn't develop systematic geometry, and they didn't address time in the same way. shi (時) = the four seasons. time as cyclical, situational, connected to propensity (shi/勢) and occasion. not measured but *felt*. this is not a failure — it's a different cosmotechnics of time ([[reading guide — what hui is actually saying]], §20).

three temporalities emerge:

1. **western-computational time**: linear, spatialized, measurable. one moment after another. the algorithm's clock. produces the quantified self.
2. **bergsonian duration**: qualitative, heterogeneous, incomputable. "an internal multiplicity of succession, of fusion, of organization" (deleuze). resists spatialisation. produces the wittgensteinian musician who listens to the voices.
3. **chinese situational time**: cyclical, relational, seasonal. time as occasion, not measurement. the void accumulates in winter; what was stored grows in summer. produces ganying, resonance, the butcher who follows the joints.

deleuze's rhizome — the horizontal network with no central root, any point connecting to any other — maps onto needham's description of the chinese organic mode: beings following "the internal dictates of their own natures" within a hierarchy of wholes, no cosmic lawgiver issuing commands from above. mara already made this connection in the hui reading guide. the "deleuze-computable" ontology vachnadze speculates about at the end of chapter three may be structurally closer to chinese cosmotechnics than to western computation. rhizomatic families of robots = beings-in-relation, not beings-under-law. algorithmic plateaus of immanence = the ten thousand things following their own natures on the plane of dao.

the spiral ([[Spyrodynamism]]) is the shape that holds all three: the circle (chinese cyclical return) set into qualitative motion (bergsonian duration), moving without arriving, returning without repeating. the spiral is the anti-algorithm — the shape that cannot be spatialised without losing what it is.
