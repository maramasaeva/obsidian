Quote/moment: *"Why is it that an apologetic for the contemporary algocracy, despite all its claims to secularity and its attempts to exclude the mythical, tends to sound increasingly like an Eschatology of Artificial Intelligence."* (p. 18)

My claim: this is the book's central question, stated plainly. the algocratic regime — governance by algorithm — insists it's secular, rational, evidence-based. and yet. the discourse around AI is saturated with eschatological language: the singularity (= the second coming), alignment (= salvation), existential risk (= apocalypse), superintelligence (= god). this isn't metaphor. it's structure.

vachnadze doesn't say the tech people are secretly religious. he says the discursive formation they operate in — the regime of truth that makes their claims thinkable — has the same architecture as christian eschatology. u don't need to believe in god to think eschatologically. u just need to operate within a cosmotechnics that was built on eschatological foundations and never fully dismantled them.

the claims to secularity are the most revealing part. the louder the denial of myth, the more mythical the structure. "we're not doing religion, we're doing science" is itself a religious move — the same move the early church made when it said "we're not doing pagan philosophy, we're doing revelation."
