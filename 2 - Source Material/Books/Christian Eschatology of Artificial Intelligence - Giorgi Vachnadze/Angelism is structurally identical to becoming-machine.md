Quote/moment: *"Who wills themselves angel makes themselves a beast and angelicism is structurally identical to the becoming of the Machine on that count"* (p. 18, louis morelle, introduction to carrière's *technically man dwells upon this earth*)

My claim: the quote comes from a long tradition. the original is often attributed to pascal: *qui veut faire l'ange fait la bête* — whoever tries to play the angel ends up playing the beast. the idea: when u try to transcend ur animal nature, u don't become more than human — u become less. the attempt to escape the body produces a new kind of monstrosity.

morelle's addition is the key move: angelism and becoming-machine are the SAME structure. when u will urself to be an angel — pure spirit, pure reason, no flesh, no mess — u are doing the exact same thing as when u will urself to be a machine: pure computation, pure function, no ambiguity, no contingency. both are attempts to escape the human condition by denying the body, the irrational, the uncomputable.

the irony: the angel and the machine are both beasts. not because they're evil, but because the denial of the animal IS the most animal move. the drive to transcend biology is itself a biological drive. the loop closes: becoming-angel = becoming-machine = becoming-beast. u can't will urself out of the flesh. the flesh is where u think from.

this connects to the AI enhancement discourse: the dream of uploading consciousness, of merging with the machine, of transcending biological limits — it's angelism in silicon. and the result, vachnadze implies, will be the same beast it always was.
