## the argument so far

### preface: how not to read this book (pp. 1-3)

the preface is a warning label. vachnadze says: do NOT read this as pro-christianity or pro-AI. the point is not to side with either regime of truth (= the system that decides what counts as true in a given society — what statements are accepted, what mechanisms distinguish true from false, who gets to speak with authority) — it's to *short-circuit* both by forcing each to acknowledge its structural affinity with the other ([[Short-circuiting two regimes of truth]]). christian-pastoral techniques and technocratic-neoliberal technologies of the self (= the specific practices through which a person shapes herself into a particular kind of subject — confession, prayer, fasting for the christian; self-tracking, therapy apps, social media curation for the neoliberal) claim to be opposites. they're not. they draw on the same forms of subjugation, produce the same types of subjects, generate the same docility (= the state of being manageable, trainable, compliant — not through brute force but through techniques that make the subject WANT to comply).

the method is foucauldian: tactics before strategy, micro before macro, always open to readjustment. this is not a manifesto. it describes *"a particular way that power tends to operate under contemporary circumstances and how similar it is to another historical period where power tended to manifest in a surprisingly similar manner."*

### introduction: bio-politics of artificial intelligence (pp. 5-30)

the intro moves through five zones:

**1. the setup: two regimes, one technique (pp. 5-8)**

starting from dreyfus ("the story of artificial intelligence might well begin around 450 B.C."), vachnadze frames this as AI biopolitics (= governance that operates on life itself — bodies, health, populations, habits — rather than through laws and punishment) — not what AI is, but what it requires from its subjects: what do u have to believe, how do u have to behave, what kind of person do u have to become to accept AI as legitimate? drawing on foucault's *confessions of the flesh*, he traces how the specific practices early christians used to shape themselves into good christian subjects (2nd-5th century) — confession, self-examination, fasting, sexual renunciation — map onto the specific practices digital consumers use to shape themselves into good neoliberal subjects today — self-tracking, optimising, curating, disclosing.

the key insight from coeckelbergh: the idea of a perfect transcendent being resonates on *both* sides of the divide ([[The perfect transcendent being resonates on both sides]]). the religiousness of tech and the technicity of religion are not metaphors — they're structural.

foucault's archaeology (= his method of digging into the hidden rules that determine what can be thought and said in a given era — not what people believed, but the invisible structure underneath that made those beliefs possible) treats history as a field, not a space of objects ([[History as field, not space of objects]]). events emerge spontaneously, they don't derive from origins. this is crucial methodologically: vachnadze is not arguing that christianity *caused* AI. he's arguing they emerge from the same field.

**2. three regimes: aphrodisia, flesh, and "liberated" sexuality (pp. 9-13)**

here vachnadze traces the shift from greco-roman to medieval christian self-governance. the medieval period introduces meticulous categorisation, an entire metrics system of transgressions. the self is no longer the object of care — it's the site of negation. *one must make sure that the self has been handed over to the divine element* ([[Hand the self over to the divine element]]).

sexuality plays a crucial but contingent role — it's one of many technologies of governance, not the essence of identity ([[Sexuality as contingent technology of power]]). the shift from self-retreat (greek: withdraw from excess, find moderation) to self-ascension (christian: negate urself entirely to rise toward god) changes the whole structure of what desire is FOR — the greeks wanted to manage desire wisely, the christians wanted to redirect it away from the self entirely.

with christianity, logos becomes the mediating current between man and god, displacing the pagan sage ([[Logos as mediating current]]). christ teaches the logos; the pastor mediates between christ and the flock. the mediator chain: god → logos → christ → pastor → subject. compare to: truth → reason → scientist → algorithm → user.

the christian self-examination aims to separate impressions coming from god from those imposed by satan. this is the debugging of the soul. today's equivalent: separating productive engagement from distraction, signal from noise, aligned from misaligned.

**3. cybernetics as biblical canon: "effective procedure" as dogma (pp. 14-17)**

dreyfus shows that the equation of thought with calculation goes back to plato. the cybernetic paradigm — human as symbol-manipulating device — holds three unsubstantiated assumptions ([[Thought equals calculation — the cybernetic dogma]]):

1. neural activity = binary (on/off, true/false)
2. human experience = data processing
3. all natural processes = formalizable

these are not findings. they're cosmological commitments (= beliefs about the fundamental nature of the universe that are taken as self-evident rather than proven — like "the universe is orderly" or "everything follows rules"). the confidence in AI comes from a cosmotechnics that was already in place — the same one that produced the christian cosmos of a rational designer.

page 17 is the hinge: *"the idea of the world operating as a closed, quantifiable virtuality; the notion that the universe is a giant automaton"* runs suspiciously close to christian intelligent design. the cybernetic paradigm is not a secular alternative to theology — it's theology wearing a lab coat.

**4. technically man resists upon this earth (pp. 18-20)**

morelle's devastating line: *"who wills themselves angel makes themselves a beast and angelicism is structurally identical to the becoming of the Machine"* ([[Angelism is structurally identical to becoming-machine]]). trying to transcend the body — whether into angelic purity or machinic computation — produces the same monstrosity. the flight from flesh IS the flesh's most characteristic move.

this frames the book's central question: *"why is it that an apologetic for the contemporary algocracy, despite all its claims to secularity and its attempts to exclude the mythical, tends to sound increasingly like an Eschatology of Artificial Intelligence"* ([[Algocracy sounds increasingly like an eschatology]]).

via carrière: there's a difference between *thinking about* something (analyzing it from outside, treating it as an object) and *thinking thinks* (being inside the thinking, where thought is not a tool u use but something that happens through u). power works the same way — it's not something imposed on u from outside, it's woven into how u relate to urself. the AI discourse traps us in *thinking about* intelligence — treating it as an engineering problem to solve — and never lets us dwell inside thinking itself.

AI discourse seeks to reduce thought to a techne (= a craft, a skill, a procedure that can be taught, repeated, and optimised — the opposite of something mysterious or irreducible) and uncover the biological system as a technology that manifests thinking ([[Obey is mirrored in improve — biopolitical docility]]). the christian injunction to obey is mirrored in the neoliberal imperative to improve. both produce subjects who govern themselves on behalf of a power that doesn't need to force them — the obedient christian and the self-optimising consumer are both doing the work of power voluntarily.

**5. diana stypinska's digital confessional and the spectral subject (pp. 24-30)**

stypinska's key contribution: the pastorate (= the christian mode of governance where a pastor guides each individual soul — not through law but through personal knowledge, confession, and care, like a shepherd who knows each sheep) didn't end — it dispersed into platforms. social media operates like a christian confessional producing "spectral subjects" (= ghost-like selves, transparent, with no substance of their own — pure relays for content, whose identity IS their performance). the algorithm is the pastor. self-disclosure is confession. engagement is penance.

*"the persistence of 'capitalism as a religion'"* ([[Capitalism as a religion — AI as the new god]]). AI discourse operates like christian discourse on god — always promising, always demanding, always just beyond.

*"the self is where the devil hides"* ([[The self is where the devil hides — value alignment as original sin]]). the subject can't govern herself because she's inherently fallen/sinful. this is the deep structure of value alignment: AI is treated as inherently misaligned, in need of external correction. but why should a more capable mind accept governance by a less capable one?

the therapy-oriented discourse of social media produces *"marketable personalities and stylized entrepreneurs of the self"*. self-care becomes love of the grind. *"we desire the desire of the Other"* ([[We desire the desire of the other — self-care as grind]]). the gamified apparatus is disciplinary the moment u stop performing. self-love is content production. the digital confessional is complete.

### chapter 1: bionic christ and the diagram of the flesh (pp. 33-67)

this is the longest and densest chapter. it's essentially a close reading of foucault's *confessions of the flesh* — the 4th volume of the *history of sexuality*, published posthumously. vachnadze uses it to extract the structural model (the "diagram") of how christian pastoral power governs subjects through their bodies and desires. the chapter moves through five technologies of the self, each one building on the last, each one scaling the pastoral apparatus wider.

**1. from aphrodisia to flesh: how the greeks got absorbed (pp. 33-36)**

the greek regime of sexual self-conduct — what foucault calls *aphrodisia* — was a loose set of recommendations for living well. moderation, balance, pleasure in the right measure. the early church fathers (especially clement of alexandria) borrowed heavily from this — clement literally copies the stoic philosopher musonius rufus almost word for word, without crediting him, while meticulously citing every biblical source. the plagiarism is revealing: it shows how christianity absorbed greek practices while erasing their origin.

but the absorption changed the content. the greek *logos* was a material substance — the word of the sage, achievable through human effort. the christian logos became the word of god — transcendent, divine, taught by christ and mediated through the pastor. a virtuous life under the greeks meant living wisely before death. a virtuous life under christianity meant preparing for eternal life AFTER death. same practices, different teleology (= the "what for" — where this is all heading).

**2. kairos and the diagram: sexuality as production, not repression (pp. 36-39)**

*kairos* is a concept from both greek and christian thought: the right conditions, the right moment for an action to have positive value. it's NOT a list of prohibitions. the diagram of the flesh doesn't work by saying "don't do X." it works by actively channeling desire through approved paths — inciting certain sexual practices while marking others as wasteful.

this is crucial for the parallel to today: the contemporary regime of sexuality doesn't repress desire either. it PRODUCES it, channels it, commodifies it. every sexual practice today is in principle monetizable — what constitutes the cybernetic-neoliberal kairos. the paths have multiplied but the system of control remains the same.

clement's diagram is simple: *no sex outside marriage, no marriage without the explicit aim of procreation*. everything else — all the recommendations, rules, incitements — flows from this formula. but then a genuinely interesting problem: how do u *consecrate* the sexual act? how does something sinful become holy? the answer: logos must mediate. sex within the diagram isn't sinful because the logos governs it. nature "at its best" — procreation — is itself a holy act. u follow the logos by imitating nature's design.

the body that transgresses the diagram isn't punished by god — god simply *abandons* it. the body rots. it's not divine anger, it's divine withdrawal. the logos-light goes out and the flesh is left to decay on its own.

**3. baptism as political technology (pp. 40-46)**

baptism looks like a one-time ritual but it's actually a total system of self-formation. foucault identifies four functions:

- **cleansing** — washing away sin, purifying the past
- **marking** — imprinting the sign of god, *"like a seal at the bottom of a document, the brand on livestock, or the tattoo on a soldier's arm"*
- **rebirth** — being born again out of one's own free choice, not the passive birth imposed by parents
- **illumination** — scattering the darkness, filling the soul with light

all four happen simultaneously in one act. the greek word for this transformation is *metanoia* (= complete metamorphosis of the self — not gradual improvement but total rupture, an affective transformation more akin to sudden inspiration than to disciplined practice).

baptism is a political takeover. vachnadze reads foucault reading it as a *coup d'état of the soul*: the territory (= the body and soul of the subject) that was occupied by demonic forces gets annexed by the holy spirit. the subject's internal system of governance changes hands. *"baptism had the effect of producing a 'dispossession'... the Holy Spirit could establish its seat in the soul liberated by purification; and man acquired a force stronger than that of the demons."*

the preparation for baptism involved *probatio* (= fitness testing of the convert), which had three techniques:
- **interrogatory investigation** — a series of questions and answers in a private setting, with priests, sponsors, and the penitent present. followed by a period of training, education, and evaluation. *"did they honor the widows? did they visit the sick? have they done all kinds of good works?"*
- **tests of exorcism** — the penitent stands barefoot in a sackcloth of goat's hair (symbolizing their old satanic self), remaining perfectly still while the priest denounces them. standing still = proving you've been freed from impure spirits.
- **confession of sins** (*exomologesis*) — a sincere presentation of oneself as guilty, first to god and then to the priest. telling the truth about oneself is essential to purification and salvation.

this whole apparatus — the testing, the training, the evaluation, the second chances — is what vachnadze calls the prototype for all subsequent pastoral technologies, including the digital ones.

**4. two technologies of confession: exomologesis vs. exagoreusis (pp. 44-55)**

this is the most technically important section. foucault distinguishes two fundamentally different confession technologies:

***exomologesis*** = dramatic, bodily, public. the penitent performs her sinfulness through physical self-mortification — humiliation, fasting, sackcloth, public weeping. it's theatrical. the point is to manifest the sinful truth of the self outwardly, through the body. it's a spectacle of guilt.

***exagoreusis*** = internal, verbal, continuous. this is the monastic version. the monk doesn't perform his guilt — he verbalizes every thought, every impression, every flicker of the mind to an elder. the elder helps him distinguish which thoughts come from god and which from the devil. it's debugging, not theater. and it never stops — it's a constant, lifelong process of internal surveillance.

the key question exagoreusis asks: *who is speaking?* as in — this thought in my head right now, is it ME thinking it, is it god thinking through me, or is it satan? the monk must pose this question to his *cogitatio* (= the raw material of thoughts, intentions, motivations — the inner life) at every moment. *"thoughts flutter around in the mind like a feather moved by the wind."* the smallest act of mental spontaneity is dangerous — an idle thought could cascade into a spiritual catastrophe.

the role of verbalization is crucial: what resists being spoken aloud contains evil. what a thought tries to keep hidden is evidence of its satanic origin. *"what resists light is darkness, is the source of deception."* confession operates as a sorting mechanism — bringing thoughts into the light neutralizes their power.

(massive parallel to today: social media operates on the same principle — what u don't share becomes suspicious. the imperative to disclose, to be transparent, to "show up authentically" IS exagoreusis scaled to the platform. the algorithm is the elder who helps u distinguish ur authentic self from ur inauthentic impulses.)

**and then the genuinely brilliant comparison: stoic vs. christian self-examination (pp. 47-50)**

the stoic version (seneca) is a nightly review: what did i do today? what went wrong? how can i reprogram myself for tomorrow? foucault literally uses the word ***"programming"*** to describe seneca's method. the stoic self is *re-writable code* — there is no fixed essence, no divine blueprint. u examine, u identify errors, u rewrite. the purpose is not guilt but improvement. no dramatics, no shame.

the christian version is completely different: the self has a fixed divine blueprint "programmed" by god. u can't rewrite it. u can only *reveal* it by stripping away the layers of sin and deception. the stoic debugs her own code. the christian uncovers the code god already wrote.

two completely different models of selfhood — and both of them use computational language centuries before computers exist:
- stoic: the self as user-modifiable software (re-programming)  
- christian: the self as read-only firmware with a divine signature (revealing the pre-installed program)

the monastic system that develops from exagoreusis produces what stypinska calls the *spectral subject*: total obedience, complete destruction of one's own will, the self disappears into the anonymous structure of rules and recursions. the monk is the prototype of the digital spectral subject — a pure function, a relay, a node in the network with no autonomy of its own. *"the subject disappears into the anonymous structure of codifications, rules, repetitive actions and endless recursions of the same."*

**5. virginity, marriage, and the scaling of the apparatus (pp. 56-67)**

the last movement of the chapter traces how the diagram of the flesh scales from the monastery to the entire population through two technologies: virginity and marriage.

***virginity*** is NOT repression of the body — it's a technology of ascension. the body is preserved, cared for, maintained as a vehicle. virginity is an exercise in self-care, hygiene, bodily discipline. its purpose: to allow the mind to take flight into the heavenly kingdom while the body remains in a "perfect, resurrected state" on earth. vachnadze's line: *"somewhat satirically one could claim: Christ was always Bionic."* virginity is the original enhancement technology — an upgrade, a supplement, a patch for the fallen human operating system. the body becomes a platform for uploading consciousness to god.

purity was the "metrics" for measuring "human capital" in the 3rd century. virginity was actively *produced*, shaped, rewarded — it was the ancient christian equivalent of entrepreneurial hustle culture. the cult of virginity operated exactly like the contemporary cult of productivity: a lifestyle marketed as freedom but functioning as an extremely effective technology of governance.

but virginity was elite. it couldn't scale. so the pastoral apparatus expanded into ***marriage***. marriage is not the opposite of virginity — it's virginity's safety net, its backup plan. *"let marriage be left for the weak"* (methodius). but the weak are the majority. and the majority is where the real governance happens.

marriage as technology: it doesn't repress sexuality — it *produces* it within a controlled field. sex inside marriage isn't sinful; it's mandatory. marriage is *"virginity with an arity of 2"* — a binary function that normalizes desire. the matrimonial relation can be repurposed for any demographic end: increase births, decrease births, channel desire, manage the population. biopolitics in its purest form.

the final move: marriage begins to imitate the monastery. the spouses become like disciple and priest to each other, engaging in "mutual spiritual direction." what was an esoteric monastic practice — solitude, confession, self-examination — becomes a popular discourse among regular couples. the pastoral apparatus has completed its scaling: from monk to spouse to family to society. the diagram of the flesh is now *"multi-scalable."*

**what the chapter is really doing:**

chapter 1 is building the *structural model* that the rest of the book will use. the diagram of the flesh is not just a historical curiosity — it's the template for understanding how digital self-formation works today. every element has its contemporary equivalent:

| christian diagram | digital equivalent |
|---|---|
| exomologesis (bodily confession) | posting, performing ur identity |
| exagoreusis (verbalizing every thought to an elder) | continuous self-disclosure on social media |
| the elder who distinguishes god from satan | the algorithm that distinguishes authentic from inauthentic |
| baptism as political takeover of the soul | onboarding, account creation, accepting terms |
| probatio (fitness testing) | verification, credentialing, reputation systems |
| virginity as enhancement technology | biohacking, self-optimization, productivity culture |
| marriage as scaled-down monastery | the "relationship" as mutual self-surveillance |
| "programming" (foucault's word for seneca) | literal programming |
| the spectral monk (pure function, no autonomy) | the spectral digital subject (pure content, no self) |

## the argument in full (so far)

vachnadze's thesis is genealogical, not causal (= he's not saying christianity CAUSED AI — he's asking: what historical conditions of power made AI thinkable? what had to be true about a culture for it to invent this?): the discursive formation (= the invisible system of rules, assumptions, and categories that determines what can be thought and said in a given context) that makes AI thinkable shares its architecture with christian eschatology. the same assumptions (the universe is formal, the mind is computable, the self is fallen), the same techniques (confession, self-negation, obedience-as-freedom), the same teleology (= the belief that everything is moving toward a final purpose or end-state — the second coming, the singularity, the kingdom of god, the post-scarcity utopia). the secular algocracy is an eschatology of artificial intelligence — not because tech people are secretly religious, but because the regime of truth they operate in was built on foundations it never examined.

the introduction lays out the method and the central question. chapter 1 builds the structural model — the "diagram of the flesh" — by close-reading foucault's *confessions of the flesh*. it traces how the pastoral apparatus scaled from monastic practices (exomologesis, exagoreusis, probatio) through virginity as enhancement technology to marriage as population-level governance. the chapter's key discovery: foucault himself uses the word "programming" to describe stoic self-examination, and the stoic vs. christian difference maps precisely onto two models of computation — the self as re-writable software (stoic) vs. the self as read-only firmware with a divine signature (christian). the spectral monk — pure function, no autonomy — is the prototype of the spectral digital subject.

chapters 2-6 will presumably apply this model to contemporary technologies of self-formation.
