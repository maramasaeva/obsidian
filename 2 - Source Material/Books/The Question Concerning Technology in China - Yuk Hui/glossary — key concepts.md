Tags: [[cosmotechnics]], [[tao]], [[epistemology]]

a reference glossary for the chinese and greek philosophical terms in hui's book. organized by tradition, then alphabetically by romanisation. page numbers refer to where the concept is most clearly defined or first used substantively.

---

## chinese

**道 dao** — the way. the unconditioned ground of everything. present in every being but escaping all objectification. "the ungraspable that could yet be known" (p. 100). not a god, not a natural law in the european sense. the supreme order that is not imposed but inherent. zhuangzi says it's in the ant, in the grass, in the piss and shit (p. 67). zi ran is the law of dao.

**德 de** — virtue, moral power, inner force. not virtue in the christian sense — more like the inherent power that comes from being aligned with dao. yang corresponds to de, yin to punishment (p. 85). a ruler who governs by wu wei has de. a butcher who follows tianli has de.

**道器合一 dao qi he yi** — the unity of dao and qi. the way and the tool are not separate (p. 65). "dao needs qi to carry it in order to be manifested in sensible forms; qi needs dao in order to become perfect" (p. 101). the most fundamental principle of chinese cosmotechnics: the tool never leaves the path.

**感應 ganying** — feeling-response, resonance. literally "feeling" and "response." the mechanism of chinese cosmotechnics. heaven acts, you feel it. you act, heaven responds. the qin and se (p. 83) are the concrete, audible demonstration. joseph needham translates it as "resonance" (p. 27).

**機 ji** — trigger, mechanism, machine. one of three words related to qi (器). "that which controls the trigger" (p. 99). the most mechanical sense of the technical.

**機心 ji xin** — machine heart. from zhuangzi (p. 105): "where there are machines, there are bound to be machine worries; where there are machine worries, there are bound to be machine hearts, and without the pure and simple, the life of the spirit knows no rest." the machine changes not just what you do but what you are.

**君子 junzi** — the gentleman, the ideal person in confucian thought. **君子不器 junzi bu qi** = "the gentleman is not a utensil" (p. 100). unbounded, not reduced to a single function. the opposite of instrumentalisation.

**理 li** — principle, pattern, natural structure. **天理 tianli** = heavenly principle, the inherent pattern of things. what pao ding follows when he butchers the ox (p. 102) — the gaps between joints that are already there. li is not imposed from outside. it is the grain of reality.

**禮 li** — ritual, propriety, rites. different character from 理 li above. the social and ceremonial order. confucianism is deeply concerned with li as the structure of proper human relations. §10.2 is titled "restoring the li."

**器 qi** — tool, vessel, container, technical object. the character has the pictogram of four squares surrounding a dog — guarding containers (p. 99). the material, the concrete, the thing you can hold. counterpart to dao in the dao-qi unity.

**氣 qi** — vital energy, breath, life-force. different character from 器 qi above. the animating energy of all things. flows through the body, through the cosmos, through the relation between heaven and human.

**琴 qin / 瑟 se** — two stringed instruments. their harmony is the concrete, audible proof of ganying (p. 83). "the resonance between human and Heaven is not purely subjective, but as objective and concrete as the resonance of these musical instruments."

**天 tian** — heaven. not a god. a moral being (p. 81). both anthropomorphic and natural simultaneously. the stars, winds, and seasons are its indications. human activity must accord with it.

**天人合一 tianren heyi** — the unification of heaven and human. the foundational principle of chinese cosmology. human and cosmos are not separate domains but one continuous reality.

**五行 wu xing** — five phases. often mistranslated as "five elements." wood, fire, earth, metal, water — not static substances but dynamic phases of transformation. dong zhongshu introduced yin-yang and wu xing into confucianism (p. 84).

**無為 wu wei** — non-doing, non-intervention. not passivity. "a letting things be, leaving room for things to grow on their own, in the hope that beings will fully realise themselves and their potential" (p. 101). **無為之治 wu wei zhi zhi** = governing without intervening — the daoist political principle.

**械 xie** — shackle, holding device. also means machine. carries the connotation of binding, restraining. one of three words related to qi (器) (p. 99).

**玄學 xuan xue** — dark or mysterious learning (p. 87). a philosophical movement blending daoism and confucianism. attempted to describe the heaven-human relation as metaphysical unity.

**陰陽 yin yang** — the complementary forces. yin dwells in winter and accumulates in the void (p. 85). yang dwells in summer and occupies itself with growing. not opposites — complementary phases of a single process.

**自然 zi ran** — literally "self-so." often translated as "nature" but misleadingly. means things being what they are, without pretention, without forcing (p. 64). modern chinese uses it to mean "the environment" but the philosophical meaning is deeper — the unconditioned naturalness of things being themselves. the law of dao.

## greek

**ἀλήθεια aletheia** — truth, unconcealment. literally "un-forgetting." for heidegger, the fundamental meaning of truth: the revealing of what was hidden. western technics is oriented toward aletheia — bringing what is concealed into the light (p. 62).

**ἀρετή aretē** — excellence, virtue. what technē aims at. not just skill but the excellence that comes from skillful practice. the relation between technē and aretē is a core debate in plato and aristotle (pp. 92–95). courage, temperance, justice, wisdom are all forms of aretē.

**δεινότατον deinotaton** — the uncanniest of the uncanny. heidegger's reading of sophocles' antigone (p. 72): the human is the most uncanny being because it oversteps limits through technē. making things is an act of leaving home, becoming unheimlich.

**δίκη dikē** — justice, fittingness. not justice in the modern legal sense. heidegger translates it as fug — a joint, a structure, an arrangement directed toward something (p. 73). "dikē means something that can be fitted into the divine order" (p. 77). closely related to geometry — the cosmic order is a geometrical projection.

**Gestell** — (german, heidegger) enframing. the essence of modern technology. the culmination of productionist metaphysics — everything is revealed as standing-reserve, as resource to be ordered and optimised (p. 97).

**Machenschaft** — (german, heidegger) machination. the late stage of productionist metaphysics where everything becomes makeable, produceable, calculable (p. 97).

**ποίησις poiēsis** — making, production. produces an external object (ergon). distinguished from praxis (p. 95).

**πρᾶξις praxis** — acting, doing. its own end, produces no external product. virtue, friendship, thinking are praxis. the collapse of praxis into poiēsis — everything must produce — is the modern condition (p. 95).

**σωφροσύνη sōphrosynē** — temperance, self-control. one of the virtues that "hold together heaven and earth, and gods and men" (p. 91, from gorgias). part of the harmonious unity of the human cosmos.

**τέχνη technē** — craft, skill, art. for the greeks, a form of knowledge: "seeing" the universal, grasping the essence (p. 96). but also violence — overstepping limits, imposing form on resistant material (p. 71). technē mediates between physis (nature) and tychē (chance). it aims at the good but is not itself divine.

**θεία μοῖρα theia moira** — divine power, divine portion. what moves through the poet (p. 93). the poet is not the author but the channel for theia moira. when technē dissolves into something higher, the greeks explain it as divine possession.

**τύχη tychē** — chance, fortune, luck. what you're subject to when you lack technē (p. 91). "experience causes our time to march along the way of technē, whereas inexperience causes it to march along the way of tychē." the entire project of greek technics is to replace tychē with technē — to overcome randomness through skill.

**φύσις physis** — nature, the process of "bringing forth." the greek word for nature carries the connotation of growth, emergence. technē imitates physis and at the same time perfects it (p. 70). in chinese thought, this relation doesn't exist — technics is subordinate to the cosmological order, not in dialogue with "nature."
