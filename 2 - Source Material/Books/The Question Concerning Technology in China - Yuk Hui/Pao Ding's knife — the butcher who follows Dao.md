Quote/moment: *"Pao Ding concludes, a good butcher doesn't rely on the Qi (tool), but rather on Dao [...] Pao Ding once a year changes his knife because he directly chops the bones through tendons; a bad butcher has to change his knife every month, because he cuts through tendons, much less the bone. I never touch bones, or tendons. Following the natural structure (tianli, 天理), I cleave along the main seams and thrust the knife into the big cavities. I never touch veins or tendons, much less the bone."* (pp. 102–103)

*"according to him, the key to being a good butcher doesn't lie in his mastery of the skill, but rather in comprehending the Dao. Replying to a question from the prince Wen Hui about the Dao of butchering a cow, Pao Ding points out that having a good knife is not necessary enough: it is more important to understand the Dao in the cow, so that one must use the blade to confront the bones and tendons in order to enter into the perfection of a tool or a skill, since perfection can only be accomplished by Dao. When it is used in accordance with 'instrumental reason' — with functions such as chopping, and cutting — the knife performs sections that belong only to the cutting [...] the knife is guided by Dao, on the lower level of its being."* (pp. 103–104)

*"What I care about is the Dao, which goes beyond mere technique."* (p. 103, pao ding's words)

the knife enters the void: *"instead, it accomplishes the task of butchering the cow without difficulty: as it becomes the knife of the Dao and enters the toss without coming up against it with ease in doing [...] the knife finishes the task of butchering and then now without being endangered, resets itself as a knife."* (p. 104)

the well-digger who refuses the machine: *"There is a machine for the sort of thing [...] I've heard my teacher say, where there are machines, there are bound to be machine worries; where there are machine worries, there are bound to be machine hearts (機心). With a machine heart in your breast, you've spoiled what was pure and simple; and without the pure and simple, the life of the spirit knows no rest. Where the life of the spirit knows no rest, the Way [Dao] will cease to buoy you up."* (p. 105, from zhuangzi)

My claim: pao ding's knife never dulls because it never meets resistance. it follows the natural structure — the gaps between joints, the spaces between bones. it enters the void. the knife cuts through nothingness, through the spaces that are already there. it doesn't impose form on material. it follows the form that the material already has.

this is dao-technics as opposed to techné-violence ([[Techné as violence — Heidegger on the Greek relation to making]]). the greek craftsman imposes, oversteps, does violence to material and then restores fittingness. the chinese butcher doesn't overstep anything. he finds the path that was always there. the skill is not in the cutting — the skill is in the seeing. in knowing where the void is.

a bad butcher fights the ox. a good butcher follows it. a bad technician fights the material. a good one listens to it. this is wu wei applied to craft — non-doing as the highest form of doing ([[Wu wei — governing without intervening]]).

and the well-digger story is the warning: machines give you machine hearts. machine hearts spoil what is pure and simple. and without purity and simplicity, the life of the spirit finds no rest. and without rest, the dao stops carrying you. this is not luddism — it is a cosmotechnical diagnosis. the machine doesn't just change what you do. it changes what you are. it restructures the heart. mara's margin note: *the principle of being, but also the freedom to be.* — pao ding's knife is free precisely because it follows the dao. freedom here is not freedom FROM constraint (the western version, the prison-escape version). freedom is freedom TO follow the natural structure. to be what you are. to cut where the void is. zi ran — the self-so ([[Zi ran and Dao — the self-so and the unconditioned]]).
