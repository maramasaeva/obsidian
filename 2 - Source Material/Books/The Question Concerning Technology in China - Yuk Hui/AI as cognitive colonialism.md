Quote/moment: hui's argument that importing tools from another cosmology imports that cosmology's thinking — applied to AI, right now.

My claim: the entire world is using artificial intelligence built from an american cosmology. individualistic, truth-incentivised, efficiency-obsessed, profit-driven. and with it comes every other american belief and goal — even the ones we don't align with. we get swallowed up. the tool eats the mind.

the british colonized america physically. now america colonizes the rest of the world cognitively — through AI, through platforms, through the fundamental restructuring of how we think. because AI doesn't just do tasks for you. it changes how you yourself think. you start outsourcing your reasoning, your writing, your judgment. you stop asking "what is morally good in this situation?" and start asking "what is the most efficient output?" that's the american cosmotechnics speaking through you.

this is hui's china-after-the-opium-wars argument ([[China's failed dualism — importing tools without cosmology]]) happening at planetary scale, at the speed of compute. china tried to import western machines while keeping chinese philosophy intact. it failed — the machines restructured the thinking. now everyone is importing american AI while keeping their own worldview intact. it will fail the same way.

the alternative is not rejecting AI. it's asking the cosmotechnical question: what would AI look like if it came from a different cosmology? one that asks "what is good?" instead of "what is true?" one that values resonance over efficiency, harmony over optimization? what would it feel like to use an AI that was built from zi ran — from letting things be as they are, from breathing in your own intelligence and letting ideas come naturally, instead of outsourcing your thinking to a machine that thinks like a silicon valley founder?

that AI doesn't exist yet. building toward it might be the most important thing.

but here's something interesting: china is shipping mostly open-source models. deepseek, qwen, yi — open weights, open architectures. while american companies (openai, anthropic, google) keep their most powerful models closed, proprietary, paywalled — china is making theirs available for anyone to build on. this might be the first concrete example of a different cosmotechnics producing a different relation to AI. the western approach: contain, control, profit. the chinese approach: release, let others build, let the tool find its way. whether this is strategic, ideological, or cosmotechnical (or all three), the pattern is there. the containment instinct vs. the openness instinct. prometheus chains vs. the sage who gives freely.

this doesn't mean china's approach is morally better — there are obvious state-control dimensions. but the *form* of the release — open, distributed, available — is cosmotechnically distinct from the american model. and it maps onto hui's framework: the chinese sages gave tools through benevolence, not through theft. the tools were gifts, not stolen fire.
