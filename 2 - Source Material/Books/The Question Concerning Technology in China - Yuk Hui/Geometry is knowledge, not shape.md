Quote/moment: *"Geometry is knowledge of space, and space is a form of intuition. Thinking of space as such an a priori form supposes this original projection... this projection is an exteriorization not only in that it allows a projection for intuition, but also a small mark that constitutes a retentional space, that is to say a support of memory."* (p. 214)

*"The ideality of geometry demands a schematisation qua exteriorization as 'writing.'"* (p. 214)

My claim: geometry is not the shape. geometry is the KNOWLEDGE of the shape. the shape in the sand is not geometry — it's the support, the memory-technology, the mark that allows geometry to exist as transmittable knowledge. same as writing: writing is not information. writing MEMORIZES information. the medium is the memory-technology, not the content.

stiegler reads the meno like this: socrates makes a slave draw a geometric proof in the sand. the slave "remembers" what he never learned — but only because the marks on the sand allow him to trace the proof. without the mark, no memory. without the exteriorization, no knowledge. technē makes knowledge possible because it makes memory possible.

this connects to **epiphylogenetic memory**: "a past that i never lived but that is nevertheless my past, without which i would never have had a past of my own" (p. 216). this memory lives in tools, languages, goods, ritual practices. it IS technics. the tool carries the memory of everyone who made it before u.

and china didn't develop this kind of memory-technology — not because it lacked memory, but because its memory worked differently. not through geometric marks in sand but through seasonal return, ritual repetition, resonance. different technics → different time → different memory → different knowledge. the absence of geometry in china is not a gap. it's a different architecture of knowing.
