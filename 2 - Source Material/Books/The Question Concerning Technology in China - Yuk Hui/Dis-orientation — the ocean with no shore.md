Quote/moment: *"a general loss of direction; one finds oneself in the middle of the ocean without being able to see either point of departure or destination"* (p. 195, echoing nietzsche)

*"Technological reason is expanding to the extent that it is becoming the condition of all conditions, the principle of all principles."* (p. 196)

My claim: dis-orientation is what happens when technological convergence erases both ends. the orient is negated — it ceases to be the orient. but then the occident also loses the orient, and therefore loses its own position (since "west" only makes sense relative to "east"). a homogeneity of nowhere.

accelerationism tries to escape this by appealing to universalism — decouple from colonialist imposition of culture. but it draws its universalism from a promethean conception of technology whose cultural specificity it never questions. the category of technics itself harbours only one destiny. so accelerationism doesn't escape — it accelerates the dis-orientation.

the result: technological reason as the ONLY reason. the principle of all principles. no outside. no shore. this is what ellul predicted: totality through technical systems. and it's what hui's entire book has been building toward — the need to create multiple cosmotechnics, to break the monopoly of one technical destiny, to find new shores.
