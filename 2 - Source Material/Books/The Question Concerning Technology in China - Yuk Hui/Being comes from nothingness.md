Quote/moment: *"in Chinese metaphysics there is virtually no problem in recognizing that being comes from nothingness, as is already stated in the Daoist classics. In Europe, ex nihilo creation is the reserve of a divine power, since it is scientifically impossible: ex nihilo nihil fit. It was not until Leibniz posed the question 'why is there something instead of nothing?' that the question of Being would be further clarified in western philosophy."* (pp. 54–55)

*"in more general terms, Chinese thinking tends to be concerned more with continuity and less with discontinuity. And this continuity is constructed by relations."* (p. 55)

My claim: in western science, we all agree that you cannot create something out of nothing. it is not scientifically possible. so we don't believe in nothingness itself — because if nothing existed, then we would not be here. the west starts from something and works outward. leibniz's question ("why is there something rather than nothing?") treats nothingness as the scandal, the problem, the thing that needs explaining away.

chinese philosophy inverts this. nothingness is not a problem. it is the ground. being comes from it naturally, without paradox, without divine intervention. the framework allows for something to come into existence *and* for nothingness to exist in its nothingness. both are part of the continuity. there is no rupture between them.

this is the deepest connection to [[zeroes and ones]] — the zero is not the absence of the one. the zero is the ground from which the one emerges. and the relation between them is not opposition but continuity. the western binary (0 vs 1, nothing vs something, false vs true) is a discontinuous dualism. the chinese framework offers a continuous one — being and nothingness weave into each other, the way yin and yang do.

but also: don't picture the zero and the one side by side. picture the zero on the ground and the one higher up. the zero is the field from which the one rises. in landian philosophy and in [[THE ZERO - manuscript]], this paradox is load-bearing — zero and one are contradicting terms, yet one emerges from zero. in the daoist frame that's not a paradox at all. *"dao engenders one, one engenders two, two engenders three, three engenders the ten thousand beings."* (p. 66) — the zero (dao) is not opposed to the one. it is the ground the one stands on. and the generative chain doesn't stop — it spirals outward into multiplicity.

and the continuity is constructed by *relations*, not by substances. this loops back to [[Relational thinking dissolves substance]] — the web of relations is what maintains the continuity between being and nothingness. without the web, you get the western rupture: something OR nothing, never both.
