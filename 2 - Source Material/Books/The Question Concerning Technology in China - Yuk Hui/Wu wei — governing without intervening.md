Quote/moment: *"the Daoist principle of governance is wu wei zhi zhi (無為之治), which means governing without intervening. This is no pessimism or passivism, but rather a letting things be, leaving room for things to grow on their own, in the hope that beings will fully realise themselves and their potential."* (p. 101)

*"Dao does not mean the 'laws of nature' as this term was understood in seventeenth-century Europe; it is rather the ungraspable that could yet be known."* (p. 100)

*"junzi bu qi (君子不器): the gentleman is not a utensil."* (p. 100, from the analects of confucius)

My claim: wu wei is probably untranslatable. governing without intervening. but "without intervening" is not passivity — it is trust. trust that beings will realise themselves. trust that the cosmos has its own direction and your job is not to impose but to make room.

this is the opposite of western governance, which is always intervention. the western state acts, regulates, enforces, produces. productivity is governance ([[Productivity is not excellence]]). wu wei says: the best governance is the one you can't see. the ruler who governs by wu wei is like water — it finds its own level, fills every gap, nourishes everything, and never forces.

"the gentleman is not a utensil" — junzi bu qi. the ideal person is not a tool. not specialised, not instrumentalised, not reduced to function. a utensil has one purpose. a gentleman has none — or rather, has all. unbounded generosity (p. 100). this is the direct opposite of what capitalism wants from people: be useful, be specialised, be a tool.

and dao is "the ungraspable that could yet be known." not unknowable — knowable, but ungraspable. you can know it without holding it. you can follow it without controlling it. this is what makes it untranslatable into western categories, because western knowledge IS grasping — comprehension literally means seizing, grasping together. to know without grasping is not a concept the west has a word for.

mara's margin note: *measuring without intervening. probably untranslatable.* — yes. and the untranslatability IS the point. it's not a failure of translation. it's a sign that you've hit the boundary between two cosmotechnics ([[Asymmetry as method — Qi and Dao not techné and physis]]).
