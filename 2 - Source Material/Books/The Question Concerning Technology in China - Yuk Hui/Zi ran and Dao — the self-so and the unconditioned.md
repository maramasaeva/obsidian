Quote/moment: *"Zi ran (自然), which is often translated as 'nature', in modern Chinese the term refers to 'the environment', to the wild animals, plants, rivers, etc. that are already given; but it also means acting and behaving according to the self without pretention, or letting things be as they are. This 'self', however, is not a robust root, but emerges out of, and is nourished and constrained by a certain cosmic order, namely Dao."* (p. 64)

*"Zi ran is the law of Dao (道法自然)."* (p. 64)

*"Dao is not a particular object, nor is it the principle of a specific genre of objects; it is present in every being, yet escapes all objectification. Dao is das Unbedingte, the 'unconditioned'."* (p. 68)

the unification of Dao and Qi (道器合一): *"in Chinese philosophy Dao stands for the supreme order of beings; and technique must be compatible with Dao in order to attain its highest standard."* (p. 65)

and zhuangzi's dialogue (p. 67): master dongguo asks where the dao exists. zhuangzi answers: in the ant. in the panic grass. in the tiles and shards. in the piss and shit. master dongguo makes no reply.

My claim: zi ran is not "nature" in the western sense (natura = the born, the given, the thing-out-there). zi ran is "the self-so" — things being what they are, without pretention, without forcing. and this self-so is not rootless or arbitrary — it emerges from and is nourished by dao.

this is why chinese technics can't be about mastering nature. there is no "nature" to master — there is only zi ran, and zi ran includes you. you are already part of the self-so. technics in this framework is about acting in accordance with what you already are, not about imposing form on resistant material.

*"furthermore, dao is not a particular object, nor is it the principle of a specific genre of objects; it is present in every being, yet escapes all objectification."* (p. 68) — and: *"dao as unconditioned that founds the conditioned perfection of all beings, including technical objects."* (p. 69) — even the most inferior and undesirable objects. to live is to maintain a subtle and complicit relation with dao, even without fully knowing it.

zhuangzi's point is devastating: if you're looking for dao in exalted places, you've already missed it. it's in the shit. the unconditioned is in the lowest, most overlooked, most abject things. this is the opposite of the promethean gesture, which reaches up to steal fire from the gods. dao doesn't need stealing. it's already here.

and dao and qi cannot be separated as if they were two entities (p. 67). they exist only in relation with each other — the way everything in chinese thinking exists only in relation. the tool and the way are not two things. they are one continuous reality seen from two angles.

*"dao stands for the supreme order of beings; and technique must be compatible with dao in order to attain its highest standard. accordingly, this highest standard is expressed as the unification of dao and qi."* (p. 65)
