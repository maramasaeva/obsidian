Quote/moment: *"According to Kant, intellectual intuition belongs only to God but not to humans. I think this is really astonishing. I reflect on Chinese philosophy, and if one follows the thought of Kant, I think that Confucianism, Buddhism and Daoism all confirm that humans have intellectual intuition; otherwise it wouldn't be possible to become a saint, Buddha, or Zhenren."* (p. 174, mou zongsan)

My claim: mou zongsan's reading of kant is one of the most radical moves in twentieth-century philosophy. kant says: humans can only know phenomena (appearances). to know noumena (things-in-themselves) directly, u'd need intellectual intuition — and only god has that. mou says: wait. chinese philosophy has been cultivating intellectual intuition for thousands of years. sagehood, buddhahood, realized being — all require direct access to reality unmediated by the senses. if chinese philosophy works, then kant is wrong about human limits.

this is not an academic disagreement. it restructures the entire map. western philosophy assumes a wall between appearance and reality — humans stuck on the phenomena side, peering through the bars. chinese philosophy assumes a door. and then says: here are the practices for opening it.

the concept that carries this is **cheng ming** (誠明) — sincerity and intelligence. knowing through moral intuition, not through hearing and seeing. this is what makes chinese philosophy a MORAL metaphysics rather than a metaphysics of nature. the west asks: what IS the world? china asks: what SHOULD we be? — and then says the answer to the second question IS access to the first.

the cost: when intellectual intuition dominates thinking, the analytical mind (logic, mathematics, science) gets suppressed. not destroyed — suppressed. china traded analytical science for moral intuition. the west traded moral intuition for analytical science. neither has both.
