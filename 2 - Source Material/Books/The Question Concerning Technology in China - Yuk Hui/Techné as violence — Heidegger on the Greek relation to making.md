Quote/moment: *"Heidegger characterises such necessary violence as the metaphysical meaning of techné, and of the Greek conception of the human as technical being."* (p. 71)

*"the human is deinotaton, the uncanniest of the uncanny (das Unheimlichste des Unheimlichen)"* (p. 72) — in the first chorus of sophocles' antigone, per heidegger's reading.

*"the uncanny is said in two senses: it is firstly said of the violence (Gewalttätigkeit), the act of violence (Gewalt-tätigkeit), in which the essence of human being as techné consists: human beings are Daseins that overstep the limits; in so doing, the Dasein of the human being finds itself no longer at home, it becomes unheimlich."* (p. 72)

and the greek contrast: *"for the ancient Greeks, technics imitates nature and at the same time perfects it. Techné mediates between physis and tyché (chance or coincidence). This idea that technics might supplement and 'perfect' nature could not possibly occur in Chinese thought, since for the latter technics is always subordinate to the cosmological order: to be part of nature is to be morally good."* (p. 70)

the resolution through dikē (justice/fittingness): *"the Greek relation to technics emerges from a cosmology, and knowledge of technics is a response to the cosmos, an attempt to 'fit' or to strive for 'fittingness'."* (p. 77)

My claim: in the greek cosmotechnics, making things is an act of violence — you overstep limits, you leave home, you impose form on material that resists. this is not evil; it's the human condition. we are the uncanny beings who make things, and making things means violating the given order and then trying to restore fittingness (dikē) afterward.

this is the deep structure underneath prometheanism: the theft of fire is violence, and the punishment (chained to a rock, liver eaten daily) is the price of fittingness. the cycle of violation and restoration IS greek cosmotechnics.

chinese cosmotechnics has no equivalent structure because it doesn't start from violence. techné as violence requires a split between human and cosmos that the chinese framework never makes. if you are already part of zi ran, there is nothing to violate. technics is subordinate to the moral-cosmological order, not an assault on it.

this has direct implications for how we understand AI. AI development in the western frame is implicitly violent — it oversteps limits, it makes the human uncanny, it disrupts the given. the question is whether we can find fittingness (dikē) afterward. in a chinese cosmotechnical frame, the question would be different: is this technology in harmony with dao? if not, it should not exist — not because it's dangerous, but because it's morally incoherent.
