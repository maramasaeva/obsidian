Quote/moment: *"bearing in mind that the circle is the most perfect geometrical form. The agora, like the earth that lies in the middle (meson), brings about a geometrical imaginary of power; a power that does not belong to any single being, such as Zeus, but to all."* (p. 79)

and: *"geometry was foundational for both technics and justice."* (p. 79)

*"the Greek relation to technics emerges from a cosmology, and knowledge of technics is a response to the cosmos, an attempt to 'fit' or to strive for 'fittingness', or perhaps harmony."* (p. 77)

fittingness (dikē) means something can be fitted into the divine order — and that order is a geometrical projection. the circle. the agora at the center of the greek city, round, open, where power belongs to no single being but to all.

My claim: the circle is the shape where geometry, cosmology, justice, and technics meet. the greeks built their city around it. the chinese find dao in it. the zero IS it.

the agora's circular power — belonging to no single being but to all — is structurally the same as dao-power: present in everything, belonging to nothing in particular, escaping objectification. the greeks arrived at it through geometry. the chinese arrived at it through cosmology. both cosmotechnics converge on the circle.

and the circle is the zero. and the spiral is the circle in time. [[Spyrodynamism]] — the spiral as the shape of becoming, the shape that moves without arriving, the shape that returns without repeating. the zero on the ground, the one rising from it, the spiral connecting them.

geometry being foundational for both technics and justice means: the way you make things and the way you organize fairness come from the same source — your understanding of the shape of the cosmos. if your cosmos is circular, your justice is distributed (the agora). if your cosmos is linear-progressive, your justice is hierarchical (modernity). the shape of the cosmos shapes the shape of the city shapes the shape of the tool.
