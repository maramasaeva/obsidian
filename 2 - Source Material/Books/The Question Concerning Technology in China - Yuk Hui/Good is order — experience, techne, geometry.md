Quote/moment: *"experience causes our time to march along the way of technē, whereas inexperience causes it to march along the way of tychē."* (p. 91, from Plato's *Gorgias*)

*"partnership and friendship, orderliness, self-control, and justice hold together heaven and earth, and gods and men, and that is why they call this universe a world order [...] You have failed to notice that proportionate equality has great power among both gods and men, and you suppose that you ought to practice getting the greater share. That is because you neglect geometry."* (p. 91, from *Gorgias*)

My claim: good is order. that's what the greeks believed. the list of virtues in gorgias — partnership, friendship, orderliness, self-control — these are all forms of arrangement. forms of things being in their right place. and this is why geometry matters: geometry is the science of arrangement. the greeks literally believed that neglecting geometry was a moral failure, because geometry IS the structure of the cosmos, and the cosmos IS moral.

experience vs. inexperience: when you have experience, you walk the path of technē — skill, craft, knowledge of how things work. when you don't, you're thrown to tychē — chance, luck, randomness. the entire project of greek technics is to replace tychē with technē. to eliminate randomness through knowledge. to bring order where there is chaos.

this is the deepest link between geometry, justice, and technics. all three are war against disorder. geometry orders space. justice orders society. technics orders material. and the cosmos — the world order — is what they're all trying to fit into.

mara's margin note: *experience of what?* — the question is real. experience of the cosmos? of the material? of oneself? for the greeks these might not be separate. experience of making is experience of the cosmic order because making is responding to that order ([[Techné as violence — Heidegger on the Greek relation to making]]). but for the chinese, the equivalent would be: experience of resonance. not of ordering, but of harmonizing.
