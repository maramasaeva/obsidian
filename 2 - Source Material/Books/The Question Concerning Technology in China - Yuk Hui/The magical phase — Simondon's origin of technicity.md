Quote/moment: *"According to Simondon, the genesis of technicity begins with a 'magical' phase, in which we find an original unity anterior to the subject/object division. This phase is characterised by the separation and cohesion between ground and figure."* (p. 20)

*"The primitive magical moment, the original mode of cosmotechnics, is bifurcated into technics and religions, in which the latter retain an equilibrium in the continued effort to obtain unity."* (p. 20)

and from gestalt psychology: in religion, the ground becomes unlimited, godlike power. in technics, the figure overtakes the ground. simondon's project is to reunify them — bring figure and ground back together through philosophical thinking.

My claim: before the split, making a tool and performing a ritual were the same act. the cosmos and the craft were one. then religion kept the ground (the cosmos, the unlimited, the divine) and technics kept the figure (the object, the tool, the specific). they separated.

this is the deep history underneath [[Demythologisation is always remythologisation]]. the bifurcation is the original demythologisation — and everything since has been an attempt to remythologise, to get back to the unity. cosmotechnics is the name for that reunion.

what the rationalist scene calls "technology" is figure without ground — tools stripped of cosmos. what indigenous and non-western traditions preserve is the ground that the figure keeps trying to leave behind. neither is complete alone.
