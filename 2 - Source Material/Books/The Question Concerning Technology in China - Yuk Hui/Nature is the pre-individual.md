Quote/moment: *"naturalism in this sense is only a product of modernity. it is 'fragile' and 'lacking in ancient roots'."* (p. 51)

montebello mobilises simondon to show that *"nature is the 'pre-individual', and that it is therefore the foundation of all forms of individuation."* and: *"nature is not the opposite of man, but the first phase of being."* (p. 51)

My claim: there is a nature we can return to, and it is not the opposite of technology. it is where everything springs forth — the ground from which individuation (becoming a distinct being, a distinct object, a distinct tool) emerges. nature in this sense is not "the environment" or "the wilderness" or "the thing we're destroying." it is the pre-individual, the undifferentiated, the field before the figure.

this connects to [[tao]] — dao is present everywhere but cannot be objectified. it is not a subject, not an object, not a thing you can point at. it is the ground of all things, including nothingness. nature-as-pre-individual and dao-as-unconditioned are pointing at the same structure from different directions.

and if nature is not the opposite of technology but its ground, then the question changes entirely. it's not "nature vs technology" (the environmentalist frame) or "technology over nature" (the promethean frame). it's: technology emerges from the pre-individual the same way everything else does. the question is whether it remembers where it came from.
