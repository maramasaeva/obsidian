Quote/moment: *the spirit of the ancient cosmotechnics is fading away, and what proves incompatible with the modern is consigned to the harmless category of 'tradition,' set apart from the forces of development.* (p. 164)

*the Chinese culture becomes purely "functional aesthetics" in Li Gushen's sense, meaning that it serves only to add an aesthetic dimension to the main driving forces of development, which will henceforth be the Occidental: sciences, dance and technology, literature and cosmopolitanism.* (p. 163)

My claim: functional aesthetics is the final stage of cosmotechnical collapse. once the dao has been separated from qi, the culture doesn't disappear — it gets demoted to decoration. the symbols stay but the system of meaning behind them evaporates. what's left is surface without depth. ornament without ontology.

mara's observation: this is exactly what's happening now. "in my chinese era." milady community using chinese characters as aesthetic. chinese captions as vibe. the symbols carry more information density and more nuanced meaning in shorter forms than latin alphabets — but we don't engage with that. we borrow the aesthetic without the cosmotechnics. it's the self-strengthening movement in reverse: instead of importing western tools without western cosmology, we import chinese SYMBOLS without chinese cosmology. same pattern. different direction. same loss.

technology merges with the question of science and becomes invisible (p. 164). scholars still confine themselves to discourse on science and democracy. they can't see the deeper question — the question of technology — because the tool has already eaten the cosmos so completely that no one remembers there was a cosmos to eat.
