Quote/moment: *the fact that they were all parts in a hierarchy of wholes forming a cosmic and organic pattern and what they obeyed were the internal dictates of their own natures.* (p. 172, needham)

*"[W]ith their appreciation of relativism and the subtlety and immensity of the universe, they were groping after an Einsteinian world-picture without having laid the foundations for a Newtonian one. By that path science could not develop."* (p. 172, needham)

My claim: the deepest difference between european and chinese cosmotechnics is the concept of LAW. in europe, the cosmos is governed by a lawgiver — god writes laws, nature obeys them. this juridical structure (stoic → roman → christian) produces science: u discover the laws, u predict behavior, u manipulate. in china, there is no cosmic legislator. things obey the internal dictates of their own natures within a pattern of organic wholes. no one gives the orders. the order emerges.

this is [[rhizomatic thinking]] before deleuze & guattari named it. the chinese organic cosmos is a network, not a tree. no root, no trunk, no hierarchy of command — just nodes following their own nature while forming a larger pattern. the european cosmos is arborescent: god → natural law → moral law → positive law, branching downward. the chinese cosmos is rhizomatic: every being IS its own law, connected to every other being through resonance ([[Ganying — resonance as Chinese cosmotechnics]]), not through obedience.

needham's insight is devastating: china was already thinking at the einstein level — relativism, organic complexity, context-dependence — but u cant get to einstein without first building newton. the mechanical reduction is a necessary stage. china tried to skip it. the organic mode of thought is more advanced but couldn't produce modern science because modern science needs the mechanical as a foundation.

what needham understood in a "whiteheadian organismic sense" by the neo-confucian school is — hui says — precisely what we describe as chinese cosmotechnics (p. 172). needham saw the thing. he just didn't have the word.
