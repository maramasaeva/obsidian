Quote/moment: *"for the Japanese philosopher, technology opens a path toward nihility. time loses all meaning."* (p. 41, on nishitani)

and p. 43: *"is it possible to understand techniques as a medium for the two ontologies? Can absolute nothingness appropriate modernity and hence construct a new world history that is not limited by western modernity?"*

the two ontologies: phenomenal (the world as it appears — being, presence, what we can see) and noumenal (the world as it is in itself — nothingness, emptiness, what underlies). kant says we can never access the noumenal. buddhism says we can — through practice, through emptiness (śūnyatā).

My claim: nishitani's move is counterintuitive and powerful. technology doesn't just make life easier — it strips away the illusions of meaning that pre-modern life provided (religion, tradition, natural cycles). what's left is nihility — nothingness. but for nishitani, drawing on buddhism, this nothingness is not negative. it's the ground of all being. so technology's destructive force (killing meaning, collapsing time) can paradoxically open a door to a deeper truth.

this is a completely different framing from the western doomer position. western AI safety says: technology is dangerous because it might destroy meaning/values/humanity. nishitani says: technology destroys meaning, and that destruction can be the gateway to something deeper — IF you have a cosmology that knows what to do with nothingness. the west doesn't. buddhism and daoism do.

the question "can absolute nothingness appropriate modernity?" is asking: can emptiness swallow the western machine and transform it from inside, rather than being consumed by it? can the zero eat the one? ([[zeroes and ones]])
