Quote/moment: *"no matter whether it is regarded as a process of making or a form of praxis, technē is considered as an assurance, as a means toward excellence and virtue, as the word aretē signifies."* (p. 95, on aristotle)

*"'making' [poiēton] and 'acting' [praktikon] are different [...] hence too they are not included in each other."* (p. 95, aristotle cited by hui)

mara's margin note: *productivity with whatever result is excellence.*

My claim: the greeks distinguished making (poiēsis) from acting (praxis). making produces an object — a table, a house, a poem. acting produces nothing external — it is its own end. virtue, friendship, thinking — these are praxis. they don't leave a product behind.

but western capitalism collapsed this distinction. now everything is making. everything must produce. and the measure of excellence became: did you produce something? is there an output? a deliverable? a result? productivity with whatever result is excellence — this is the modern equation that the greeks would have found insane.

because for aristotle, technē aims at aretē — excellence, virtue. the product is not the point. the excellence is the point. a bad doctor who produces many prescriptions is not better than a good doctor who produces few. the carpenter's aretē is not in the number of tables but in the quality of craft. excellence is the relation between maker and material, not the output count.

mara's critique: our current society measures productivity as excellence regardless of what is produced. the tool that produces most is the best tool. the worker who outputs most is the best worker. AI is the ultimate expression of this — infinite production, zero praxis. what does it mean to produce without acting? to make without being? that is a cosmotechnical question silicon valley never asked.
