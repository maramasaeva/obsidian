Quote/moment: descola maps four ontologies based on whether interiority and physicality are seen as similar or dissimilar between humans and non-humans (pp. 24–25):

| | similar interiority | dissimilar interiority |
|---|---|---|
| **similar physicality** | totemism | naturalism |
| **dissimilar physicality** | animism | analogism |

- **naturalism** (western default): shared bodies, different minds. the nature/culture split.
- **animism**: shared souls/spirits, different bodies. the amazonian/indigenous frame.
- **totemism**: shared in both. total kinship.
- **analogism**: different in both, but connected through correspondences. chinese cosmology lives here.

and ingold's cree hunter: *"at that crucial moment of eye-to-eye contact, the hunter felt the overwhelming presence of the animal; he felt as if his own being were somehow bound up or intermingled with that of the animal — a feeling tantamount to love."* (p. 25)

My claim: naturalism is not the truth about nature. it is one ontology among four, and it happens to be the one that enables industrial capitalism, promethean technics, and the exploitation of everything-that-is-not-human as "resource." the other three ontologies don't produce that outcome because they don't start from the same split.

the table is a map of [[mode collapse]] in reverse — it shows what multiplicity looks like before everything gets flattened into naturalism. every time we assume "nature" means what western naturalism says it means, we're collapsing four possible worlds into one.

the cree hunter feeling love from the animal is not naive — it's a functioning cosmotechnics where the technical act (hunting) and the cosmic order (reciprocity with beings) are unified. ingold calls it "sentient ecology." it's what technology looks like when figure and ground haven't been separated.
