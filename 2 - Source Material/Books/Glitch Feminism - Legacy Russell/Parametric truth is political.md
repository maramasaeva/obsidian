*"A lot of these processes and workflows demand content that is very specific to their definition of the human form in order to function. As a result, they don't account for anything that diverges from that norm, establishing a parametric truth that is biased and discriminatory. This raises the question of what that norm is and how, by whom and for whom it has been defined." — Simone C. Niquille* (p. 137, ch. 11, Glitch Is Remix)

niquille built 3D body models using Fuse (adobe) and found the software has embedded assumptions about what a body should look like. bodies that don't match can't be rendered as bodies — they literally fail to qualify. this is the AI bias argument made material: the norms baked into technical systems are not neutral parameters, they are political decisions made by specific people in specific contexts.

when the program is pushed to its limits, it fails to recognize certain corpo-realities, "establishing that bodies that do not blend seamlessly cannot qualify as a body at all." this is what happens when AI systems classify gender — the training data *is* the political decision, and transparency frameworks that don't address the categories themselves are just making the bias more efficient.
