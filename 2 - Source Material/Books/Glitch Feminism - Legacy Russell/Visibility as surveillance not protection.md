*"The whole concept of visibility assumes that you're not in a system that wants you dead. I think a lot of people forget that many of the places we are inserted in want to kill us … We're not supposed to be there." — Sondra Perry* (ch. 06, Glitch Encrypts)

*"Readability of bodies only according to standard social and cultural coding (e.g., to be white, to be cisgender, to be straight) renders glitched bodies invisible, extends safety, keeps bodies un-surveilled. Glitched bodies pose a very real threat to social order: encrypted and unreadable within a strictly gendered worldview, they resist normative programming."* (p. 85)

the whole "trustworthy AI" transparency push assumes that being seen by the system is a good thing. but for bodies the system was never built to hold, legibility is not safety — it's targeting. regulation that demands more transparency can just mean more efficient surveillance of the people it claims to protect. the question is never "can the system see you" but "what does the system do when it sees you."

russell calls gender a "hyperobject" (via timothy morton) — so big it becomes invisible, so normal it becomes violent in its normalcy. the "normative ordinary" is how control hides in plain sight.
