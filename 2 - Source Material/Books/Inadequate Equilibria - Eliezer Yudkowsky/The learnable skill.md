Quote/moment: *"When I try to explain a phenomenon, I'm also implicitly relying on my ability to use a technique like 'don't even start to rationalize,' which is a skill that I started practicing at age 15 and that took me a decade to hone to a reliable and productive form."* (p. 126)

also: *"But at the same time, it seems to me that there is a learnable skill here, one that entrepreneurs and venture capitalists at least have to learn if they want to succeed on purpose instead of by luck."* (p. 126)

also: cognitive techniques listed: "detecting goodness of fit" (does the explanation feel forced?), "try further critiquing the answer," "notice when you're confused about something" — all skills, not talents.

My claim: the key word is *learnable*. yudkowsky isn't saying "i'm smarter." he's saying "i practiced this specific technique for a decade." that reframes the entire rationalist project — it's not about being a certain kind of person, it's about building specific cognitive tools over time.

this connects to the epistemological clarity video ([[Epistemological clarity]]): "try to make sure you'd arrive at different beliefs in different worlds." that's the test. it's not a personality trait. it's a checkable condition. you either would or wouldn't arrive at different beliefs, and you can practice until you would.

in the age of LLMs this is the skill that atrophies first. if the model gives you a plausible-sounding explanation, you skip the step where you check whether the explanation feels forced. the "detecting goodness of fit" skill never gets practiced. the model provides the fit. you accept it.
