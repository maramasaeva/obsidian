Quote/moment: *"To win, choose winnable battles; await the rare anomalous case of, 'Oh wait, that could work.'"* (p. 111)

also: *"most of the time systems end up dumber than the people in them due to multiple layers of terrible incentives, and that this is normal and not at all a surprising state of affairs."* (p. 106)

also: *"if you haven't practiced thinking about organizations' comparative advantages from that perspective long enough to make that lens more cognitively available than the status comparisons lens, then it makes sense that all talk of relative performance levels between you and doctors, or you and academia, or whatever, will be autoparsed by the easier, more native, more automatic status lens."* (p. 106)

also: *"you do not generate a good startup idea by taking some random activity, and then talking yourself into believing you can do it better than existing companies. Even where the current way of doing things seems bad, and even when you really do know a better way, 99 times out of 100 you will not be able to make money by knowing better."* (p. 110)

My claim: this is the framework for why projects that fill real gaps don't take off. the gap being real is necessary but not sufficient. yudkowsky identifies three layers:

1. **the equilibrium has to be genuinely inadequate** — not just suboptimal but stuck in a nash equilibrium where no individual actor can profitably deviate. most systems are inadequate. this is the easy part to see.

2. **the inadequacy has to be exploitable by you specifically** — your particular skills, position, knowledge, resources have to give you comparative advantage in *this specific case*. not "i'm smart and this is broken" but "the specific thing that makes this exploitable is something i happen to have."

3. **the status lens has to not kill it** — even if 1 and 2 are true, when you try to explain why your approach is different, people's default cognitive machinery translates it into a status claim. "my nonprofit has different incentives" → "she thinks she's better than academia." the structural argument can't land because the status parser intercepts it.

applying this to the projects in the vault:

- **kaios** (music AI): the gap is real (AI-generated music is mostly soulless), mara has genuine comparative advantage (musician + engineer + theorist), but the status lens problem is brutal — the music industry parses "AI music" as either threat or gimmick, not as a new instrument.
- **think workshop**: the gap is real (kids are being destroyed by platforms), mara has advantage (young, trans, technical, speaks their language), and the status lens is *actually favorable* here — being young and non-institutional is an asset with teenagers. this might be the winnable battle.
- **the book / substack**: the gap is real (queer theory and AI theory don't talk to each other), mara has advantage (lives in both worlds), but the audience for this specific synthesis is small and scattered. the question is whether urbanomic is the right vehicle — it sidesteps the status lens by operating in a space that already values correct contrarianism.
- **the website / 3d void**: this is play, not a battle. and that's fine. yudkowsky's framework doesn't apply to art-for-its-own-sake. but it *does* apply if mara wants it to be a vehicle for something.

the key yudkowsky move: don't chase every broken thing. scan and keep scanning. wait for the anomalous case where all three layers align. the think workshop might be that case — the gap is real, the advantage is genuine, and the status lens works *for* you instead of against you.
