Quote/moment: *"modest people end up believing that they live in an inexploitable world because they're trying to avoid acting like an arrogant kind of person. Under modest epistemology, you're not supposed to adapt rapidly and without hesitation to the realities of the situation as you observe them, because that would mean trusting yourself to assess adequacy levels; but you can't trust yourself, because Dunning-Kruger, et cetera."* (p. 104)

also: *"examining particular empirical predictions of the goblin hypothesis, on the other hand, does provide strong evidence about what world you're in."* (p. 104)

also: the MIRI anecdote from p.105 — european existential risk community asks "why do you think you can do better than academia?" yudkowsky responds: we have different incentives. every time he said "the nonprofit I work at has different incentives," it got parsed as "the nonprofit I work at is *better* (higher status, more authoritative, etc.) than academia." (p. 106)

My claim: modesty is a self-reinforcing trap. if you can't trust your own judgment, you can't identify the places where the system is broken, which means the broken system looks like the best anyone can do, which confirms that modesty was the right call. the loop closes.

the goblin test cuts through this. you don't need to trust yourself in the abstract — you test specific predictions. do goblins leave tracks? do the closet doors rattle at night? if your specific predictions hold up, you're not being arrogant — you're observing. the move from "trust yourself" to "test your predictions" is what makes this non-arrogant.
