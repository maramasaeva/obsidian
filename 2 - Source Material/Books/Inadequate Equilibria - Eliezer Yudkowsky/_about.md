# Inadequate Equilibria: Where and How Civilizations Get Stuck — Eliezer Yudkowsky

published 2017, MIRI. the book asks: when is it rational to think you can do better than the existing system? when should you trust your own judgment over the establishment? and when are you just being arrogant?

the core framework: civilizations get stuck in inadequate equilibria — nash equilibria where everyone is individually incentivized to keep doing the thing that makes the collective outcome worse. these aren't bugs, they're the default. the question isn't "is the system broken" (it usually is), it's "is this a place where *i specifically* can do better, given my particular position and knowledge?"

mara is 120 pages in. the chapter that hit hardest: "Living in an Inadequate World" (pages 104-113) — on the status lens, correct contrarianism, picking winnable battles, and why most "i can fix this" thoughts are traps.

## potential threads

- **the status lens vs the competence lens** — [[exocapitalism]], [[Attention collapse and the automation of the social]]: yudkowsky says people default to interpreting all claims about comparative advantage as status claims. "my approach has different incentives" gets heard as "i'm better than you." this is exactly what happens when mara pitches projects — the system can't hear the structural argument because the status lens intercepts it first.
- **picking winnable battles** — [[value alignment]], [[Think Workshop]]: "to win, choose winnable battles; await the rare anomalous case of 'oh wait, that could work.'" don't chase every broken thing you see. scan and keep scanning. the question isn't "is X broken" but "is this the rare case where i have genuine comparative advantage?"
- **correct contrarianism vs crackpottery** — [[hyperstition]], [[Humanity spoke AGI into existence - response to Angelicism]]: "you can often know things the average authority doesn't know — not because you figured it out yourself, but because you picked it up from the right contrarian source." the vault is full of correct contrarian positions. the question is which ones are *actionable*.
- **terrible incentives as default** — [[exocapitalism]], [[Loops allow future expectations to shape present behavior.]]: "most of the time systems end up dumber than the people in them due to multiple layers of terrible incentives, and this is normal and not at all a surprising state of affairs." this is the loop problem — the system reproduces its own inadequacy.
- **the ketosoylent principle** — practical: even when you can't pick your battleground, don't try to synthesize from scratch. every element should be based on existing work. "not a single element of my homemade ketosoylent was based on my personal, private theory." applies to mara's projects: build from existing correct contrarianism, don't reinvent.
- **moloch and the nash equilibrium** — [[AGI's natural enemy]], [[exocapitalism]]: yudkowsky's framework is basically a practical manual for identifying where moloch is weak. where the equilibrium is bad *and* exploitable. most equilibria are bad but not exploitable — that's the trap.
