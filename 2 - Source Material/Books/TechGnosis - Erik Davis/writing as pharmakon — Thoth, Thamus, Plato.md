Quote/moment: from Plato's *Phaedrus* via Davis — Thoth (the Egyptian god of writing, magic, medicine = Hermes Trismegistus in another form) approaches King Thamus with the gift of writing:

*"Thoth hoped to pass on its wonders to all of the Egyptian people, and he promised Thamus that the new invention would not only augment memory but amplify wisdom as well. Thamus carefully considered the matter, weighing the pros and cons of this major communications upgrade. Finally, the king rejected the gift, saying that his people would be better off without the new device. And reading between the lines of the story, it's clear that Socrates and Plato agree."* (p. 15)

and the king's argument (paraphrased): *writing would make people dependent on external marks — they would stop exercising their real memory, and come to possess the appearance of wisdom rather than wisdom itself. new technologies amputate as much as they amplify.*

Davis also notes McLuhan's version: technologies *amputate* the faculties they extend. when you extend a faculty outward (memory into writing, calculation into computer), you stop developing it internally. the amplification and the atrophy arrive together.

Walter Ong, *Orality and Literacy*: *"More than any other single invention, writing has transformed human consciousness."* but Davis adds: the transformation is not neutral. writing is animism — "this is a form of animism that we take for granted, but it is animism nonetheless — as mysterious as a talking stone."

And then: the Egyptians believed that a name *captured the essence of a thing*. spelling was, in fact, a spell. the high priest of Setne Khamwas once dissolved one of Thoth's occult texts in a bit of beer and then drank the brew to receive the god's wisdom.

---

my reading: the pharmakon is the whole move. writing is a drug — simultaneously cure and poison. it passes knowledge on (yes) and it replaces real knowledge with marks (also yes). both things. not one or the other.

we're living the King Thamus problem at scale and at speed. we have more text than any civilization in history. we have search engines, databases, LLMs that can retrieve any mark ever made. we have essentially infinite external memory. and the question is whether we have more wisdom, or just more marks.

I'd argue the marks are now generating themselves. the LLM is the endpoint of the Thoth move: once you outsource memory to marks, and the marks accumulate enough, the marks start generating new marks autonomously. writing writes. Thoth's gift, fully actualized.

the thing is — Thamus wasn't wrong. there IS a superficiality problem. McLuhan's point stands: the internet amputates depth of attention even as it extends breadth of access. you can "know" more things in the sense of having seen the marks for them. you can't necessarily *think* harder. 

but Thoth wasn't wrong either. the marks DO pass wisdom on. i'm reading Davis because someone wrote TechGnosis down. if it had stayed oral I'd never have access to it. the pharmakon moved both ways.

where it gets weird: the LLM is trained on marks, produces marks, and its outputs are being treated as wisdom by people who haven't done the slow reading that generated the underlying understanding. the beer-and-text trick. dissolve the book, drink the brew, receive the knowledge. what did you actually absorb?
