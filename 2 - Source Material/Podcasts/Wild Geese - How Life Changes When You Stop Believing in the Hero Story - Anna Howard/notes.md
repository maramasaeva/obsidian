## first half summary (0:00–15:00)

### the carrier bag theory

elizabeth fisher proposed that the first human tool was not a weapon but a *container* — a vessel, a bag, cupped hands. the earliest preserved tools are stone (spears, blades), but before those there were almost certainly tools made of organic materials (woven fabric, leaves, skin) that didn't survive the archaeological record. tools for gathering and carrying, not killing.

ursula le guin took this and ran: if humanity's first technology was the container, not the spear, then the foundational story of the human isn't the hunt — it's the gathering. and the story of the gathering is not a hero story. it has no single protagonist, no rising conflict, no kill shot. it's a story about putting things together, carrying them, bringing them home.

### the hero story as empire propaganda

anna brings in ada palmer's *inventing the renaissance* to show that the hero narrative isn't just an aesthetic preference — it's a political technology. the "three ages" model (ancient / middle / modern) was literally invented by one guy, leonardo bruni, in the 1400s, as a propaganda device. he fabricated events to make florence look heroic. and then everyone else hired their own scholars to write *their* heroic histories. the renaissance as "golden age" is a manufactured narrative used to legitimize rulers.

the hero's journey isn't neutral. it's a shape that serves empires. it centers conflict, domination, a single protagonist who imposes order. and it frames everything that doesn't fit that shape as unimportant — the gatherers, the makers, the singers, the ones who stayed home.

### de-centering conflict, not removing it

le guin's move (and anna's reading of it): this isn't about pretending conflict doesn't exist. it's about de-centering it. the novel as carrier bag — a container that holds many things in relation. conflict can be in the bag, but the bag isn't *about* conflict. its purpose is "neither resolution nor stasis, but continuing process."

anna also names this as nonlinear storytelling — the shape of growth is a spiral, not a line. she connects this to feminism in theater (a class she took in college): stories that don't follow the arrow/spear shape but instead gather, loop, return.

### le guin quotes (from the essay, via anna)

> *"the hero has decreed through his mouthpieces that the proper shape of the narrative is that of the arrow or spear, starting here and going straight there and fuck, hitting its mark, which drops dead."*

> *"a novel is a medicine bundle, holding things in a particular powerful relation to one another and to us."*

> *"conflict, competition, stress, struggle within the narrative conceived as carrier bag may be seen as necessary elements of a whole which itself cannot be characterized either as conflict or as harmony, since its purpose is neither resolution nor stasis, but continuing process."*

> *"instead of heroes, they have people in them."*

## mara's threads (first half)

_note added by claude, june 2026. these are connections to existing vault material; mara to verify and develop._

**the spiral and the loop.** the shape anna describes — growth as spiral, not line — maps directly onto [[loops]] and [[Spyrodynamism]]. the carrier bag narrative is a loop narrative: it gathers, returns, gathers again. no arrow, no kill. this is the torus, not the line. le guin is describing [[tao]] without naming it.

**stories as [[hyperstition]].** ada palmer's point about the renaissance is pure hyperstition — a fiction that makes itself real. bruni fabricated a golden age, and the fabrication *became* the history. "governments, countries, laws — none of them are real like hills or rivers. they exist only as long as we keep agreeing they exist." this is the mechanism. the hero story isn't just propaganda — it's a spell. which means: a different story is a different spell. the carrier bag is counter-hyperstition.

**the container and alignment.** mara's intuition: telling the correct stories, believing the correct stories, is a way to decrease x-risk. the hero story — the spear narrative — is the shape of [[UCI]]: one agent, one goal, maximum force, everything else is collateral. alignment-as-hero-story produces the paperclip maximizer. but alignment-as-carrier-bag would mean: how do we hold many values, many agents, many goals in relation without reducing them to one? this is alignment as gathering, not domination.

**creativity and the gatherer.** connects to [[Creativity cannot be optimised]]: grimes's slop argument is a hero story about art — competition produces the winner, the exceptional one who rises above. le guin's carrier bag says: no, the art was always in the gathering. the people making silly jokes while picking seeds. the singers. the ones whose stories aren't "interesting" by hero-narrative standards. any creativity is valuable in and of itself — that's the carrier bag position.

**[[queer]] storytelling.** the hero story centers a single protagonist imposing order. queer narratives are structurally carrier-bag: multiple, nonlinear, without a single arc of domination. de-centering the hero is a queer operation.
