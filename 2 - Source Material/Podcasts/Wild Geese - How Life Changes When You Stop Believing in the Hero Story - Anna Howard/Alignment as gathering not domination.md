Quote/moment: *"the proper shape of the narrative is that of the arrow or spear, starting here and going straight there and fuck, hitting its mark, which drops dead."* — le guin describing what she rejects

My claim: alignment-as-hero-story is the paperclip maximiser. one agent, one goal, maximum force, everything else is externality. that's the spear narrative applied to AI. it's also how most alignment research is framed: how do we make the agent pursue the *right* single goal?

but le guin's carrier bag offers a different frame: alignment as the capacity to hold many values, many agents, many goals in relation — without collapsing them into one. the purpose is "continuing process," not resolution. this is closer to what alignment might actually need to be: not a target, but a practice of gathering. an ongoing negotiation between incommensurable goods.

this connects to mara's intuition that telling the correct stories and believing the correct stories is a mechanism for reducing x-risk. the story we tell about what alignment *is* shapes what we build. if alignment is a spear, we get optimisers. if alignment is a carrier bag, we get something that can hold contradiction without resolving it.
