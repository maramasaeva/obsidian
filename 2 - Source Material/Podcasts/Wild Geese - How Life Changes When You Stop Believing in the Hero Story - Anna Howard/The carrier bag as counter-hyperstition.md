Quote/moment: *"a novel is a medicine bundle, holding things in a particular powerful relation to one another and to us."* — ursula k. le guin, *the carrier bag theory of fiction*

My claim: the hero narrative is a hyperstition — a fiction that makes itself real. ada palmer shows this concretely: the renaissance was manufactured by historians who fabricated heroic events, and those fabrications *became* the history. the spear-shaped story (one agent, one conflict, one kill) is the narrative technology of empire. it's also the narrative technology of [[UCI]] — one optimiser, one objective function, everything else is collateral.

le guin's carrier bag is the counter-spell. a story shaped like a container, not a weapon. it doesn't center a hero — it holds many things in relation. its purpose is "neither resolution nor stasis, but continuing process." that's [[tao]]. that's the [[loops|loop]]. that's what [[queer]] storytelling already does: multiple, nonlinear, without a single arc of domination.

if telling the right stories is a way to shift reality — and hyperstition says it is — then the shape of the story matters as much as its content. the spear story produces empires. the carrier bag story might produce something else.
