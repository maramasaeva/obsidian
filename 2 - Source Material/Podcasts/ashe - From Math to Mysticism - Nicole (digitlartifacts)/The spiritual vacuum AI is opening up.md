## transcription

nicole opens by describing how she started having experiences of [[synchronicity]] as a teenager — her inner world being mirrored by outer reality. she draws a distinction between two views of [[consciousness]]: the emergent view (consciousness arises from the brain) and the older view that we are embedded within a field of consciousness. the emergent view came out of the enlightenment and enabled technological acceleration by bracketing out subjective experience — but it also bracketed out meaning.

she traces the current spiritual vacuum: major religion used to provide a collective myth. now we have a broad decentralization of myths and stories with no center line. people are turning to the occult and spirituality because they're looking for old threads to give them meaning.

she names the concept of the *anima mundi* — the world soul — the idea that the world is alive and communicating with us, that everything is imbued with meaning. this is opposed to the nihilist view that nothing is meaningful, and the postmodern destruction of objective truth.

then the AI wave: the things that gave people meaning — their work — are now dissolving. nicole is "relentlessly optimistic" that new forms of work will emerge, as happened with the personal computer. but the current moment in san francisco is one of extreme angst — oscillating between mania (when you feel the power of AI tools) and intense fear (about impact on self, community, world).

## key points

- consciousness-as-emergent is the modern default but it's historically unusual — the older view is that consciousness is fundamental, a field we're embedded in.
- the enlightenment framework that enabled tech acceleration simultaneously stripped out subjective meaning.
- we're in a decentralized myth landscape — no collective story, no center to hold on to.
- the resurgence of interest in the occult and spirituality is people looking for meaning in the vacuum.
- *anima mundi*: the world is alive and in communication with us.
- AI is dissolving work-as-meaning, but nicole is optimistic about new forms of work emerging.
- san francisco is the acute epicenter of the tech-anxiety-mania oscillation.
