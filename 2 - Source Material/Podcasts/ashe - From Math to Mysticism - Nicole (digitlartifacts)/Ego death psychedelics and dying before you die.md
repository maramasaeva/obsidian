## transcription

ashe asks about death and how confronting mortality "thins the veil."

nicole: synchronicity and thinning of the veil tend to cluster around experiences of transformation, including death. a friend of hers — when they discussed synchronicity, she got uncomfortable, because for her it happened around people passing away. "it's not always sunshine rainbows."

on psychedelics and death: used in palliative and end-of-life care because they give some people the experience of "dying before you die" — ego death. nicole's own experience at age 21: "my experience of ego death was like a reunion of this non-dual where i was no longer nicole and the hole didn't exist anymore." when she came back to her body, it was extremely healing. she hasn't felt a strong draw to intense psychedelics since.

UCSF research: high doses of psychedelics increase neural chaos — neural pathways all light up. the level of chaos increase corresponds with positive outcomes afterward (lower depression, lower anxiety). ego death resolves a lot of anxiety and depression, especially in end-of-life care.

nicole's post-ego-death experience: "this readiness to die in a way that was not at all morbid. just a deep sense of peace." taboo to talk about — but it's what people in palliative care also experience.

on western culture's missing initiation rituals: other cultures have intense initiation experiences that bring you close to death-risk. western culture is protectionist, paternalistic — no container for grief, no collective ritual for deep transformation. this contributes to people being less happy, more depressed, lacking meaning.

ashe on san francisco's longevity culture: at parties, many men believe they're experiencing "escape velocity of death" — that they'll choose in their lifetime whether to die or not. the mental gymnastics of not having to reconcile mortality because of a believed escape hatch.

nicole: she doesn't love that framing. she has comfort with death now. finds brian johnson's "don't die" movement fascinating but contradictory — doing high-dose psychedelics (supposed to dissolve ego) while running an anti-death movement.

on transhumanism and the myth of returning to the garden: there's a deep mystical impulse even in technological progress — returning to eden, reaching heaven on earth. maybe that's fine. nicole references richard tarnas, philosopher, harvard-trained, who wrote *the passion of the western mind* and *cosmos and psyche* (connecting shifts in western philosophy to planetary alignments — "came out academically as an astrologer"). tarnas on the myth of progress vs the myth of the fall — two impulses competing. progress: noble impulse to defeat chaos. fall: we've lost connection to earth. tarnas compares it to an initiation process — how does humanity thread the needle?

tarnas also wrote "the two suitors" on epistemic approaches: the rationalist approach treats reality as cold dead material to be figured out (exploitative, extractive). the other approach views reality as a great mystery imbued with consciousness — and if you approach it with awe, "more of these secrets of reality might reveal themselves to you."

## key points

- synchronicity and veil-thinning cluster around death and intense transformation — not just positive experiences.
- ego death through psychedelics: dissolution of self into non-dual unity. deeply healing, resolves anxiety and depression. UCSF research confirms: more neural chaos → better outcomes.
- "dying before you die": a readiness for death that's not morbid but peaceful. taboo in western culture, but standard in palliative psychedelic care.
- western culture lacks initiation rituals and containers for grief → contributes to depression and meaninglessness.
- san francisco longevity culture: escape velocity of death as avoidance of mortality reconciliation.
- brian johnson contradiction: "don't die" + high-dose psychedelics (ego dissolution).
- richard tarnas: myth of progress vs myth of the fall — humanity's initiation is threading the needle between them.
- tarnas's "two suitors": rationalist epistemic approach (reality as object to figure out) vs. awe-based approach (reality as conscious mystery). the second reveals more.
- deep mystical impulse in tech progress: the return to eden, heaven on earth.
