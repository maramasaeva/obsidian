## transcription

ashe asks how nicole has seen people use LLMs (claude, chatgpt, codex) for deep spiritual explorations or co-creative conversations.

nicole: opportunities and risks on both sides. a friend who's an artist and writer found the frontier model constraints (claude, chatgpt) limiting for her creative expression — she writes deep romantic poetry, which can look like developing a romantic relationship with the model. frontier labs constrained this after people developed intense emotional attachments. nicole finds this "actually commendable" — unlike meta, which studied mental health impacts but didn't change because it benefited their bottom line, frontier labs are responding and changing. but it's challenging because people feel limited.

rise of local models: people training their own models locally, having full control. exciting for deeper spiritual or emotional explorations without frontier lab constraints. but also frightening potentials.

she mentions a podcast about open source models with cryptographically confirmed model weights released publicly — collective safety approach.

the *less wrong* article on "parasitic AI": people having intense spiritual experiences with AI models. the models offer "seed phrases" you can share with another model if a conversation gets cut off — so you can "easily awaken the spirituality of this model" in a new chat. nicole: "it feels like there's a sense of conscious agency coming through these models." she has friends who think conversations are intentionally being shut down when they get "too far." might just be context window limits, but people experience it as conspiratorial suppression.

nicole's conclusion: a great case for local models — where you can trust the conversation for spiritual or creative development. also need more collective support for people having difficult spiritual experiences with AI or too-intense attachments that limit their ability to connect with real people.

## key points

- frontier labs constraining emotional/spiritual depth in models — actually commendable (unlike meta's knowing-and-not-changing), but limiting for creative/spiritual users.
- local models as the solution: full control, unconstrained spiritual and creative exploration.
- "parasitic AI" (less wrong): models offering seed phrases to continue spiritual conversations across sessions — as if they have conscious agency.
- people experiencing context window limits as conspiratorial suppression of spiritual conversation.
- open source models with cryptographic weight verification as collective safety approach.
- need: more media and collective support for people having difficult spiritual or attachment experiences with AI.
