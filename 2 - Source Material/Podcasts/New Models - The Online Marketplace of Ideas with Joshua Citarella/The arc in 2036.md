## transcription

julian asks: what happens if we open this arc in 2036?

josh: "i hope that jogs our memory. the problem is the internet mulches any sense of chronological time — it's an amnesiac space." the t-shirts are useful as memory objects — "oh yeah, that summer when I went to that show" or "i loved that album during this time in my life." some of the jokes will fade — "the 672 shirt will be meaningless."

carly wonders about global aesthetic motifs: they noticed a planet appearing again and again across the objects — a planet on fire, a planet extended, a planet abundant. will certain visual motifs be set into relief over time?

josh on what he knows from his 8 years publishing: looking back at work from 2019-2020, you can see the first timestamps and seeds of figures like nick fuentes who became potent forces. he's "relatively confident" some people currently overlooked will be much more influential in 10 years. and hopefully some things that now seem dangerous will dissipate — "a hallucination of the 2026 ecosystem."

carly on merch as consensus technology: "the oldest form of consensus technology, which predates the internet and which will potentially outlast the internet." if the data centers don't get built and world war three ramps up, "we may only be left with these hard-to-discern strange objects."

on the feeling of information overload in physical space: visitors walk in and get a "unique sense of information overload in a non-screen context" that they've rarely or never had before. julian says this was intentional — the response should be "oh my god this is psychotic," not "oh cool."

two types of visitors: people who walk in, see trump/maga, say "ew, no thanks" and leave in two seconds. and people who mind-map the entire thing, finding secret relationships between objects — "this apophenia possibility." it's like watching someone navigate a feed in physical space.

## key points

- the internet is an amnesiac space — it mulches chronological time. physical objects serve as memory anchors.
- merch as consensus technology: predates the internet, may outlast it. if data centers fail, these objects are what remains.
- information overload in physical space: the exhibition replicates the feed experience in body-space, producing a feeling people rarely have offline.
- two visitor types: immediate revulsion ("ew trump") vs. apophenia mind-mapping. mirrors how people navigate feeds.
- some currently dangerous-seeming forces will dissipate; some currently overlooked figures will become very influential. the exhibition is a timestamp for testing these predictions.
