## transcription

lil internet (julian) introduces the project: the online marketplace of ideas, created by himself, caroline busta, and joshua citarella, with spatial design by sub (julia, bill, stefano), for the *strange rules* exhibition at palazzo diedo in venice. curated by holly herndon, mat dryhurst, hans ulrich obrist, and adriana rispoli. the show's theme: practices that engage with the protocol layer — the rules that determine how culture is created, transmitted, remembered.

josh describes it: around 400+ items — the memeplex of internet culture. books, theory, a call her daddy tee, a joe rogan t-shirt, baby doll tees, tote bags, hats, all the ephemera and curio of internet culture. "this insane hot topic for the 21st century." a "deranged psycho-shopify concept store."

his favorite item: a vintage t-shirt that says "alex jones did nothing wrong" with the original infowars logo on the back, found unbought on ebay. a timestamp — while they were installing, tim heidecker became the host/creative director of infowars after the onion purchased it.

key logistical detail: they conceived the project in private, without contributors' knowledge. ~95% was purchased off existing e-commerce stores. ~5% is from new models, do not research, friends, artists — "the emerging institutional structures that we see as potent players in the landscape of internet culture." urbanomic helped with key books.

julian frames it as the unofficial american pavilion — the actual american pavilion at the biennale was a non-event (curated by a pet store owner installed by friends of trump). america's primary cultural export is now internet ideology and media, pushed through the digital stack.

## key points

- 400+ objects purchased online without creators' knowledge — a "deranged form of 21st century curation" where curation = consumption.
- the project is a physical timestamp of internet culture at the moment of de-globalization (tariffs, infowars purchased by the onion).
- julian: this is the real american pavilion — america's primary export is now internet ideology pushed through the digital stack.
- part of *strange rules*: engaging with the protocol layer — the rules that produce culture, not the culture itself.
