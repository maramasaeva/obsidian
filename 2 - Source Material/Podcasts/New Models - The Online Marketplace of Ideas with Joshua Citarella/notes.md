## the project

joshua citarella + NEW MODELS (caroline busta & lil internet) built an exhibition of 400+ items purchased online — merch, books, zines, ephemera — displayed without the knowledge of their creators. contributors range from joe rogan to chapo trap house to call her daddy to alex jones vintage tees. architectural design by sub (julia, bill, stefano). production assistant: elena zagas.

part of **Strange Rules** at palazzo diedo, curated by holly herndon, mat dryhurst, hans ulrich obrist, and adriana rispoli. the show's theme: practices that engage with the *protocol layer* — the rules that determine how culture is created, transmitted, remembered.

the thesis: alt-media has become *quantitatively larger* than legacy publications. *"we don't even have the rhetorical tools to describe what this thing means. we're still functioning in this outmoded language of counterculture as if there is some kind of mainstream and an opposition."* — citarella. the people making this stuff are an "accidental avant-garde for 21st century media."

josh calls it *"this insane hot topic for the 21st century"* — a psycho-shopify concept store. julian (lil internet) frames it as the unofficial american pavilion — america's primary cultural export now is internet ideology and media, pushed out through the digital stack.

## key concepts

**protocol-level control / market hegemony**: a young turks tote bag and a sam harris t-shirt use the same graphic motif (the "thank you" chinese takeout bag design). polar opposite positions, same visual language. and beneath that — same gildan t-shirts, same DTG printing, same hugo hats, same shopify → warehouse logistics. citarella: *"there seems to be this underlying economic megastructure... that is uniting all of these things."* when you see 400 objects together, you can start to see that *"all of these different options that espouse different editorial positions... have a limited range of influence, but there seems to be this underlying economic megastructure."* the market is the actual hegemon, not any ideology.

**merch as cultural technology**: millennials cohabitate, sublet, move around, don't have basements. *"the one thing they'll do is a t-shirt, or a book, or a bumper sticker, a pin. maybe they have a bong. like, these are the agentic material objects of millennial and younger generations."* merch is "the oldest form of consensus technology, which predates the internet and which will potentially outlast the internet." it's also memetic — limited drops function both as revenue and as content/advertising in the network.

**media-politics convergence**: the 2024 "podcast election." two key examples: gavin newsom starts his own podcast, adopts content creator + memetic strategy, now considered frontrunner for the democratic party. claudia sheinbaum in mexico hosts a daily livestream (the mañanera) — quantitatively the most watched media program in the country. citarella: *"the content creators are best at getting the message out. they're also best at fundraising and they're also best at what we call calls to action."* the structural convergence: the politician becomes the content creator becomes the institution.

**the K-shape / cambrian processes**: julian sees K everywhere — K-shape economy, K-punk era. same platforms, same merch processes (consolidation at the base), total decentralization and cambrian explosion of media entities at the top. also: uniparty — voters have no say on israel or universal healthcare (foreclosed from democratic process), but ideologically it's fully exploded. *"you could be whoever you want to be, so long as you never get sick and support israel."*

**para-institutions / pseudo political parties**: citarella's definition of institution: *"any group of people in which decisions about production... is deliberated around a table."* platforms removed that deliberation. we didn't fix the problems of institutions; we just liquidated them. now: do not research runs courses that pay better than adjunct professors. people "squad up" — joining collectives is now in your own individual interest. the pseudo political party may outperform the legacy party. example: american communist party, founded by twitch streamers, attempted to infiltrate CPUSA.

**vibeocracy**: julian (via jacqueline fent): we now live in a vibeocracy. community personality matters — DNR, new models, grimes, charli xcx's brat. the critical skill is conducting a vibe and an audience through it. informational-rhythm analysis shows vibes as mathematically objective objects in information space.

**4chan source code as protocol weapon**: julian made a poster of the entire 4chan source code (futaba.php) — under a thousand lines. deployed first in japan, then translated to english. the downstream social effects (memes, alt-right, incel movements) are similar in every country where it's deployed. *"if you change the rules, you change the subjectivity."*

**vibe coding and the future**: with vibe coding, it becomes feasible to open-source alternatives to platform middlemen (an open source uber, etc). julian's conspiracy theory: tech oligarchs know their monopoly window is closing, so they're accumulating capital to escape before it collapses. also: DeFOS (dark forest operating system) being built by yancey — one-stop shop for communities, with micropayments for contributors.

## the "ew trump" moment

julian describes visitors entering the exhibition: *"some people came in and they were like, in two seconds, ew, no thanks. trump, trump, what? maga, no thank you. bye, and walk out."* others come in and mind-map the entire thing, finding secret relationships between objects — an *"apophenia possibility"* with all the objects. it's like watching someone navigate a feed in physical space.

then joshua goes off:

*"people who are like, walk in and be like, trump, maga, ooh — this now has majority democratic support. these channels are larger than the legacy institutions that they inherited and dedicated their lives to building. these things are now steering not just culture, but politics."*

*"and that revulsion, that response from people who are in these elite positions within those institutions — is part of why the institutions have become literally out of touch with their political constituencies. that revulsion is the evidence of them being out of touch."*

*"as cultural influence has moved away from these museums and cultural institutions... over to the platforms, you see a similar type of disconnect. these two things are actually mirroring each other."*

*"so I think part of what we're trying to do is to reflect to the art world how ugly and chaotic and dysfunctional all of society has become because our institutions have drifted out of touch with their constituencies and with their publics."*

and then the kicker: *"they built this entire system that ground artists down to where they're living at the poverty level in a society without health insurance or social democratic infrastructure. they can be upset for a few minutes."*

so the comparison joshua is making is not just that online communities "touch people more" — it's a structural argument. the institutions (museums, legacy media, political parties) drifted away from their publics. the platforms filled the gap. the people who walked out with "ew trump" are performing their own irrelevance — the revulsion itself is the proof that they can't see what's happening. they're still operating in the old categories (maga = bad = leave) while the actual power has already moved to the protocol layer, to the merch supply chains, to the content creators who are now the most effective political organizers.

it's not that these online communities are *nicer* or more *authentic*. it's that they're structurally coupled to how people actually live, buy, identify, and organize now. the institutions lost that coupling. the "ew trump" reflex is a symptom of the decoupling.

## my open questions

- the structural coupling argument: online communities are effective not because they're better communicators but because they're *structurally coupled* to how people live now — merch-as-identity, posting-as-organizing, subscriptions-as-membership. legacy institutions lost that coupling. is this the full picture or is there something else?
- how does this connect to [[exocapitalism]]? the exhibition seems to be saying: the market already ate politics, and the only question is whether you can see it or not. the people who walked out can't see it. julian explicitly says exocapitalism would blow tucker carlson's mind — *"he's already haunted by demons"*
- the "accidental avant-garde" — how does this compare to remilia's *intentional* avant-garde? remilia knows it's hacking the protocol. these creators mostly don't. does it matter?
- citarella's neoliberal imaginary argument: the platforms instantiated a 1940s neoliberal vision of frictionless markets, and progressives cheered it on without noticing the material reorganization happening underneath. *"we did their dirty work for them."* is this right? is there a version where the left could have engaged differently?
- the para-institution thesis: if squading up is already individually rational, and pseudo parties already outperform legacy parties on fundraising and mobilization — what's the bottleneck? why hasn't the full transition happened yet? is it just inertia? legal structures? or something about scale that the protocol layer can't solve?
