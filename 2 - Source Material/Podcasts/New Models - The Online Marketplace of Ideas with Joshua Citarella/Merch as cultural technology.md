## transcription

josh on merch functioning on two layers simultaneously: (1) revenue through scarce limited drops, and (2) memetic content/advertising. example: tucker carlson's merch — "i'm sorry i said you were a fed" and "siop critic" — these are jokes that function as discussion points, working on both layers at once.

carly on generational material culture: millennials cohabitate, sublet, move around, don't have basements. "the one thing they'll do is a t-shirt, or a book, or a bumper sticker, a pin. maybe they have a bong. these are the agentic material objects of millennial and younger generations." she jokes about leaving their daughter rubbermaid containers full of podcast t-shirts as inheritance.

josh: "most of it is digital. these are the traces that we're carrying with us through life." mat dryhurst donated objects from his collection — rubbermaid containers with the first ethereum shirt and other weird objects.

on value: do not research hoodies going for $300+ on ebay. some books that josh lost are now $800 on amazon. archival value is different from previous generations' collections but not zero.

josh notices formal similarity across the 400 objects: a young turks tote bag and a sam harris t-shirt both use the "thank you" chinese takeout bag graphic motif. polar opposite editorial positions, identical visual language. beneath that: same gildan t-shirts, same DTG printing, same hugo hats, same shopify-to-warehouse logistics.

## key points

- merch works on two layers simultaneously: scarce revenue object + memetic content/advertising in the network.
- millennial material culture: t-shirts, books, pins, bongs — "the agentic material objects of millennial and younger generations." everything else is digital.
- merch as "the oldest form of consensus technology, which predates the internet and which will potentially outlast the internet."
- formal/material similarity across ideological poles: young turks and sam harris using same graphic motifs, same gildan blanks, same supply chains. the market is the actual hegemon.
- merch drops function like NFTs — introducing scarcity — except they actually work because they're physical.
