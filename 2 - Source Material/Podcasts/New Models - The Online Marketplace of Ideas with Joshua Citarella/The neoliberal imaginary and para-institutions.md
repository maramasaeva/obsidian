## transcription

josh on the neoliberal origin of platforms: there was a utopian vision at the core — a neoliberal imaginary going back to the 1940s. a global society where distribution of resources and interaction happens through frictionless markets. the platforms tried to instantiate that in code. "as people who are progressives and would describe themselves as being opposed to that vision, we kind of just let it happen and cheered it on along the way." progressives said unfettering all opinions would lead to more progressive ends. "we missed the reorganization of materials in the world that the platform economies have now thrust us into." the radical ends of the political spectrum — far-right and far-left t-shirts — are produced through identical just-in-time delivery. "we did their dirty work for them."

on the return of institutions: we're seeing the return of states and institutions. the legacy of institutional critique (art world conversation for 20+ years) was that institutions are totalitarian, gatekeepers corrupt. but in embracing platforms as the alternative, "we just liquidated all of them. we didn't actually fix the problems. and we lost that space for democratic deliberation around the table."

josh's definition of institution: "any group of people in which decisions about production — what they write, what they publish, what they make together — is deliberated around a table." platforms removed that deliberation.

on para-institutions and pseudo political parties: josh proposes these nascent formations will become very influential. current examples:

- **american communist party**: founded by twitch streamers. attempted to infiltrate CPUSA, were blocked from leadership positions, formed their own splinter party.
- **do not research**: now offers courses that pay people better than adjunct professors at accredited universities. rival structures to legacy institutions.
- josh is "inclined to take them seriously" — legacy universities can't compete on job placement statistics anymore.

on squading up: the zone is flooded with competition. starting an individual substack now is much harder than in the early adopter era. people are joining collectives (new models, DNR) because it's in their individual interest — the best way to advance your career. macro shift in incentives toward collective organizing.

the thesis: "we need to start thinking about — dare i use the phrase — new models to develop new institutions for the 21st century."

## key points

- platforms instantiated a 1940s neoliberal imaginary of frictionless global markets. progressives cheered it on without noticing the material reorganization. "we did their dirty work for them."
- institutional critique led to liquidating institutions entirely rather than fixing them — we lost the space for democratic deliberation.
- institution = any group where production decisions are deliberated around a table. platforms removed that deliberation.
- para-institutions (DNR, new models) are nascent but on a trajectory to outperform legacy institutions — already paying better than adjunct professors.
- pseudo political parties (american communist party, founded by twitch streamers) may outperform legacy parties on fundraising and mobilization.
- squading up: individual substacks are flooded; joining collectives is now individually rational. macro incentive shift toward collective organizing.
