## transcription

julian on the shift: in the early 2010s, individual creators (musicians, journalists, artists) became less culturally influential than the designers of the platforms. "if you really wanted to have a big impact on culture, rather than releasing a grand opera or a wonderful novel, you could tweak some piece of code in this algorithm that then had all of these cascading effects for a mass audience."

josh: what we call "alt media" is now quantitatively larger than legacy media. has been for nearly a decade. "we don't even have the rhetorical tools to describe what this thing means. we're still functioning in this outmoded language of counterculture as if there is some kind of mainstream and an opposition."

the platforms made everything countercultural, everything oppositional. but any counterculture playing out within platform protocols is feeding the hegemonic structure — it's not countercultural, it's part of it. josh calls this "a suburbanization of resistance."

example of legacy media denial: the new CEO of condé nast claiming their reach is superior to substacks. josh: "is it? i don't know if that's true." legacy media inflates engagement numbers — counting a TV playing in a doctor's waiting room as highly engaged viewers.

the aggregate of all these seemingly disparate ideological positions is far more influential than even the aggregate of mainstream media channels (CNN, Fox News combined).

## key points

- alt media is now quantitatively larger than legacy media — has been for nearly a decade, but we lack the language to describe it.
- "suburbanization of resistance": everything has become countercultural, everything oppositional, but all of it feeds the platform hegemony.
- platform designers became more culturally influential than individual creators — the protocol layer matters more than the content layer.
- legacy media inflates engagement stats (doctor's offices counted as viewers) and is in denial about the structural shift.
- carly busta (cited by josh): any counterculture within platform protocols feeds the hegemonic structure. it's not opposition, it's participation.
