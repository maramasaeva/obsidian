Quote/moment: *"there are just enormous economic and military incentives to develop greater goal-seeking behavior in AIs... A military drone that can autonomously assess a new battleground, make its own complex plans, and strike with superhuman speed will often be preferred to one that's 'merely' superhumanly good at identifying targets... An AI that can autonomously completely run a factory... you know what's even more useful?"* (linch, "the case for AI catastrophe, in four steps," feb 2026)

My claim: linch and i agree on the daoist alternatives. the problem is not that we don't know what a non-desire-driven AI could look like — the problem is that no one is incentivized to build one. companies are incentivized to build what returns a profit, not what doesn't kill us all. and goal-seeking agents are more profitable than wu wei agents, because goal-seeking agents *do more*. they run factories, they strike targets, they optimize engagement. a wu wei agent that converges toward minimal intervention is, from a quarterly earnings perspective, an agent that converges toward not being worth paying for.

the entire structure of incentives — venture capital, quarterly reports, military contracts, market competition — selects for agents that maximize. not agents that find the void. not agents that let the result come of itself. the market doesn't reward de. it rewards extraction.

this is the thing the daoist framework can diagnose but not solve. the dao de jing is a text about governance, not about markets. laozi assumed a ruler who could choose to govern through wu wei. the modern equivalent isn't a ruler — it's a board of directors, and the board answers to shareholders, and shareholders want growth. the sage-ruler who governs without intervening gets fired.
