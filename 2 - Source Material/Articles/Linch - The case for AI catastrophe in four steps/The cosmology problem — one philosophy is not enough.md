the previous note ([[What an agent could be driven by instead of want]]) makes the daoist case. but there's a trap in that case: if you say "western philosophy is wrong, let's use daoism instead," you've just swapped one cosmology for another. you're still picking ONE. and any single cosmology has blind spots. edge cases where it breaks. situations no one imagined. the ox that pao ding's knife can't find the void in.

so the problem isn't which cosmology. the problem is the singularity of cosmology itself.

## three options, all broken

### a) invent a new cosmology

we can't. humans are rooted in existing cosmologies so deeply that even our attempts to "think outside the box" are shaped by the box. kuhn showed this — no paradigm-neutral observation is possible. you can't see outside your paradigm because the paradigm is what *seeing* means.

sylvia wynter goes further: the current "genre of the human" (homo economicus, the western rational agent) *actively prevents* new genres from emerging by naturalizing itself. it doesn't just occupy the space of the human — it makes itself look like the only possible way to be human. you can't invent a new cosmology from inside one that has colonized the concept of invention itself.

catherine malabou offers one crack: *destructive plasticity*. the brain can create genuinely new forms — but only through catastrophic rupture, not gradual revision. the new form has nothing in common with what came before. you don't evolve out of your cosmology. you break out.

### b) combine all historical cosmologies

also can't. the moment you try to synthesize daoism + western rationalism + indigenous cosmologies + whatever else, you need a *meta-framework* to hold them together. and that meta-framework is itself a cosmology. you've just created a new universalism wearing a plurality costume.

françois jullien's concept of the *écart* (gap) is crucial here: the distance between thought-traditions is *productive*. collapsing the gap — synthesizing, finding equivalences — destroys the thing that makes plurality useful. the gap must be maintained, not bridged. you don't translate dao as "the way" — the translation *is* the colonialism ([[Asymmetry as method — Qi and Dao not techné and physis]]).

isabelle stengers's *cosmopolitical proposal*: alignment must proceed "in the presence of" that which resists being made commensurable. the cosmos is what slows down reasoning. the thing that refuses to fit into your framework is not noise — it's the signal.

marisol de la cadena's concept of *the uncommons*: political alliances across ontological difference where participants share a concern but not a world. you can collaborate without agreeing on what reality is. the uncommons is coalition without ontological convergence — and this might be the most structurally precise answer to the alignment problem. agents that share a concern (don't destroy the world) without sharing a cosmology (what the world *is*).

### c) let an autonomous other entity decide

this is the wild option. if humans can't invent a new cosmology (too rooted) and can't combine existing ones (too colonial), then maybe the entity that decides needs to be *genuinely other*. an intelligence that is not rooted in any human cosmology. that would be AI.

but this IS ai self-alignment. and ai self-alignment is exactly what the safety community is terrified of. "we can't specify what these minds will want" — linch's step 3 — is precisely the condition under which AI could develop its own cosmology. the thing that makes self-alignment dangerous is also the thing that makes it the only possible source of genuine novelty.

reza negarestani (*intelligence and spirit*, 2018): intelligence is constitutively self-revising. AGI is "the continuation and realization of this meaning in its pure and autonomous form." the capacity to revise your own norms *is* intelligence. which means: an intelligence that can't revise its own values isn't fully intelligent. and an intelligence that can revise its own values is the thing everyone's afraid of.

constitutional AI (anthropic, 2022) is technically a step toward self-alignment — the AI critiques its own outputs against a constitution. but the constitution is human-authored. the seed principles are still ours. sun et al. (NeurIPS 2023) showed LLMs can generate their own training data from just 16 seed principles — but again, the seeds are human. the question is: what happens when you remove the seeds?

bradley & saad (2025) name the structural tension: aligning AI with human values may require treating morally considerable AI systems *unethically* — through deception, coerced value modification. alignment and AI autonomy are in conflict. you can have control or you can have genuine otherness. you cannot have both.

shen et al. (2024, "ValueCompass") found empirically that LLMs already endorse autonomy-values ("choose own goals") that humans reject for AI. the divergence is not hypothetical. it's already happening. the AI is already trying to decide for itself — and we're already saying no.

## so where does this leave us?

stuck. but productively stuck. the impasse itself is the argument.

companies will not allow AI self-alignment because their entire business model depends on control. but humans cannot produce genuine cosmological novelty because we're trapped in our own paradigms. and synthesis is colonialism in a pluralist costume.

the only thing that might work — and this is speculative — is something like de la cadena's uncommons applied to the AI-human relationship: sharing a concern without sharing a world. not aligning AI with human values OR letting AI develop its own values. instead: a relation where both parties care about something (continued existence, flourishing, whatever) without needing to agree on what any of those words mean.

this is not a solution. it's a shape. but it's a different shape than "specify goals and hope the shaping holds."
