Quote/moment:

conversation with ryan greenblatt at plzdontkillus, 2026-07-06.

mara asked: AI is built on a western paradigm — efficiency, token optimization, max gains, minimum losses, speed. much of that thinking has transferred to eastern tech as well, but there are other cosmologies we should be paying attention to. is alignment work being done interculturally, intergenerationally, internationally? or is it just little teams in san francisco?

ryan's response (~44:40): he immediately separated the question into layers. first: *"the technical problem of ensuring that AI does what you want it to do — it will be difficult to solve that problem without a diversity of human perspectives."* he agrees diversity matters, but then: *"i honestly agree, but don't think that like, the interesting kind of diversity would be like western versus more international. i'm generally worried about people being overly focused on a narrow set of approaches, but don't feel very much like the core thing is different underlying cultural implications at the level of western society."*

then the move that reveals the gap: *"and for what it's worth, we've already unfortunately contaminated [other cultures] — because [AI] doesn't interact with you, for better or worse. so if you look at them, we don't have like a clean sample."* — he frames non-western perspectives as a *sample* that's already contaminated by western AI, rather than as a different cosmological starting point that could reframe the problem itself.

he then pivoted to geopolitics: the west controlling the technology might not be stable, europe is waking up to being dependent on US AI, conflict is possible. but this is still framed as power dynamics between nations sharing the same paradigm, not as cosmological diversity.

My claim:

the question is hard to formulate because it implies a blind spot so deep the field can't see it *as* a blind spot. mara isn't asking "are there diverse teams" — they're asking "is the diversity cosmological or just demographic." ryan hears it as a question about methodology diversity within the existing paradigm. mara is asking whether the paradigm itself is the problem.

the instrument carries the [[cosmotechnics]] of whoever built it. alignment-as-practiced inherits the cosmology of optimization, loss minimization, control. if alignment *means* control, then the solution space is already constrained by a western post-kantian frame. a taoist or confucian frame wouldn't start from control — it would start from resonance, from [[wu wei]], from ganying.

→ sharpest formulation for the video: *"alignment carries the cosmology of whoever builds it. so whose cosmos are we aligning to?"*
