Quote/moment: the tweet itself:

*i read hyperpolitics a while ago and it made me realize that there used to be an infrastructure of collective thinking, especially about politics (unions, workers' parties, etc) that we now just dont have anymore. it was replaced by clicking & sharing & posting and trying to get on top of everyone for you-feeds,,*

*this isnt the only space where we've lost ground; how we think, how we connect, how we spend our attention, how much attention we can spend, .. its all being colonized, stolen from us. but the loss in political collective thought-space feels especially harmful since its about everyday struggles for*

*i dont think democracy is supposed to work like that. democracy is for the People, capitalized, but we're not a People, we go to vote in our individual booths, we decide which political party to vote for on our individual phones, talking abt politics in public is to most ppl kinda horrifying and taboo ... collective thinking is gone? democracy requires a collective subject but every interface we have now produces individuals*

*so how does democracy work anymore? isnt collective thought essential to democracy? we're decapitated.. democracy is dead*

some of my friends here were filling in a grant application and one of the questions was: what is something you believe that no one agrees with? and thats a difficult one to answer, but for me is, basically, i think democracy is impossible in a post-internet 

My claim: this is the political face of the simulation-depression spiral. the same mechanism that closes the dream gap ([[The dream gap]]) also closes the political gap — the space where collective thought happens. simulation replaces the agora with feeds. the communist party embedded in daily life (carting, bowling, funerals — all the same activity) was infrastructure for thinking together. social media is structurally different: millions of members, no binding presence, no real connections. baudrillard's hyperreality applied to democracy itself — we're simulating political participation now.

the "individual booths, individual phones" move is the concrete version of the abstraction: democracy requires a People (collective subject) but every interface we have now is designed for individual users. the infrastructure produces individuals, not peoples. so the democratic subject dissolves not through oppression but through UX.

connects to the post-labor argument too ([[Post-labor ontology — transcending productivity]]): if human value was grounded in productivity & productivity is being automated, and collective thinking was grounded in shared infrastructure & that infrastructure is gone — then building new rooms (workshops, residencies, physical spaces) isnt just nice work. its the precondition for anything else.
