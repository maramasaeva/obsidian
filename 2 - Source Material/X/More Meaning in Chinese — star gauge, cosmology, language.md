## mara's position

zolo says the fascination with character compression is orientalism. mara disagrees — not because compression is the point, but because compression is the WRONG frame. the claim isn't that chinese fits more characters into less space. the claim is that chinese contains meanings that cannot exist in english at all. different cosmology → different language → different meaning-space. the meanings are untranslatable not because we lack the words but because we lack the world they come from.

yuk hui / needham (p. 168–169): needham defended chinese pictographic writing as more effective and expressive than alphabetical writing — enabling expression "with much greater brevity." but mara's claim goes further than needham's: it's not just brevity. it's that the STRUCTURE of the language (pictographic, tonal, spatial, compositional) mirrors a different ontology than alphabetic languages do.

the proof mara reaches for: **su hui's star gauge** (璇璣圖, xuánjī tú, ~375 CE). a 29×29 grid of 841 chinese characters that can be read forward, backward, up, down, diagonally — producing thousands of distinct poems. su hui wove it into a brocade for her exiled husband. it is considered essentially untranslatable because the spatial-directional reading structure IS the meaning. the relationships between characters across axes, the tonal shifts when read in different directions, the way one character participates in dozens of poems simultaneously — none of this can exist in an alphabetic language because alphabetic languages are linear. they go one way. chinese can go every way at once.

this connects to hui's argument about cosmotechnics: language is itself a cosmotechnical artifact. chinese writing mirrors the organic cosmos — relational, multi-directional, compositional, where every element participates in the whole. alphabetic writing mirrors the mechanical cosmos — linear, sequential, one direction, one meaning at a time. different cosmos → different script → different thinkable thoughts.

the LLM tweet extends this: if a language can contain more meaning per symbol AND meanings that don't exist in other languages, then an AI reasoning in that language has access to a larger meaning-space. after enough turns, that advantage compounds.

the connection to "functional aesthetics" ([[Chinese culture as functional aesthetics]]): people who use chinese symbols as aesthetic decoration are doing exactly what zolo SHOULD be worried about — that's the real orientalism. taking the surface (the character as image) without the depth (the cosmology the character carries). mara's claim is the opposite of orientalism: it's taking the depth seriously.
