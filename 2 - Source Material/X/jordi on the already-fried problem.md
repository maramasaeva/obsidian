My claim: jordi names the hardest problem with the workshop — you can't appeal to a before-state kids don't remember. the answer isn't argument, it's experience. they propose a concrete experiment: abstain for a period (even one day), see what happens. *"if it's so harmless, why don't you try to go without it and see what happens, can't you do it or what?"* — the difficulty of abstaining IS the evidence. also: reading (fiction + non-fiction) was the thing that personally cured jordi's brain fog. not a dopamine detox (they call that "mostly bullcrap"), but actually replacing the empty time with something that rebuilds.

key observations:
- kids will resist any moralizing framing — anti-drug campaign energy doesn't work
- young teachers already have to "play the influencer game" to hold attention in class
- the dopamine detox framing is wrong — it's not about detox, it's about experiencing the difference between modes of attention and then making a conscious choice
- reading is specifically named as the recovery mechanism, not just "go outside" or "be present"
- jordi says *"it's a battle I somewhat gave up for now"* — this is what happens to adults who see the problem but have no structural support. exactly the adult-facing version mara wants to build eventually.
