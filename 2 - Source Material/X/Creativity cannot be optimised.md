Source: mara's response to grimes's "slop is good for artists" argument (june 2026). see [[Grimes — AI slop is good for artists]].

My claim: grimes's argument is accelerationist — worsen the system and the good stuff will emerge. this confuses survival with flourishing. pressure produces mutation, but most mutations die unfunded and unheard. the artists who break through the slop aren't proof the system works; they're survivors of a system that buried thousands.

the deeper problem: she treats creativity as *reactive* — human art as always-already-in-response-to the market, to AI, to competition. but creativity doesn't need an antagonist. you can't optimise for the unprecedented, because the unprecedented is precisely what escapes optimisation. that's what [[rationalism]] structurally can't see — not because it's wrong, but because its method requires a calculable object, and genuine novelty isn't one.

the [[queer]] / [[becoming]] thread: queer art has always existed under hostile conditions, but "hostile conditions produce good queer art" is exactly the logic used to justify the hostility. "suffering makes great art" is the oldest excuse for not changing the system. any creativity can emerge under any circumstances, and any creativity is valuable in and of itself.

there's also a flattening move: grimes treats music as *content* (fungible, competitive, platform-native) rather than as *practice* (embodied, relational, situated). this is the [[exocapitalism]] lens — the platform doesn't care what the music *is*, only how it circulates. the slop argument operates entirely within that frame.

what this means in practice: even if it's true that some artists respond to slop by making wilder work, the current music industry infrastructure won't support them. discovery, funding, income — all broken. the argument is true in the abstract and false in the material. that gap is the rationalist blind spot.
