Quote/moment: *"baudrillard warned us against this"* — in reply to a game about walking and talking. then the thread became the argument:

*"i do believe that certain characteristics of our current post-modern era are driving people, including creatives, to believe that experiencing a simulation of the real is equal to experiencing reality. there should be a drive toward real storytelling, instead of its simulation. this is difficult to request tho, because simulations are rewarded, and humans need the (monetary) rewards to live"*

*"look at this phenomenon thru history and you notice simulation of reality happens incrementally. first we simulate speech (telephone), then vision (photography), then movement (videography) etc etc."*

*"i agree that voice chat in itself isnt the worst way to simulate things; it definitely has caused more good than harm. however, that doesnt mean it cant snowball into something larger. it definitely has."*

My claim: the thread produced two things worth keeping.

first: the escalation is not abstract. it has a concrete historical sequence. telephone → photography → videography → games where ur body is simulated but ur voice is real. each step simulates one more layer of human interaction. the game BIG WALK is literally about walking and talking — the most basic human social act — and now it's a product on steam. baudrillard's point, made material.

second: the economic trap. sean kennedy pushed back saying voice chat isnt simulated, its mediated. and he's right at the granular level. but the larger point holds: creatives are driven toward simulation because simulations are rewarded. the monetary structure of late capitalism rewards the copy over the original. not out of malice. out of incentive structure. u cant ask artists to make real things when the market pays for simulated ones and they need to eat.

the nuance matters tho: simulation isnt binary. its layered. voice chat keeps the voice real but simulates the body. a podcast simulates presence but keeps the voice. parasociality simulates relationship but keeps the content. each layer is its own partial simulation. and the stack keeps growing. this is the escalation described in [[Hyperreality killed the dream — Roderick on Baudrillard]] but with the historical mechanism made explicit.
