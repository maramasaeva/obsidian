Quote/moment: *scheduled tweet, june 2026:*

*if AGI simulates everything until nothing feels real anymore, in other words turning reality into hyperreality, our current dystopia, then what defeats it? what is AGI's natural enemy?*

*a reality that hasn't been simulated yet, a reality you just have to chase after. the thing you believe in before it exists.*

*to defeat AGI... i think we need to dream*

My claim: this is the second movement of [[AGI's natural enemy]]. the first video asked *what can't AGI eat?* and answered: the incalculable — the stuff that dies when you turn it into data. this tweet asks the same question from the other direction and answers: the dream — the stuff that doesn't exist yet and therefore can't be simulated. the incalculable is what AGI can't digest. the dream is what AGI can't preempt.

both answers point to the same gap. baudrillard calls it the distance between the real and the hyperreal. [[hyperstition]] calls it the space where fiction becomes fact. the dream lives in the gap that simulation is trying to close. AGI's natural enemy is not a weapon or a regulation — it's the human capacity to want something that hasn't been rendered yet.

this also completes the loop from [[The dream gap]]: if simulation closes the gap → we stop dreaming → depression → can't create → more simulation, then the antidote is dreaming *deliberately*. not as escapism but as an act of resistance. dreaming is the counter-technology.
