Quote/moment: *"because simulation and postmodernity has killed our capacity to dream, people don't really dream anymore, which is why they cannot fulfill their dreams. they don't have dreams, which is why they stay depressed, and that's the exact spiral."*

My claim: this is a loop that feeds itself. hyperreality closes the gap between imagination and experience — you can step inside a simulation of the vietnam war, you can see dinosaurs on a screen more vivid than anything your brain could render. so you stop dreaming. and without dreams there's no motivation from the core, no passion that arouses itself from within. that's not clinical depression. that's what fisher calls the slow cancellation of the future, but from baudrillard's direction: the future didn't get cancelled by capitalism alone. it got cancelled because simulation ate the space where futures are imagined.

the spiral:

hyperreality replaces experience → dreams become unnecessary → dreamlessness becomes depression → depression kills the capacity to create → less creation means more dependence on simulation → simulation deepens → loop repeats.

you can't exit this unless hyperreality transforms into something else. not back to "normal reality" — that's gone. forward into the next level. but what is the next level? if the trajectory is photography → film → television → 3D → VR → direct neural experience, then we're making everything more artificial, not less. each step makes the simulation more total and the dream more redundant.

the only exit i can see: [[hyperstition]]. if fiction can pull futures into being, then art is the counterspell to simulation. not because it's more real — it isn't — but because it reopens the gap between what is and what could be. simulation says "here, experience it now." art says "imagine something that doesn't exist yet." that gap is where dreams live.

this also connects to the brangus argument ([[plzdontkillus application - brangus reply thread]]): making beautiful things doesn't solve alignment, but it IS central to keeping people capable of dreaming. and if we want alignment to be beneficial for humans, why would we not care about their capacity to dream right now? alignment for a species that has already stopped dreaming is alignment for zombies.
