rob miles argues that we use examples of something totally different from the current situation to justify that things will be okay. people do this with AI constantly — in movies there's this or that trope, and historically we've never accidentally extinguished our species, so it'll be fine. he's pointing at a kind of false comfort: we pattern-match against irrelevant precedents and call it reasoning.

but i think there's something he's missing, or at least something i want to push back on.

it's a *good thing* that we don't have really negative stories about species extinction from AI. because if we did — if the dominant cultural narrative was doom, was certainty of doom — that would be a way to make that future come true. [[hyperstition]] works both ways. you can hyperstition yourself into flourishing or into annihilation. a culture saturated in doom imagery would be rehearsing its own death.

the work should be: yes, the risk is real, yes it's increasing, yes we need solutions. but the orientation should be realistic *and hopeful*. not naive, not dismissive, but not doom-fixated either. focusing on doom and believing doom will come true *will make it come true*. the belief becomes the infrastructure.

so: be honest about the possibility. then say — and we're going to do this, this, this. channel the energy into building, not into dread.

this is what i want to bring to plzdontkillus. the daily videos shouldn't be doom content. they should be *what we're doing about it* content. the difference between a warning and a prophecy.
