# what a sign taught me about bodies and words

*[to camera. close. already in it.]*

so people are telling me to use more responsible language. while other people are asking me if I'm "fully female." while someone else is telling me to take antipsychotics. while someone is making a meme about how my career in AI is over because I'm "just a girl."

and I'm being told to be responsible.

*[beat.]*

and the thing is, I don't care about the sign. I'm not here to explain the sign. I'm not going to say "I didn't make it" like that's a defense, because it doesn't matter who made it. that's not the point. the point is everything that happened around it. because I also made a video that week about what actually pisses me off about the x-risk community. four minutes of me saying what I think about how the rationalist framework has blind spots. and the responses to that were people commenting on my face. someone saying there's no subject they could "brain fuck about" for four minutes. someone just saying "yikes."

nobody argued with what I said. they argued with the fact that I was the one saying it.

*[pause.]*

and if you're watching this and you don't know who I am, maybe look into that before you decide what I think. because the amount of people who assumed I'm anti-AI, who assumed I'm some leftist who hates technology, who didn't take thirty seconds to look at my profile or my work or anything... that's part of the problem. you should not be assuming things. you should be curious. that's supposed to be the whole thing about this community, right? curiosity? rigor? and then a photo shows up and all of that just disappears.

*[shift.]*

because here's what I keep coming back to. I've been trying to say something substantial about alignment for a while. and every single time, it gets filtered through my body first. how I look. how I present. whether I'm trans. what I'm wearing. the ideas don't get engaged with. they get filtered and most of them don't survive the trip.

a man says the exact same thing and people argue with the argument. I say it and people argue with my existence.

and I know this because it's not the first time. this is a pattern. every time I try to get a point across about alignment, about the things I think are missing from the conversation, it's never taken seriously. and it's because of my gender identity and the way that I present myself. the transphobic comments in the replies just make that visible. but it was already there.

*[beat.]*

but I actually want to talk about something that I think is more interesting than the fact that people are shitty online.

there's this word, "clanker." it's a slur for robots. for AI. and people love saying it. there's this person on twitter, barbitas, who described seeing a kid on a train listing AI slurs for thirty seconds straight with, and I'm quoting, "the giddy excitement of a boy who thinks he's being naughty."

and I keep thinking about that. the excitement. the thrill of it. because the whole anti-AI slur thing, the "total clanker genocide" energy, it doesn't have political depth. it's not a movement with a program. it's a vibe. it's aesthetic transgression. it's the same energy as the Milady thing. the whole post-ironic edgelord ecosystem. there's no substance. there's a feeling. a collective thrill of pushing past a boundary together. and that feeling is real. it generates real community, real loyalty, real energy. even though the content underneath is hollow. or worse.

*[closer.]*

and here's where it gets interesting for me. what if that feeling, that specific thing, the rush of saying something you're not supposed to say, of being in on the joke together, what if that could be generated through something that isn't destructive? what if the problem isn't that people want to feel transgressive, but that the only templates we've given them are racist memes and ironic nihilism?

because I think that's actually an alignment question. not a technical one. a felt one.

*[pause.]*

and this is where my body comes back into it. because to even explore that question I have to show up. I have to present myself. my aesthetics, my sense of what beauty can carry. and the moment I do that as someone who presents the way I do, the beauty becomes the thing people attack. or consume. or reduce me to. I want to express what I think is beautiful. I want to be honest about that. but as someone who presents as female, that expression gets turned into the entire conversation. the substance disappears behind the surface.

a man exploring the aesthetics of transgression gets called a theorist. I get called a body.

*[beat.]*

and that collision, between bodies and words and who gets to say what, that's actually the thing I think the AI safety conversation is missing. who says a word changes what it means. how it's said. what body is next to it. a sign says the same words no matter who stands next to it but the meaning changes completely. 400,000 people saw a photo and almost none of them saw what was actually happening.

we keep trying to solve alignment like it's a math problem. and it is, partly. but it's also about who gets to speak. what feelings we're allowed to invoke. whether we can build something beautiful enough to compete with the thrill of destruction.

I don't have the full answer yet. I'm working on it.

*[hold. cut.]*
