## the read-this-or-don't-go layer

three things. if you read nothing else, these three. they're the texts the room you're walking into is *currently* having opinions about.

### 1. Yudkowsky & Soares — *If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies* (2025) ✓ done

mara has read this (physical copy). leaving it in the list as the anchor: the book that pulled this whole moment mainstream and the immediate reason plzdontkillus exists — aella + ronny built the residency in part to translate this book's argument into something that doesn't sound like math people yelling.

### 2. Eliezer Yudkowsky — *Inadequate Equilibria* (2017)

short (~200 pages), free online, less ranty than the sequences. yudkowsky on **why functional markets / institutions fail to do obvious things**. this is the gateway drug to his thinking style without committing to a 2000-page commitment. you'll notice it's also a very good lens on a lot of things you already think about (kotopia funding gaps in europe, why "experimental engineer rooms don't exist," etc.). free at equilibriabook.com.

### 3. Scott Alexander — *Meditations on Moloch* (2014)

single essay, ~1 hour read, available at slatestarcodex.com archive. the canonical rationalist text on **coordination failures + the structural inevitability of bad outcomes from individually rational actors.** liv boeree's entire win-win podcast is downstream of this. once you've read it you'll see "moloch" referenced unmarked in conversations — they're talking about this essay. essential vocabulary.

## the canon, if you want to go deeper

### Yudkowsky

- **Harry Potter and the Methods of Rationality (HPMOR)** — 2010-2015 fanfic, ~660k words. yes it's a harry potter fanfic. yes it's load-bearing for the entire scene. **sonia joseph read it four times in her teens** (per bloomberg). half the people you'll meet had their worldview shaped by this. you don't have to finish it but you should at least sample a few chapters around the "rationality lesson" parts to get the register. free at hpmor.com.
- **The Sequences / *Rationality: From AI to Zombies*** (2006-2009, compiled 2015) — the foundational corpus. 2000+ pages. don't try to read cover-to-cover. instead read the opening sequence *"Map and Territory"* and *"Mysterious Answers to Mysterious Questions"* — together maybe 150 pages, and you'll have absorbed 80% of how rationalists talk about epistemics.
- *The Hidden Complexity of Wishes* — single essay, ~5 minutes. the foundational AI-alignment intuition pump.

### Scott Alexander

- *I Can Tolerate Anything Except the Outgroup* — to understand his thinking on tribes/coalitions. (he's the closest the scene has to a public intellectual + the source of most of its prose style)
- *Meditations on Newcomb's Problem* — for his decision-theory voice
- *Unsong* — his fantasy novel. optional but a window into the aesthetic register

### Bostrom

- *Superintelligence* (2014) ✓ done — mara has read this. it's what the room read; you have the foundation.

### Nate Soares

- *Minding Our Way* — his blog, mintingoutway.com. shorter essays on rationality, motivation, agency. easier register than yudkowsky.

## the bridges (this is the part for you specifically)

these are the books that connect what you already think to what they think. they're not on the plzdontkillus syllabus but they're **the texts that let you be in the room without becoming the room.** match your reading practice (CCRU, preciado, wittig, fisher, land, plant, bratton, negarestani-adjacent) to the room's vocabulary.

### Reza Negarestani — *Intelligence and Spirit* (2018) — skipped

mara has read other negarestani (Machine Decision Is Not Final, the accelerationism canon) and finds I&S too dense to commit to right now. that's a fair call — I&S is brutal and requires sellars/brandom familiarity. you have enough negarestani for the room.

### Sadie Plant — *Zeroes and Ones* (1997)

you already have it on the favorites list. **reread it before going.** it's the queer-cybernetic counter-canon to the linear rationalist story of AI. you'll need the reminder that the "ai future" is not the only frame.

### Donna Haraway — *Staying with the Trouble* (2016)

mara has already read *cyborg manifesto*; this is the next one. *staying with the trouble* is the more recent (and more relevant) "what kind of futures do we want to be making" book. haraway is **the** counter-yudkowsky in the long view: instead of "if anyone builds it, everyone dies" she says *"we are already in trouble, make kin not extinction."*

### Mark Fisher — *Flatline Constructs: Gothic Materialism and Cybernetic Theory-Fiction* (1999, his PhD)

your fisher reading you already have; this is the deeper-cut one, his PhD thesis. **bridges CCRU → AI consciousness questions** directly. read alongside *intelligence and spirit* and you have the connective tissue between your theoretical practice and what they're up to.

### Yuk Hui — *The Question Concerning Technology in China: An Essay in Cosmotechnics* (2016)

the **tao bridge** mara asked for. hui's foundational text: why was *technē* never thematized in chinese thought? what happened to the relation between *qi* (器, tool/utensil) and *dao* (道) when china welcomed technological modernity? he reads heidegger's *question concerning technology* against chinese philosophy and argues there is not one universal technics but **cosmotechnics — a plurality of relations between cosmos and tool.** fits your existing tao/loops/cybernetics thinking precisely and gives you a non-western counter to the lesswrong assumption that "intelligence" or "alignment" is one universal thing. start here for the tao question.

### Beatrice Fazi — *Contingent Computation: Abstraction, Experience, and Indeterminacy in Computational Aesthetics* (2018)

shorter, deeply useful. on the *materiality* of computation in a way that bypasses the lesswrong frame of "intelligence as substrate-independent." would arm you for arguments where someone tries to wave away the body.

### Wendy Hui Kyong Chun — *Programmed Visions* (2011) and/or *Updating to Remain the Same* (2016)

both excellent. *programmed visions* is the more theoretical, *updating* is the more political. either gives you frames the room won't have.

### Kate Crawford — *Atlas of AI* (2021) — skipped

mara already knows the material-politics-of-AI argument (mining, labor, water, carbon). save the time.

## the tao bridges (added at mara's request, may 2026)

mara wants more tao, specifically *bridges* between tao and current AI / cybernetics / queer thinking — not the *Tao Te Ching* itself. three picks, in priority:

### François Jullien — *A Treatise on Efficacy: Between Western and Chinese Thinking* (1997, EN 2004)

**short, surgical, do this first.** jullien is a french sinologist who set out to show that *strategy* itself is conceived differently in chinese and western thought. the west: *form a model → execute the plan → assert the will against reality*. china: *read the propensity inherent in the situation (shì 勢) → let the result come of itself.* this is **wu wei as strategic theory**, written for someone fluent in western philosophy. it will detonate your understanding of how rationalist "agency" gets framed (the entire EA/rationalist worldview is a maximalist version of the *western* mode jullien diagnoses). ~200 pages. perfect prep companion to *if anyone builds it, everyone dies*. — [goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/330323.A_Treatise_on_Efficacy)

### Yuk Hui — *The Question Concerning Technology in China: An Essay in Cosmotechnics* (2016)

(see above — moved into the main bridges list)

### David Hinton — *China Root: Taoism, Ch'an, and Original Zen* (2020)

less academic. hinton is a translator and poet, not a philosopher. he argues that ch'an (= zen, before japan) is buddhist-flavored *taoism* — that the standard english translations of zen texts have erased the daoist substrate. earthy, embodied, ecological. would feed your sense that "intelligence" is not a disembodied thing — which is a useful counterweight to lighthaven's whole vibe. ~200 pages. — [goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/54607566-china-root)

## the rationalism-deeper layer (added at mara's request, may 2026)

mara wants to get further into rationalism specifically. picks, in priority:

### Julia Galef — *The Scout Mindset* (2021)

galef is a co-founder of CFAR. **this is the accessible, non-doom-flavored primer on what rationalists actually try to do epistemically.** "scout mindset" = motivated to see things as they are, vs. "soldier mindset" = motivated to defend a position. ~280 pages, very readable. if you want to know what they *aspire* to (separate from what the scene is sociologically), this is it. — [goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/42041926-the-scout-mindset)

### Toby Ord — *The Precipice: Existential Risk and the Future of Humanity* (2020)

the **philosopher's framing of x-risk.** ord is at oxford's future of humanity institute. less yelling than yudkowsky, more numbers and arguments. estimates 1-in-6 existential catastrophe in the next century, dominated by AI. the canonical academic-side x-risk book. read alongside or after *if anyone builds it* to see two modes of arguing the same conclusion. — [goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/50485582-the-precipice)

### Daniel Kahneman — *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011)

the cognitive-bias canon. **yudkowsky cites it constantly; the sequences are partly a popularization of it.** if you haven't read it, the language of "system 1 / system 2," anchoring, availability heuristic, etc. is the room's shared vocabulary. you don't have to finish — the first half is the load-bearing half. — [goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/11468377-thinking-fast-and-slow)

### Will MacAskill — *What We Owe the Future* (2022)

**the longtermist case, made by a philosopher who isn't yelling.** macaskill is the EA-canonical author. you should know this argument cold even if you reject it — it's the moral justification for *why* SFF/MIRI fund things like plzdontkillus. — [goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/56269264-what-we-owe-the-future)

### optional: Philip Tetlock — *Superforecasting* (2015)

if the prediction-markets dimension of the scene (manifold, manifest, polymarket) interests you. liv boeree references this. — [goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/23995360-superforecasting)

### optional: Douglas Hofstadter — *I Am a Strange Loop* (2007)

bridges to [[loops]] and [[the zero]]. hofstadter on **strange-loop self-reference as the substrate of identity.** the rationalist scene has a soft spot for hofstadter; reading this gives you a shared reference that's less doom-coded than yudkowsky. — [goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/123471.I_Am_a_Strange_Loop)

## the funhouse mirror layer (added june 2026)

_note added by claude, june 2026. mara is building an argument connecting goodhart's law to cosmotechnics — the funhouse mirror direction. this reading arms that argument specifically._

the core insight: everyone in alignment knows goodhart's law ("when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"). what nobody is connecting it to is *cosmotechnics* — the distortion has a direction, and that direction is the cosmology of the builder. this is mara's opening. the angle that hasn't been taken yet.

### goodhart's law + alignment (know this cold)

- **"Classifying specification problems as variants of Goodhart's Law"** — alignment forum post (manheim & garrabrant). introduces four types: regressional, causal, extremal, adversarial. the standard taxonomy. free on alignmentforum.org. this is the vocabulary the room uses when they talk about goodhart. read this before anything else in this section.
- **Stuart Russell — *Human Compatible: Artificial Intelligence and the Problem of Control* (2019)** — the best book-length treatment of the value specification problem. russell's argument: we shouldn't build AI that optimizes for fixed objectives at all; instead AI should be uncertain about human preferences and defer to humans. his framing (the "king midas problem") is the accessible version of what mara is saying about the funhouse mirror. ~300 pages, readable. — [goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/44767248-human-compatible)
- **"The Specification Trap"** (2025, arxiv) — recent paper arguing that better specification doesn't solve alignment because specification *itself* introduces distortion. exactly mara's cosmotechnics point, arrived at from a different direction. worth citing.
- **"On Goodhart's law, with an application to value alignment"** (2024, arxiv) — formalizes goodhart for alignment contexts. dense but the abstract + conclusions are enough.

### what nobody is saying yet

the above is established. what isn't established — what mara brings — is:

1. the distortion has a *direction* → the builder's cosmotechnics
2. therefore "better specification" can't fix it → it just refines the mirror
3. the solution might be cosmotechnical pluralism → different AIs from different cosmologies, distortions cancel/complement
4. or: stop specifying values entirely → AI as medium, not agent ([[Should we give AI values at all]])

this is the pitch for plzdontkillus. the rationalists have the goodhart framework. mara has the cosmotechnics angle that completes it. nobody else is making this connection.

### the bridge to the room

read russell first, then the alignment forum taxonomy. when you walk into a conversation about specification problems, you want to be able to say: "yes, and the distortion isn't random — it has a direction." that's the sentence that will make the room listen.
