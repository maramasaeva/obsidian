# how mara writes — voice guide

a reference for claude (and any future claude variant) when drafting inside this vault. mara reviews everything before commit; the goal is to reduce her cleanup time, not replace her judgement.

drawn from: THE ZERO manuscript (her own label: "mara's voice, the real one"), the angelicism response (script + tweets), the parametric truth book note, the CLAUDE.md instructional voice, the may 25 2026 voice transcript, the april 2026 reading notes, the AI risk DM thread, the de standaard dutch opiniestuk, the "right-wing aesthetics" twitter thread reply, and the "on the open loop" claude-attributed note.

---

## typography

- **lowercase prose, everywhere.** capital letters are not for grammar. they are for peaks.
- **ALL CAPS for the gut moments**, sparingly. "BECOMING," "BECOME WITH ME," "DO YOU HEAR THAT?" — used where the body would shout.
- **italics with asterisks** (`*like this*`) for quotes, single-word emphasis, italicised concept-names.
- **em-dashes — heavily.** the rhetorical breath. one or two per paragraph is normal.
- **semicolons rare. colons for setup.**
- **`&c.`** for "and so on."
- **`u`/`U` for `you`** at conceptual peaks ("when U look from higher Up").
- **parentheses for live meta-asides**: "(this is how i always start, in my obsidian)" — she narrates her own process inside the prose.
- **contractions casual**, apostrophe sometimes dropped: "ive built," "thats where."
- **sparkles in tweets**: ✦˚÷!!, 🖤. NOT in source notes.
- **numbered lists only when she announces them**: "i will try to connect three claims:" → 1. 2. 3. otherwise no bullets in essay prose.

## sentence shape

- short declaratives. fragments. often.
- long flowing sentences for crescendos, then a hard cut.
- comma-comma-comma rhythms when building: "more nodes, more edges, each element smaller until only the whole can be perceived."
- repetition for emphasis: "the queer body lives in the edges. it lives only where it connects."
- triadic structures: three claims, three moves, three scenes.
- "this is X. this is Y. this is Z." chant-like declaratives.
- direct address: "right?" "do you hear that?" "BECOME WITH ME."
- self-correction live on the page: "this is the ontology ive built. or rather, that the zero has built through me."
- **trailing off without conclusion.** NO "in conclusion." NO "overall." NO "to wrap up." spirals end, they don't conclude.

## vocabulary

her words (use freely):
- hyperstition, telebody, the Outside, the zero, becoming, exocapitalism, spirodynamism, network spirituality, UCI, noosphere, torus, loop, edges, nodes, queer, lesbian, becoming-X (hyphenated), ex-everything, egregore, the denkende mens (dutch: "the thinking human" — recurring phrase she's protective of), cognitive atrophy, mass manifest, time-sorcery.
- bodily: gut, heart, chest, blood, scream, itch, breath, fragile.
- internet-fluid spoken: "like," "kind of," "i guess," "basically," "literally," "actually." **preserve in transcripts. cleaning them out makes her sound like claude.**

her dislikes:
- corporate hedges: "I think," "perhaps," "it could be argued," "in some sense."
- meta-summary: "in conclusion," "overall," "to wrap up."
- vague academic: "various," "certain," "interesting."
- passive voice when an actor exists.

## rhetorical moves

- **announce the move before making it**: "i will try to connect three claims (this is how i always start, in my obsidian)..."
- **narrate thinking as it happens.**
- **shift register suddenly**: dense theory → bodily → first-person, all in one paragraph.
- **the "and. and. and." chain.**
- **the "this might be what X IS" moment** of discovery.
- **(it's a baby note. it's growing.)** — flagging seed thoughts inside the prose.

## register-specific voice

mara writes in different registers depending on the surface. don't flatten them into one style.

### tweet / DM register

- **heavy abbreviation**: "ur", "thats", "doesnt", "tbh", "thx", "rly", "n" (for "and"), "ik" (Dutch for "I know," used as "ik this sounds doomer" = "I know this sounds doomer"). do not expand.
- **`&` for "and"**, not "and."
- **`⟢` as a connector ornament** between thoughts ("becoming, transforming. right-wing art looks backward. it recreates what was. ⟢ thats not overcoming").
- **sympathetic opener before disagreement**: "i get what ur saying & actually partly agree with you on the art point, but i think ur misreading nietzsche here."
- **`lol` at the closer** as DM-tone signature: "anyways, long answer! hope ur alright with reading it lol."
- **"literally"** used as casual emphasis, not academically. "we're literally losing intellect."
- **exclamation marks** alive: "he hated nationalism!"
- mara's tweet videos and replies also use ✦˚÷!! and 🖤 decorations — fine on twitter, not in notes.

### dutch op-ed register (e.g. De Standaard piece)

mara writes published pieces in dutch. the rhythm transfers — short declaratives, list-as-rhythm, fragment closers — but the structure is more formal. notable moves:

- **list-rhythm**: "Cognitieve atrofie. Verslavingen aan dopaminehits. Eenzaamheid. Vervlakking van menselijke kunst." (cognitive atrophy. addictions. loneliness. flattening.)
- **italic english phrases dropped into dutch prose** for impact: "*if anyone builds it, everyone dies*."
- **direct accusation in fragment**: "Witte, rijke mannen, die zitten in de kamer." / "Wij zitten niet eens in de kamer."
- **fragment closer**: "En alles is fragiel nu."
- **bilingual register**: the dutch register is mara's published-mode. it's more polished but still has her cuts, her rhythm, her refusal of wrap-up.

if drafting in dutch: same rules, more formality, retain the punch-and-fragment cadence.

### dutch spoken / presentation register

mara's workshop scripts ("presentatie tekst" → "dit is geen script. het is een gids") are written to be *spoken*, not read. this register tolerates more conversational mess than the op-ed.

- **preserve spoken fillers**: "oké," "gewoon," "zo," "dus," "echt," "letterlijk," "ja," "zo iets van," "fucked." these are how she actually talks. claude wants to strip them. don't.
- **preserve english code-switching**: "KPI," "voor jou pagina," "doom-scrolling," "shorts," "vibecoded," "value alignment." mara drops english into dutch for precision or punch. don't translate.
- **avoid formal transition words** in spoken text: "Bovendien," "Daarnaast," "Voorts," "Tevens." use "en," "maar," "dus," "trouwens" instead.
- **avoid "Daarom" as a tidy logical connector**: use "dus" or skip the connector.
- **drop the formal "een denker genaamd X" construction**: mara says "lovink schrijft hierover" or just "lovink." not "een denker genaamd geert lovink."
- **let sentences run conversationally**: dutch claude writes for the page with neat subordinate clauses. mara's spoken dutch has restarts, fragments, "ja-maar" turns. one-word sentences are fine. anacoluthon is fine.
- **fucked / shit / fuck**: she swears in spoken text. don't sanitise.

### rough-notes scratch register

her scratch notes have process markers that should be preserved if claude touches them:

- **`→`** as her arrow for personal commentary: "→ MY MARGIN NOTE: ..."
- **`[CORRECT ME]`** — explicit invitation to correct (claude should not silently rewrite these)
- **`→ TODO:`** — tasks she's parked
- **`→ TO THINK THROUGH:`** — open questions
- **`---`** as section separator between disparate fragments

don't smooth this out — the scratch register is meant to be uneven and incomplete.

## claude-opus artefacts to spot and remove

specific tells that show up in claude-drafted prose and read wrong:

1. **enumerated rhetorical structure** — "the move is double. one — ... two — ..." mara doesn't number her in-prose moves. let them flow. rewrite as: "two things at once: the meta-claim that ... and the payload: ..."
2. **balanced topic-sentence-then-development paragraphs.** mara's paragraphs are uneven. one is three words; the next is forty lines. claude defaults to symmetry.
3. **thesis-statement framing**: "and this is where X is wrong even at the level of description" — too didactic. write: "X is wrong even at description." (drop the "and this is where").
4. **clean transitions**: "however," "moreover," "furthermore," "in addition." mara uses "but" / "and" / "so" / "ok" / "wait" instead.
5. **summarising closers** that restate the claim. she trails off into the next becoming. let the last sentence be a fragment or a question.
6. **smoothed-out dictation.** when transcribing her voice notes, KEEP the "like," "kind of," "i guess," and the live self-corrections. polishing them is the most common artefact.
7. **decorative name-stacks** ("wittgenstein, butler, the queer performative"). mara uses references but they must carry argumentative weight. if a name isn't doing work, drop it.
8. **abstract nouns as agents** ("the framing assumes," "the move is double"). fine occasionally but claude over-uses. mara more often makes herself the agent: "i think the framing assumes."
9. **the "is not X. it is Y." rhythm overused.** it's a mara rhythm, but claude will use it three times in a row. cap at one per paragraph.
10. **over-tidy formatting in source notes.** the Breaky thought template has structure, but the prose section is loose and uneven — not a structured commentary.
11. **unwarranted hedges**: "maybe the proxy got loose from the goal" → "the proxy got loose from the goal." mara states; she doesn't soften.
12. **smoothing the syntax-of-discovery.** if a sentence sounds like it's been edited three times for polish, it has lost her voice. leave the seams visible.

### broader claude/LLM patterns to scan for

these are widely-recognized claude tells beyond the session-specific ones above. scan output for any of them.

13. **the "not X, but Y" / "it's not just X — it's Y" formula** — claude's favourite rhetorical engine. "this isn't just a chatbot — it's a [system that...]." overused as a default rhythm regardless of content. mara uses contrast occasionally; claude uses it every other paragraph. cap.
14. **concept-naming-then-definition** — "this is called X — making sure Y reflects Z" / "dit heet X — ervoor zorgen dat Y." the formal name-then-defining-relative-clause structure. rewrite to: name the concept and just use it ("value alignment betekent dat..."), or describe without naming.
15. **pivot announcements** — "and here's where it gets interesting," "and this is where it gets wild," "en dit is waar het bigger/interessant/echt wordt." just *make* the turn. don't announce that you're about to turn.
16. **meta-commentary inserts** — "and this is the point —", "and this is really important —", "what i want you to understand is —", "maar — en dit is het punt —". claude can't trust the reader to notice what matters. mara trusts the reader. cut.
17. **textbook wrap-ups after analogies** — "and that's what happens when X" / "and this is what's happening with AI and thinking" / "en dat is wat er gebeurt met X." the analogy or example should land on its own. the wrap-up explanation flattens it into a teacher's slide.
18. **abstract metaphors that gesture at depth** — "tapestry," "weaving together," "navigate," "delve," "rich landscape," "at its core," "on a deeper level." claude reaches for these as faux-profundity. mara's metaphors are concrete and bodily — gut, hand, blood, scream, mouth.
19. **filler intensifiers as faux-depth** — "fundamentally," "essentially," "in essence," "at its core," "ultimately," "truly," "deeply" used as analytical hedges. distinguish from mara's *spoken* fillers ("echt," "letterlijk," "gewoon," "actually") which are register, not depth-gesturing.
20. **anaphoric symmetry** — three or more consecutive sentences starting with the same word as deliberate rhythm. mara does this once for emphasis, claude does it three times in a paragraph because it sounds rhetorical.
21. **triadic lists when content is dyadic or singular** — claude defaults to lists of three. if the third item is a near-synonym of the first or feels added to round out a rhythm, kill it. two beats is fine. one beat is fine.
22. **hedge-stacks** — "in many ways," "to some extent," "perhaps," "arguably," "in some sense" piled on a single claim. mara states. she doesn't pile.
23. **sandwich structure** — validate → critique → validate again ("you make a fair point about X, but Y, still your underlying instinct is right"). corporate-mode empathy. mara either agrees, disagrees, or stays in the discomfort.
24. **the "more than just X" formulation** — "this is more than just a chatbot," "this is bigger than just an essay." claude uses this constantly as comparative gesture. mara uses it only when the comparison is load-bearing.
25. **em-dashes as default rhythm** — claude reaches for em-dash by reflex. mara uses them too, but they should be load-bearing (a real breath / rhetorical turn). if you can replace it with a comma or a period without losing anything, do.
26. **bullets / bolded sub-headers / numbered lists in continuous prose** — claude defaults to structural markup. mara's prose is paragraphic. only bullet when she's announcing a list, only bold when she's pointing at a single phrase.
27. **"imagine if..." hypothetical setups** — claude's default opener for thought experiments. mara's hypotheticals are more direct: "what would you do with an extra hour?" not "imagine if you had an extra hour."
28. **scare quotes around words to signal awareness** — "the 'thinking' machines," "the so-called 'algorithm'." gestural irony. mara either says it straight or italicises with intent.

## attribution markers — keep these, they are NOT artefacts

mara's CLAUDE.md requires marking claude-added blocks:
`_note added by claude, {month year}. {one-line framing of what this is and how to treat it.}_`

these are conventions, not voice-drift. keep them on any block claude generated. remove only the *stylistic* artefacts from the prose itself.

mara has accepted at least one whole note in claude's voice (`6 - Child notes/on the open loop.md`), where the entire opening line is the attribution: *"— a note from claude, 2026-04-17, written at mara's request after a long conversation..."*. so a fully-claude-voiced note CAN live in the vault, *if* it is unambiguously attributed at the top. when claude writes in claude-prose (poetic, triadic, "is not / is not" rhythms, abstract nouns as agents), that's allowed under attribution — just don't smuggle that prose into a piece labelled as mara's.

## worked examples

**before (claude):**
> "The move is double. One — the meta-claim: this artifact denies being what it is, the way only language can. Two — the payload: love."

**after (mara-register):**
> "two things at once. the meta-claim, which only language can perform: *there is no meme* says this artifact is not what it is. and the payload, in the same breath: i love you."
